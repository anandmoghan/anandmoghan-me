DDPM noises clean data \( x_0 \) over \( T \) steps, and any step has a closed form. A network \( \epsilon_\theta \) learns to recover the added noise:

\[ x_t = \alpha_t\,x_0 + \sigma_t\,\epsilon \] \[ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\,x_0,\,\epsilon}\big\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\big\|^2 \]

Here \( \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \) is the noise. \( \beta_s \) is the noise variance added at step \( s \). \( \alpha_t^2 = \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s \le t}(1 - \beta_s) \) is the signal variance left. \( \sigma_t^2 = 1 - \bar\alpha_t \) is the noise variance. The loss is plain regression, with \( t \) uniform over the steps. It drops the weights of the variational bound, which gave better samples.
