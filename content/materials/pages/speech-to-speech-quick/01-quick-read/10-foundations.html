Foundations: the S2S design space

  • Every S2S system answers five questions: input rep, output rep, text/speech coupling, duplex scheme, acoustic renderer.
  • Core tension: speech encoders emit 50 Hz frames (Whisper in Voxtral; arXiv 2507.13264) while text is far sparser.
  • Each choice tries to give the LLM a text-like-cost sequence without losing prosody, speaker identity or timing.
  • If the backbone sees embeddings and emits tokens for a separate renderer, the LLM can be swapped, frozen or lightly tuned (Freeze-Omni freezes it; arXiv 2411.00774).
  • Open problem: keeping text-level intelligence once the model must speak.
  • URO-Bench reports that open-source spoken dialogue models lag their backbone LLMs on instruction following and suffer catastrophic forgetting (arXiv 2502.17810).
Decision Common pick Inference
Input representation Continuous embeddings from an ASR-pretrained encoder at about 12.5 Hz
Output representation Semantic or codec tokens, rarely continuous
Text/speech coupling Text-first or interleaved
Streaming/duplex scheme Chunked, with a duplex layer on top
Acoustic renderer AR LM for content, flow or ConvNet for acoustics
In the interview, say:

"I split any S2S system into five choices: input rep, output rep, text coupling, duplex scheme and renderer. Most new models differ in one or two of them, not all five."

Three traps to avoid out loud
  • MiniMax-Speech introduces a "Flow-VAE" (arXiv 2505.07916). Do not read the name as flow matching.
  • Qwen3-Omni's waveform decoder is a lightweight causal ConvNet over a multi-codebook codec, not a DiT or flow model (arXiv 2509.17765).
  • MaskGCT is mask-and-predict in both stages (arXiv 2409.00750). Do not call it flow matching.
