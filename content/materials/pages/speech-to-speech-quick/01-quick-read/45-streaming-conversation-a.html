Streaming conversation: rates, Moshi and full-duplex models

Stage Rate Period
Mel frames (25 ms window, 10 ms hop; arXiv 2212.04356) 100 Hz 10 ms
Whisper encoder (stride 2) 50 Hz 20 ms
After Voxtral's 4x adapter (arXiv 2507.13264) 12.5 Hz 80 ms
Mimi codec frames (arXiv 2410.00037) 12.5 Hz 80 ms
Mimi tokens: \( 12.5 \times 8 \) (derived) 100 tokens/s 1.1 kbps (\( \times 11 \) bits)
  • Moshi runs text, its own audio and user audio on one 12.5 Hz clock: 17 streams per 80 ms frame (arXiv 2410.00037).
  • A 4096-dim, 32-layer temporal transformer runs once per frame; a 1024-dim, 6-layer depth transformer fills the sub-tokens.
  • Moshi's text stream is about 65% PAD, so it fits the audio clock; GLM-4-Voice instead interleaves 13 text : 26 speech tokens (arXiv 2412.02612).
  • Moshi's latency is 160 ms in theory and about 200 ms in practice; DSM applies a fixed delay per stream, 2.5 s default for ASR text (arXiv 2509.08753).
  • Duplex families: dual-stream (Moshi), chunked (SyncLLM, arXiv 2409.15594), flattened (OmniFlatten, arXiv 2410.17799), control head (MinMo, arXiv 2501.06282; Freeze-Omni, arXiv 2411.00774), action channel (DuplexSLA, arXiv 2605.20755).
  • Latency figures use different definitions and are not comparable across systems.
  • Tokens per second is frame rate times codebooks, and bitrate adds the bits per token: \( 12.5 \times 8 \times 11 = 1100 \) bps for Mimi (derived).
  • Lower output rates shorten sequences but cost intelligibility: SiTok WER is 23.0, 4.06 and 3.05 at 6.25, 12.5 and 25 Hz (arXiv 2602.06602). Timing logic
In the interview, say:

"Mimi is 12.5 Hz, so 80 ms a frame, 100 tokens a second and 1.1 kbps. Text and both audio streams share that clock, which is why there is no explicit turn state."
