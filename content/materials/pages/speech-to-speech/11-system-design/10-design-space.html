The design space: three ways to build a voice agent

Every voice agent has to decide where listening, thinking and speaking live. There are three common answers, and they sit on a line from most modular to most native. You pick a point on it by how much you value control, the data you can get, and timing that feels human.

Think of a call centre. In a cascade, a transcriber, an analyst and a voice actor pass notes on paper. In the unified design, one agent hears and speaks, and phones an expert for facts. In native full duplex, a single person does everything in one breath.

How it works

The cascade is the familiar pipeline: streaming speech recognition, a text LLM, streaming text-to-speech. Its weakness is the text boundary. Emotion, pace and uncertainty vanish when speech becomes a transcript, and the turn logic is a separate heuristic: a voice activity detector (VAD, introduced in The speech encoder) plus a turn manager that ends the turn after enough silence. Native full duplex sits at the other end. One model listens and speaks on the same clock and reasons inside, so nothing is lost and timing is learned, but it is hard to swap the reasoning, hard to evaluate, and starved of data. The mechanics are in Conversation.

The unified design sits between them. One speech LLM owns everything audio: streaming transcription with paralinguistic tags, turn-taking and expressive speech. Reasoning goes to a separate text LLM, called the way an agent calls a tool. No design wins on every axis, as the table shows.

Axis Cascade Unified speech LLM + text LLM Native full duplex
Emotion, rate and voice Lost at the text boundary; TTS voice is separate from what was heard Kept in the listen head and passed as tags; one model hears and voices the reply Kept end to end
Turn-taking Heuristic VAD and turn manager across three models Learned, in one model on one clock Native, every frame
Swap or upgrade the reasoning Easy Easy: the LLM sees text Hard: retrain or distil
Tools, safety, evaluation In text; evaluated per stage In text (retrieval and moderation run on transcript and reply); errors stay attributable per head Harder: text is a side stream; end-to-end evaluation only
Latency Stages stack, one hop per model One extra hop (the LLM's first token), covered by a filler Lowest; no hop
Data Abundant for each stage Speech data for the heads only Dual-channel conversation is scarce
Inference: how to choose

Inference The target is the unified speech LLM with a downstream text LLM. It keeps the cascade's strengths (swappable reasoning, text-level tools and safety, attributable errors) and learns the timing the cascade has to fake. The cascade is the baseline: the launch fallback, the comparison point and the first generator of training dialogue. Native full duplex is the research track, because the data and the evaluations are the long pole.

There are precedents for each piece. Kyutai's streaming models apply one architecture to both recognition and synthesis by delaying the text behind the audio or the audio behind the text. Qwen3-Omni splits a Thinker from a Talker, the in-model analogue of the tool-call split. Freeze-Omni and MinMo keep a text LLM and add a small decision module beside it.

Adding a face: cascaded or joint

The same choice returns when the agent has an avatar. A cascaded avatar makes the voice first and a face model second; a joint avatar generates sound and picture in one network. The trade-offs, including why the 2026 pattern is a distilled streaming student, are in Problems in audio-visual systems. What goes wrong

  • The hop erases the latency gain. The LLM's first token arrives after end of turn, so without a filler the unified design is no faster than a good cascade.
  • Tags the LLM cannot use. Anything the tags omit, the LLM never sees.
  • Two models drifting apart across versions. Version them and evaluate them together.
  • Going native too early. Real dual-channel conversation with genuine overlap is scarce, and the authors of one duplex training method describe a tension between interactive dynamics and reasoning ability.
In the interview, say:

"I would build one speech model that owns everything audio, with reasoning in a swappable text LLM behind a tool call. A cascade is the baseline and fallback, and native full duplex is where the speech model goes next, once the data and evaluations are there."
