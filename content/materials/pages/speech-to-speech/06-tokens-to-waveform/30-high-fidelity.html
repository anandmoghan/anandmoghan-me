Getting high fidelity out

Fidelity is set at the last stages, and several independent knobs control it. Think of a photo: resolution, compression level, sharpening and the quality of the print all matter, and fixing one does not fix the others. For audio the knobs are sample rate, bitrate, training losses, vocoder choice and any post-processing.

How it works

Each knob limits the output at a different place, so the weakest one wins.

  • Sample rate sets the top of the frequency band. Most systems here use 24 kHz mels or audio (F5-TTS, CosyVoice 2, Mimi); DAC targets 44.1 kHz.
  • Codec bitrate sets how much of the signal the tokens can carry. DAC's quality score (ViSQOL, defined in Judging a tokenizer) rises from 3.76 at 1.78 kbps to 4.18 at 8 kbps.
  • Training losses set what "close" means. Reconstruction losses reward matching the reference; adversarial and feature-matching losses reward sounding real.
  • Vocoder sets how well mel becomes waveform, as the previous section covers.
  • Post-processing such as super-resolution can widen the band afterwards. Some models fold this into the decoder: VoxCPM2's AudioVAE encodes 16 kHz audio and reconstructs 48 kHz, with no separate model.

The losses matter more than they look. Mimi trained with only adversarial and feature-matching losses had worse objective scores but sounded better to listeners (the MUSHRA numbers are in Codec mechanics). Objective reconstruction scores and listener preference can disagree.

What goes wrong

  • Metallic or buzzy timbre. This typically points at the tokenizer or decoder (too few codebooks, weak decoder), not the LM; the sampling-setting test is in Multi-codebook patterns. Inference Catch it with codec reconstruction scores and listening tests.
  • Band-limited data, wide-band model. DAC's authors warn that models trained on data whose true sampling rate is lower than its nominal rate often fail to reconstruct content above that true rate. Upsampling hides the gap but does not fill it.
  • High rate, wrong model. Stable Audio Open generates 44.1 kHz stereo yet its authors state it cannot produce intelligible speech.
  • Metrics that mislead. UTMOS predicts naturalness, not fidelity to a reference. F5-TTS scores 3.72 against a ground truth of 3.53 on one test set, but 3.89 against 4.09 on another. Report a listening test or ViSQOL alongside it.
  • Fixing the wrong knob. Raising the sample rate cannot repair a low-bitrate tokenizer, and a bigger vocoder cannot restore bandwidth the data never had.
In the interview, say:

"Fidelity is capped in several places: bandwidth of the training data and sample rate, codec bitrate, the losses used to train the decoder, and the vocoder. Adversarial losses can sound better while scoring worse on reconstruction metrics, so I always include a listening test. A buzzy timbre that survives every sampling setting is a tokenizer or decoder problem."
