Codec decoders and vocoders

Both turn a compact representation into a waveform, but they are built differently. A codec decoder is half of an autoencoder: it was trained together with the encoder that made its input. A vocoder is a separate model, trained to turn a mel spectrogram, made by anyone, into audio.

The hard part for both is phase (see STFT, magnitude and phase). A mel spectrogram has no phase, and a codec's frames are learned features, so the decoder has to invent a waveform whose phase is consistent across frames. Why mel discards it is in Mel spectrograms.

How it works

A codec decoder is trained end to end with the encoder and quantiser to reconstruct audio, so it learns to expect exactly the codes the quantiser produces. DAC compresses 44.1 kHz audio about 90 times, to 8 kbps. The training losses are in Codec mechanics.

A vocoder is trained on pairs of mel spectrogram and waveform. HiFi-GAN generates raw waveforms with a GAN, using a multi-period discriminator plus a multi-scale one, with feature-matching and mel L1 losses. BigVGAN generalises to unseen speakers and recording conditions without fine-tuning by adding two ideas. A Snake activation adds a sine wave to its input, which suits the repeating cycles of voiced speech and music. Anti-aliased resampling upsamples, filters and downsamples around each nonlinearity, so new high frequencies do not fold back as false low-frequency buzz.

Vocoders recover phase in one of three ways.

Strategy How phase comes back Notes
Iterative (Griffin-Lim) Alternates between a phase estimate and a consistent STFT Classical; slow and artefact-prone
Time-domain GAN (HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN) The network writes samples directly; phase is implicit in the waveform it learns to produce Discriminators judge whether the result sounds real
Spectral (Vocos) The network generates Fourier spectral coefficients, then an inverse STFT makes the waveform Reports an order-of-magnitude speed-up over time-domain GAN vocoders at matching quality

Newer decoders attack the other cost, the number of steps. Flow2GAN pretrains with flow matching, then fine-tunes with a lightweight GAN so that 1, 2 or 4 steps suffice. It predicts the endpoint (the clean audio) instead of the velocity, which avoids trouble in silent regions, and weights the loss by spectral energy so quiet regions count. The one-step MeanFlow decoder goes further and jumps from noise to the waveform in a single step. Teacher-student training for fewer steps is introduced in Diffusion and flow: levers.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Codec decoder vs separate vocoder Codec decoder fits its codes exactly; a vocoder works with any mel A vocoder can be swapped or upgraded alone, but needs the right mel settings
Discriminator design Multi-period and multi-resolution discriminators push the output toward realistic fine structure More training cost; a harder GAN to stabilise Inference
Snake activation and anti-aliased resampling (BigVGAN) Generalisation to unseen speakers, languages and recording conditions Larger model (up to 112M parameters)
Spectral decoder (Vocos) Speed: generates Fourier coefficients and inverts them Output is limited to what an inverse STFT can express Inference
Few-step decoders (Flow2GAN, one-step MeanFlow) Flow2GAN runs in 1, 2 or 4 steps after GAN fine-tuning; the MeanFlow decoder makes a waveform from tokens in one step, with up to 17 times better real-time factor than multi-step baselines Flow2GAN adds a fine-tuning stage; the MeanFlow decoder needs fine-tuning to fix the latent-decoder mismatch, with negligible quality loss reported
Swap the vocoder, keep the model In F5-TTS, Vocos to BigVGAN lowered WER from 1.83 to 1.62 at 32 steps WER measures intelligibility, not audio quality directly

What goes wrong

  • Phase inconsistency. When overlapping frames disagree, audio sounds smeared, reverberant or hollow, sometimes called phasiness. Inference
  • Out-of-distribution input. A vocoder trained on clean read speech can fail on new speakers or noisy conditions. BigVGAN targets exactly this.
  • Mel mismatch. The acoustic model and vocoder must agree on bin count, hop and normalisation. Inference
  • Blaming the wrong stage. A metallic or buzzy timbre that survives every sampling setting points at the tokenizer or decoder, not the LM (the diagnosis is in Multi-codebook patterns). Inference
In the interview, say:

"A codec decoder is trained with its encoder; a vocoder is a separate model that turns mel into audio. Both have to invent phase, because mel threw it away. GAN vocoders like HiFi-GAN and BigVGAN do it in the time domain, and Vocos generates spectral coefficients and inverts them for speed."
