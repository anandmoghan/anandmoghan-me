Four routes from model output to audio

A speech language model does not write samples. It writes something compact: codec tokens, semantic tokens, continuous latents or mel frames (see Mel spectrograms). A second component then renders that into a waveform.

Think of an architect's drawing and the finished building. The model produces the drawing; the decoder or vocoder is the construction crew that fills in every brick. The four routes differ in what the drawing contains, and so in how much work the crew has to do.

How it works

The diagram shows the four endings. The numbered notes below it say what each one means in practice.

  1. Codec tokens, codec decoder. The LM predicts every codebook of each frame, and a decoder trained together with the codec turns frames into samples. Only one stage follows the LM, and it can be causal (see Streaming encoders). Moshi's Mimi decoder works this way with an 80 ms frame.
  2. Semantic tokens, flow matching, mel, vocoder. The LM predicts cheap, coarse tokens that carry content but little voice detail. A flow-matching model restores the voice from a speaker prompt and outputs a mel spectrogram, and a vocoder turns that into a waveform. CosyVoice 2 does this with a 50 Hz mel. The flow stage is covered in Diffusion and flow.
  3. Continuous latents, VAE decoder. A diffusion head predicts a continuous latent per frame (VibeVoice uses 7.5 frames per second), and the tokenizer's own decoder returns audio. Some "diffusion TTS" systems, such as DiTTo-TTS, diffuse in a mel-VAE space and still finish with a vocoder, so they end like route 2.
  4. Mel frames, vocoder. The LM regresses continuous mel frames directly, and a vocoder renders them. MELLE does this with HiFi-GAN.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Route 1: codec tokens One causal stage after the LM; audio can leave right after each frame The LM must predict every codebook of every frame (depth transformer in Moshi, multi-token prediction in Qwen3-Omni) Inference
Route 2: semantic tokens plus flow Cheap tokens for the LM; the flow stage restores voice detail from a speaker prompt Inference Extra stages and look-ahead; Kimi-Audio's look-ahead delays only the first chunk
Route 3: continuous latents Fewer decoding steps at a low frame rate; no quantisation loss Needs a diffusion head; the decoder still has to stream
Route 4: mel regression Simple target; reuse a standard vocoder Continuous regression is harder to sample from than discrete tokens Inference; phase is left to the vocoder
Predicting samples directly Nothing to decode A token-by-token LM at 24 kHz would face 1,920 samples per 12.5 Hz frame, a sequence 1,920 times longer (the arithmetic is in Waveform and sample rate). Only flow-matching models such as BareWave work on the waveform itself, and they are the exception

Recent work also shrinks the last stage instead of choosing a different route. A one-step decoder applies MeanFlow to collapse route 2's flow, mel and vocoder chain into one stage, and BareWave needs no pretrained component at test time. Both are new, so treat them as a direction rather than the default. Inference The decoder-side details are in Codec decoders and vocoders.

Model-by-model examples of each route are in From tokens to waveform in the literature review. The tokenizers behind route 1 are in Tokenizers.

What goes wrong

  • Choosing by name instead of by ending. "Diffusion TTS" can end in a mel and a vocoder or in a VAE decoder. Ask what the last box is.
  • Streaming surprises. Routes 2 and 4 add a vocoder, and route 2 adds a flow stage with look-ahead, so first-audio latency adds up across stages.
  • Voice detail lost at the token stage. Semantic tokens drop voice detail by design. If the flow stage or speaker prompt is weak, the voice drifts. Inference
In the interview, say:

"There are four endings: codec tokens through a codec decoder, semantic tokens through flow to mel to a vocoder, continuous latents through a VAE decoder, or mel frames through a vocoder. Streaming duplex systems like Moshi and Qwen3-Omni use the codec route, because it has one causal stage after the LM. The flow route buys voice quality at the cost of more stages and look-ahead, and newer work is collapsing that last stage into one step."
