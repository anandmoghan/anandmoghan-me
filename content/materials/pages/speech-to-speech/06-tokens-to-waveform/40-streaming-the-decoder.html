Streaming the decoder

A live voice cannot wait for the whole sentence before it starts decoding. The decoder must produce audio chunk by chunk as tokens arrive. Anything it needs from the future is look-ahead (introduced in Look-ahead), and here it is delay the listener hears.

It is like a live translator at a conference: starting early is fast but risky, waiting for the next few words is safer but slower.

How it works

There are two ways to stream the last stage. A causal decoder uses only past frames, so each frame becomes samples as soon as it exists; Mimi is causal for both encoding and decoding, with an 80 ms frame. A chunked decoder, typically a flow model, renders a chunk at a time and peeks at the next chunk's tokens to keep the boundary smooth. The chunk masks and look-ahead for flow decoders are explained in Streaming a flow decoder; here only the cost matters: one block of look-ahead is one block of delay. Inference

The first packet arrives after the LM time to the first chunk, plus the look-ahead the decoder waits for, plus the decode time itself.

System How it streams the decode
Mimi (Moshi) Causal encoder and decoder; 80 ms frames
Qwen3-Omni A lightweight causal convolutional network replaces block-wise diffusion
Qwen2.5-Omni Codec diffusion transformer with 2 blocks of lookback and 1 block of look-ahead
CosyVoice 2 Chunk-aware causal flow matching serving streaming and non-streaming from one model
Kimi-Audio Chunk-wise detokenizer with look-ahead (example: 1 s chunk, 4 future semantic tokens)
GLM-4-Voice 0.8 s decoder blocks; at least 10 speech tokens are needed for the first output

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Causal, low-rate codec Fewer steps per second of audio and no future context needed The LM must produce all codebooks per frame
Chunk-aware training One model serves both streaming and offline (CosyVoice 2 mixes attention masks in training) Streaming quality can trail the offline mask
Look-ahead size Smooths chunk boundaries One block of look-ahead costs one block of delay
Fewer flow steps Distillation and mean-flow decoders cut decoder time; FlashTTS reports 325 ms first-packet latency at 2 steps Needs distillation; reported figures use different definitions
Chunk size First-packet time and throughput Decision wait averages half a chunk (Conversation clock); small chunks mean more boundaries

First-packet numbers from different papers use different definitions and hardware, so do not compare across them. As reference points: Moshi reports 160 ms theoretical and about 200 ms in practice, and Qwen3-Omni reports a 234 ms theoretical cold-start first packet. The conversational side of latency is in Conversation.

What goes wrong

  • Chunk seams. Clicks or prosody jumps at chunk boundaries. Streaming trades first-packet time against boundary quality. Teams catch them by measuring boundary discontinuity and listening. Fixes are overlap, look-ahead and crossfade.
  • Hidden look-ahead. A decoder with a block of look-ahead adds that block to latency, which is easy to miss if only the LM is profiled.
  • Missing a deadline. A duplex model has an 80 ms deadline each frame. A late decode is audible as a gap. Measure the worst frame, not the average.
  • Quoting latency without a definition. State whether it is theory, first packet, first chunk or total, plus batch size, GPU and cold start.
In the interview, say:

"The decoder streams too, and its look-ahead is latency: one block of look-ahead is one block of delay. A causal codec decoder like Mimi's pays none; chunked flow decoders pay a look-ahead on the first chunk. I budget first-packet time per stage and watch chunk seams, which show up as clicks or prosody jumps."
