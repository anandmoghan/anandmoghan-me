STFT: magnitude and phase

A raw waveform hides what a speech model cares about: which frequencies (pitches) are present, and when. The short-time Fourier transform (STFT) makes that visible. It slides a short window along the signal and, at each position, asks how much of each frequency is in the window.

Think of listening to a song through a keyhole that shows only a quarter of a second at a time, and writing down a chord for each peek. For every frequency, each peek yields two numbers: how strong it is (magnitude) and where it is in its cycle (phase).

How it works

  1. Cut the waveform into overlapping windows. Whisper uses a 25 ms window and a 10 ms hop at 16 kHz, which is 400 samples per window and a new window every 160 samples.
  2. Multiply each window by a smooth taper, so its edges do not create false frequencies.
  3. Run a fast Fourier transform (FFT) on each window. The result is one complex number per frequency bin.
  4. Split each complex number into magnitude (its length) and phase (its angle).

Magnitude says how much energy sits at a frequency in that frame. Phase says where each component is in its cycle, which fixes how the components line up in time.

Is the STFT reversible?

It depends on what you keep.

What you keep Reversible? How audio comes back
Complex STFT (magnitude and phase) Yes, if the window and hop overlap correctly Inverse FFT per frame, then overlap-add
Magnitude only No: many signals share one magnitude Phase retrieval, such as Griffin-Lim, or a neural vocoder
Mel spectrogram No, and lossier: the mel filterbank also merges frequency bins Neural vocoder (HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN, Vocos)

What phase adds

Phase is what makes a sound sharp and consistent. A plosive such as "p" is sharp because many components peak at the same instant; keep the magnitude and scramble the phase, and that energy smears across the frame. Overlapping frames must also agree on phase for overlap-add to rebuild a clean signal, and inconsistent phase is what phase retrieval has to repair. Inference

The research record is mixed. Early work argues that phase carries much of a signal's structure. Later listening tests find that for speech-length windows the phase spectrum can contribute to intelligibility comparably to magnitude, when the window is chosen well. Yet speech recognisers fed MFCC features did much worse on phase-only input. So phase matters for what people hear, and matters less for classic recognition front ends.

Inference Models avoid predicting phase directly because it wraps every \( 2\pi \) and looks noisy from frame to frame. Most systems model magnitude or mel and let a vocoder or codec decoder supply consistent phase. Vocos is the exception: it generates the spectral coefficients itself.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Window length (such as 25 ms) Long windows resolve frequencies finely; short windows locate events in time You cannot have both; speech settles on tens of milliseconds Inference
Hop (such as 10 ms) Feature frame rate (100 Hz for a 10 ms hop) and overlap between windows Smaller hop means more frames and more compute; too little overlap breaks overlap-add
Keep complex vs magnitude only Whether inversion is exact Complex values are hard to model; magnitude-only needs phase retrieval later
Phase retrieval method Griffin-Lim iterates between a phase estimate and a consistent STFT; Fast Griffin-Lim speeds it up Slower and more artefact-prone than learned vocoders
Let a vocoder generate the signal HiFi-GAN and BigVGAN generate the waveform; Vocos generates spectral coefficients and inverts them Needs training; Vocos reports an order-of-magnitude speed-up over time-domain GAN vocoders at matching quality

How vocoders recover phase is the subject of Tokens to waveform.

What goes wrong

  • Phase artefacts. Griffin-Lim output sounds smeared or reverberant, because the recovered phase is only approximately consistent. Inference
  • Window and hop that do not overlap properly. The inverse STFT then cannot reconstruct the signal exactly.
  • Treating the magnitude spectrogram as the whole signal. Different waveforms share one magnitude, so a model that outputs only magnitude leaves a design question (who supplies phase) unanswered.
In the interview, say:

"The STFT gives a complex number per time-frequency cell: magnitude says how much, phase says where in the cycle. Keep both and it inverts exactly. Keep magnitude or mel and you have thrown phase away, so something downstream has to invent a consistent one: Griffin-Lim classically, a neural vocoder or codec decoder today."
