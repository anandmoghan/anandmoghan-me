Mel spectrograms

A mel spectrogram is a compressed picture of the STFT magnitude. Time runs along one axis, a few dozen frequency bands along the other, and brightness shows energy. It keeps fine detail at low frequencies, where speech carries most of its information, and blurs the high frequencies together.

It is the speech equivalent of a thumbnail: small, easy for a network to read, and not enough to rebuild the original photo exactly.

How it works

  1. Take the STFT magnitude for each frame: a list of how much energy sits at each frequency, like the bars of a graphic equaliser.
  2. Group those bars with a bank of overlapping triangular filters, spaced on the mel scale: fine steps at low pitch, coarse steps at high pitch, the way hearing works.
  3. Take the logarithm, because loudness is heard on a log scale and the log keeps one very loud band from swamping the rest.

The result has one column per hop and one row per mel band. Whisper's front end is the canonical example: an 80-channel log-magnitude mel spectrogram from 16 kHz audio, with a 25 ms window and a 10 ms hop, in 30 s segments (large-v3 uses 128 bands). How an encoder then reads these frames is the topic of Listening.

Inference Models like mel features because they are far smaller than the raw STFT (80 numbers per frame instead of hundreds of bins), their spacing follows how listeners hear pitch, and a log-mel is a stable, bounded input that behaves like an image for convolutional stems.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Number of mel bins (80, 100, 128) Frequency detail available to the model and the vocoder More bins mean more detail but a bigger input; Whisper moved from 80 to 128 in large-v3
Hop size Mel frame rate; Whisper's 10 ms hop gives 100 Hz, a 256-sample hop at 24 kHz gives about 94 Hz (derived) More frames for a flow or autoregressive model to produce
Match mel settings to the vocoder The vocoder is trained on one mel configuration; the acoustic model must produce it Inference Changing either side means retraining the vocoder
Predict mel vs a codec token Mel is continuous and dense; MELLE regresses mel frames autoregressively, CosyVoice 2 samples a 50 Hz mel with flow matching Needs a vocoder and cannot stream as simply as a causal codec decoder
Better vocoder, same mel In F5-TTS, swapping Vocos for BigVGAN lowered WER (figures in Decoders and vocoders) Larger vocoder; WER (word error rate) is an intelligibility proxy, not a direct quality score

The generation side (mel to waveform) is covered in Tokens to waveform.

What goes wrong

  • Information is gone. The filterbank merges bins and the STFT phase is already dropped, so a mel spectrogram cannot be inverted exactly. A vocoder must regenerate fine pitch detail and phase.
  • Mismatched mel settings. An acoustic model that outputs a different bin count, hop or normalisation from the one the vocoder was trained on produces garbled or off-pitch audio. Inference
  • Quiet frames and the log. A log of near-zero energy exaggerates noise in silent regions, so pipelines normalise and floor the values. Inference
  • Unseen conditions. Vocoders trained on one corpus can fail on new speakers or recording conditions; BigVGAN was built to generalise to those without fine-tuning.
In the interview, say:

"A log-mel spectrogram is STFT magnitude pushed through a mel filterbank and a log: compact, perceptually spaced, easy for a network to read. It is lossy, so it needs a vocoder to become audio, and the mel settings must match between the model and the vocoder."
