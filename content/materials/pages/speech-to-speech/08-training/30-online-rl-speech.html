Online RL for speech generation

Imagine a singer practising with scorers listening from the next room. One counts wrong words, one checks the voice still sounds like the singer, one rates how pleasant it is. The singer tries a phrase eight times, sees which tries scored best, and shifts toward them. Online RL for speech is this loop, with models as the scorers.

The machinery is the GRPO loop from Aligning a flow model with rewards: sample a group of rollouts per prompt, score each, compare within the group, update with a KL penalty to the reference. This section covers what is specific to speech. The policy is usually a codec or discrete-token language model, sometimes a flow-matching model, and rewards come from the decoded audio, not from the tokens. That one fact drives most of the engineering cost.

How it works

Decoding rollouts to audio. Each rollout becomes a waveform, then goes through a recognition model and a speaker model, so the reward is a function of sound. Decoding and reward-model calls dominate cost. Cheaper options are rewards on codec tokens, cached reference embeddings and a small recogniser for the reward. Inference Larger groups give a better within-group baseline and cost proportionally more decoding. When every rollout in a group scores alike, the advantage is zero and the update is empty.

The reward stack. A common stack combines several signals, each normalised within the group before they are summed. Quality metrics themselves (MOS, speaker similarity, judges) are defined in Measuring speech generation.

Reward How it is computed What it protects
Intelligibility Character or word error from an off-the-shelf ASR on the decoded audio Words come out right
Speaker similarity Cosine similarity of speaker embeddings against the reference voice The voice stays the same
Quality predictors Learned naturalness scores Audio sounds clean and natural
Reward models and audio-LLM judges A generative reward model with a written rationale, or a large audio model that rates or compares clips Naturalness, expressiveness and paralinguistics where no formula exists, with biases
Timing rewards (duplex) Voice-activity events on generated audio against annotated agent events Turn initiation, backchannels, yielding, participation

Timing rewards for duplex. Per-axis rewards cover pause handling, turn-taking, backchanneling and user interruption. Training uses windows around annotated agent events instead of whole conversations. A text-quality reward is added so timing gains do not cost content.

Validate the reward model first. A reward model is a judge you must test like any model. Audio-LLM judges show verbosity and position bias, and the best judge in one study agreed with human pairwise preference under 70% of the time. On a benchmark that turns mean-opinion-score data into pairwise "which sounds better" tests, simple scalar models scored best, above 74% accuracy, but did worse on synthetic than on human speech and struggled when two clips differed little. Randomise clip order, ensemble several aspects, and check the judge against human labels on the audio you will generate, including close pairs.

Flow models. A flow model's ODE sampler is deterministic, so it gives neither varied rollouts nor a per-step probability. Flow-GRPO therefore converts the sampler to an SDE; GROW skips the conversion and reweights the flow-matching loss by group advantage. Both are worked through in Why RL needs the SDE.

Best-of-N as the baseline. Best-of-N needs no training: sample N candidates, score them, return the top one. A study of codec language models trained with GRPO on learned scores for speaking style, naturalness, likability and arousal, plus a character-error limit against transcript drift, found that each reward mainly lifted its own metric. Human A/B tests showed uneven transfer, and best-of-8 reranking matched GRPO perceptually. So run best-of-N with the same reward first; if RL cannot beat it, you are paying training cost only to save inference cost. Inference

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Reward weights Balance between accuracy, voice, quality and timing Weight accuracy too high and prosody flattens; too low and words slip
KL strength How far the policy may drift High KL is safe and slow; low KL is fast and open to hacking
Reward granularity Per phone, per character or per utterance Finer scoring helps languages with ambiguous readings; one report cut Japanese reading error with kana-level scoring
Best-of-N as baseline Whether RL is needed at all Costs N times the inference compute but no training; RL pays once and then samples once
Judge choice and validation Which model scores quality, and how far it is trusted on close pairs Learned judges capture naturalness, add cost and can be exploited; validation needs human-labelled pairs from your domain
Event windows for duplex Which part of a conversation is rolled out Focuses reward on timing events; loses long-range context

Trade-offs are engineering reasoning from the cited reports. Inference From text GRPO you keep group sampling, advantage normalisation, KL and clip monitoring. What is new is decoding to audio, competing rewards, and hacking that shows up as flat prosody, not a bad transcript. The same loop trains recognition and voice agents; see RL beyond speech generation.

What goes wrong

  • Prosody collapse under error-rate rewards. Optimising character error alone made speech monotone, and adding speaker similarity destabilised training in the same study. The fix there was iterative DPO (see post-training) on a few hundred human preference pairs per round.
  • Semantic drift. Optimising timing can degrade what the model says; keep a response-quality reward.
  • Reward equals evaluation. If the training ASR is also the test ASR, WER gains can be hacking. Judge bias and reward exploitation more broadly are in Reward hacking and its guards.
In the interview, say:

"I would run GRPO with a composite of recognition error, speaker similarity and a quality model, normalised per group, with KL to the reference. For duplex I would add per-axis timing rewards from voice-activity events plus a text-quality term. Single-score rewards get hacked, so I validate every reward against held-out judges and listeners."
