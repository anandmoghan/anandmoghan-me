The staged training recipe

Teaching a text model to talk is like teaching a well-read person a second language. You do not start from zero. You keep what they know, introduce the new sounds slowly, and test their old knowledge after every lesson so it does not slip.

Nearly every open speech-to-speech model follows that shape. Start from a strong text LLM, line audio up with it, pretrain on mixed speech and text, tune on spoken dialogue, then add the conversational behaviour. The real choice is whether the text backbone stays frozen or trains on a mixed corpus.

How it works

  1. Tokenizer and codec first. Speech must become tokens before anything else (see Tokenizers). Freeze it after a reconstruction check.
  2. Align audio to a text LLM. The audio encoder and adaptor from Audio meets text map the waveform into the space the LLM reads. Train both on recognition data while the LLM stays frozen. Every clip comes with its exact transcript, so the model learns that this sound means these words without disturbing the text weights.
  3. Mixed speech-text pretraining. Unfreeze the LLM and train on text only, audio only and interleaved sequences, where text and speech alternate inside one sequence (see Text and speech together). Interleaved data is usually synthesised: render chosen spans of a text corpus into speech tokens with TTS and a tokenizer. The model learns that the two forms mean the same thing. One report lifted spoken question answering from 13% to 31% this way.
  4. Spoken instruction tuning. Train on dialogue where the user speaks and the model answers in speech. An LLM writes the dialogue and TTS renders both sides. Real recorded turns and many TTS voices keep the model from copying one synthetic style.
  5. Duplex alignment. Add turn-taking from real two-channel conversation, a small head on frozen states, or both. One base duplex model fine-tuned on about 2,000 hours of phone conversation. Another trained only a small predictor on 4,000 hours of human conversation.
  6. Agentic tuning. A first-party realtime report adds supervised tuning, a distillation stage, then GRPO in executable environments where the model calls tools and is scored on finishing the task. It reports the response rate to background speech falling from 73.0% to 13.0% on one duplex benchmark. The RL half is in RL beyond speech generation.

Frozen against mixed. Two recipes protect the text ability. Freezing the LLM keeps text ability by construction; it suits models that wrap a fixed LLM with a decoder and a state classifier, and it caps how deeply speech can reshape the representations. Training on a mixed corpus lets speech reach deeper, and the mix ratio becomes a tuned hyperparameter. One base model keeps half of its pretraining batches text-only; another pairs 585B text tokens with 585B audio tokens. An unbalanced mix is the main risk.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Text share in the mix How much text ability is rehearsed during speech training High share protects knowledge; it spends compute on text and slows speech learning
Freezing schedule Which parts train at each stage Freezing early avoids forgetting; freezing throughout caps speech quality
Interleaved synthetic data volume Speech-text alignment inside the LLM Scales cheaply and brings TTS artefacts
TTS voices and real turns Diversity of the spoken instruction data More of both reduces voice overfit; real data is scarce
Stage gates When a checkpoint may advance Catches forgetting early; slows iteration

Trade-offs are engineering reasoning from the cited recipes, not controlled comparisons. Inference Gate each stage on recognition error, a strict text question-answering score and, for duplex, timing.

What goes wrong

  • Forgetting. Adding speech can degrade knowledge and reasoning even for text inputs, and more for spoken queries. Measure the speech-versus-text accuracy gap on the same questions.
  • TTS-voice overfit and flat delivery. The model absorbs the voice and prosody of the synthetic data, and text-written dialogue carries no emotion or hesitation. Mix real turns and many voices.
  • Unbalanced mix. Too little text and the model forgets; too much and speech lags.
  • Timing from synthetic dialogue. Backchannels inserted at regular intervals teach a regular rhythm.
In the interview, say:

"I would keep the text LLM frozen while aligning audio, then unfreeze it with a high text share and interleaved synthetic data. Spoken tuning uses many TTS voices plus real turns. Every stage is gated on recognition error and strict text QA, because speech training erodes text ability."
