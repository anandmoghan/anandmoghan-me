Reward hacking and its guards

When a score becomes the goal, people find ways to raise it without doing the thing it measures. A student who learns the marking scheme and skips the subject is the usual picture. A policy under RL does this far more tirelessly, and in speech the scorers are themselves models with blind spots. A model can learn sounds that please the scorer and annoy a listener.

How it works

The pattern is the same every time. One optimised score rises, a held-out measure stalls or falls, and a human listener hears the damage. Each guard below breaks a different link in that chain.

Hack What you hear or see Guard
Single quality predictor optimised Its score climbs while held-out judges and listeners get worse Equal-weighted or calibrated composite; held-out judges
Error-rate and likelihood rewards Lower error rates and monotone prosody Iterative DPO with human preference pairs
Single enhancement metric Fast metric gains, degraded fidelity Multi-metric reward
Reward model exploited by acoustic artefacts Spurious reward with worse perceived quality Held-out judges and a listening panel
Judge verbosity and position bias Rewards follow length or order Randomised order; multi-aspect ensemble; validate on human labels
Timing optimised alone Semantic drift in what the model says LLM-based response-quality reward
Interaction reward met by stopping early Better interruption and pause scores, replies cut short Check response completeness next to every timing gain

The pairing of hack and guard is my own reading except where a source is named. Inference Evaluation in general, and what to measure beyond rewards, is in Evaluation and problems.

Monitoring. Track these during every run, with the definitions from online RL for speech (KL to the reference, zero-advantage groups):

  • Per-reward mean and variance: variance near zero means every rollout scores alike and the reward no longer separates them.
  • Fraction of zero-advantage groups: a rising fraction means wasted compute.
  • KL: a steady climb signals the policy is leaving the safe region.
  • Clip fraction: the share of updates that hit the clipping limit on step size (see Why RL needs the SDE); high values mean the policy is trying to move faster than the update allows.
  • End-of-sequence behaviour: a policy that learns to end early or never end can be reward hacking; one duplex study found a model gaming a hybrid reward through incomplete responses.

Add acoustic statistics: fundamental frequency (pitch) spread, energy, speaking rate, silence ratio and the duration distribution. A flat pitch spread or a shrinking duration range is an early signature of prosody collapse. Stop when held-out metrics degrade even if the training reward rises, and spot-check the audio by ear.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Held-out judges A second ASR, a second speaker model and a listening panel outside the reward Detects hacking; costs evaluation time
Composite reward, with a duration-ratio penalty against speech that is too fast or truncated Spreads pressure over several scores Resists gaming; weights need tuning
KL to the reference Limits drift Slows legitimate gains too
Human preference pairs Corrects what no verifiable reward sees A few hundred pairs per round help; labelling is slow
Early stopping on held-out metrics When training ends Safe; may leave gains unused

What goes wrong

  • Guards share blind spots with rewards. A held-out judge from the same family as the reward model fails the same way.
  • Train reward treated as the metric. Report held-out results from different models than the ones used in training.
  • Listening skipped, forgetting returns. Automatic metrics are optimisable, so a human listening gate is the final check. RL can also erode text ability; keep the strict text QA gate from earlier stages.
In the interview, say:

"Every reward I optimise gets a held-out twin I never train on: a second ASR, a second speaker model, and a listening panel. I watch pitch spread and speaking rate for prosody collapse, and I stop when held-out metrics fall even if the reward rises."
