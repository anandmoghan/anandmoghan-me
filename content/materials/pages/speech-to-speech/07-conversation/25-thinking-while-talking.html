Thinking while talking

A good listener starts forming an answer before you finish the sentence. A good speaker plans the next clause while saying the current one. A basic voice pipeline does neither: it waits for the user to stop, then thinks, then speaks, so every second of reasoning is a second of silence.

Duplex models open three ways to hide that reasoning time. The model can think while the user is still speaking, think while it is speaking, or hand the hard thinking to a larger partner model and keep talking in the meantime. All three use time that was already being spent on something else.

How it works

Thinking while listening. A duplex model that is not talking is usually idle: for every frame of the user's turn it just predicts "silence" for its own output. Chronological thinking replaces that idle output with reasoning that is strictly causal, meaning it updates its working answer from the audio heard so far and never peeks ahead. When the user stops, the model stops thinking and starts speaking at once, so the reasoning adds no delay.

The reasoning need not be in words. One approach feeds the hidden vector the model just produced back in as the next input, so a chain of silent thoughts builds up during the user's turn. Training uses an objective that learns these thoughts from ordinary dialogue, so nobody writes reasoning examples. Inference The cost is that the reasoning leaves no readable trace to inspect. A cascade of separate models can do the same: a trigger decides when the partial speech forms a meaningful prefix, and a background thinker keeps a running state while a foreground speaker tries a speculative answer.

Thinking while speaking. Chain-of-thought reasoning is too slow to finish before a real-time reply. The dual-brain idea splits the jobs: a reasoning component works out the answer and sets the pace, and a separate speaking component voices it without waiting for the whole chain to finish. Keeping them separate avoids the model switching between "thinking mode" and "talking mode", which can disturb the reasoning. Reported results are close to models that finish their reasoning before speaking, at much lower latency.

Fast talker, slow thinker. A tandem keeps a compact duplex model in charge of the floor and lets a larger model or a retriever work alongside it. The compact model starts answering immediately, and the larger model's text arrives while the sentence is under way and is injected into what the compact model says. It works because of a natural gap: the first words of an answer take time to say, and the facts need not be ready until the core of the answer arrives. The speech model stays small and the knowledge is borrowed, which is a different answer to the intelligence gap from freezing a large LLM. A variant splits the work into an interaction layer for streaming audio and video and a swappable thinking layer for reasoning and tools.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
Where the thinking happens Listening window, speaking window or a second model Listening costs no delay but works from a partial question; speaking needs pacing; a second model adds an interface
Reasoning form Words against hidden vectors Hidden vectors need no reasoning labels; words can be read and debugged
Trigger rule When a cascade starts to think Fixed chunks or silence splits break meaning; speculative answers waste work when they must be rolled back
Pacing the voice How far the speaker may run ahead of the reasoner More lead lowers latency; the voice may commit to words before the answer is settled Inference
Size of the fast model How much knowledge the front end must hold Small keeps real-time inference cheap; it leaves knowledge to the partner
Retrieval or reasoner choice Which partner supplies the facts One system lets the retrieval method change without retraining the front end; the answer is only as fast as the slowest partner

What goes wrong

  • Answering from a half-heard question. Thinking during the user's turn works from partial audio; a late "actually, I meant..." can invalidate the reasoning. Teams test with natural pauses and self-corrections.
  • Speaking before knowing. A voice that runs ahead of the reasoner can commit to a wrong opening it must then contradict. Inference
  • Dead air while the partner is slow. A tandem is only as good as its slow side; measure the time from the end of the user's turn to the first useful fact, not only to the first sound.
  • Lost audio detail across the hand-off. If only text crosses to the larger model, tone and hesitation are lost. Inference
  • Unfair comparisons. These papers use different benchmarks (spoken maths, speech-synthesised multi-turn QA, factuality sets), so their numbers do not compare.
In the interview, say:

"A duplex model wastes the user's whole turn predicting silence, so recent work spends it on causal or hidden-state reasoning that stops at turn end and adds no delay. The other option is a tandem: a small duplex model keeps the floor and a bigger model or retriever feeds it text while it talks. The open question is that the papers use different benchmarks, so I would test on my own use case."
