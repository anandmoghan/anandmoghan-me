Continuous latents and the VAE

A discrete codec rounds each frame to a codebook entry. A continuous tokenizer keeps the frame as a smooth vector instead. It is the difference between a switch with K positions and a dial.

The dial loses nothing to rounding, so the ceiling on naturalness is higher. But an LM cannot take a softmax over a dial, so a small diffusion or flow head must sample the next vector. That head is the subject of Diffusion and flow.

How it works

A variational autoencoder (VAE) learns an encoder and a decoder. The true likelihood of the audio under the model cannot be computed, so training maximises a quantity that is guaranteed to be no larger than it, a lower bound on the log-likelihood (the evidence lower bound, ELBO). The encoder does not output a single latent; it outputs a mean \( \mu \) and a standard deviation \( \sigma \) per dimension, which define a Gaussian posterior. A prior, a centred Gaussian \( \mathcal{N}(0, I) \), sits opposite it.

The loss has two parts: reconstruct the audio, and keep the posterior near the prior. The KL divergence \( D_{\mathrm{KL}} \) measures how far one distribution is from another; it is zero when they match.

\[ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q(z \mid x)}\big[\log p(x \mid z)\big] - D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(z \mid x) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, I)\big) \]

With both sides Gaussian, the KL term has a closed form and needs no sampling. It pulls \( \mu \) toward 0 and \( \sigma \) toward 1, which keeps the latent space smooth and bounded.

\[ -D_{\mathrm{KL}} = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_j \left( 1 + \log \sigma_j^2 - \mu_j^2 - \sigma_j^2 \right) \]

To train through the sampling step, the reparameterisation trick moves the randomness into a noise variable.

\[ \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \qquad z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon \]

Now \( z \) is a deterministic function of \( \mu \) and \( \sigma \), so reconstruction gradients reach the encoder. At generation time nothing samples from the prior: the LM's diffusion or flow head produces the next latent from context, and the decoder turns it into audio. The prior only shapes the latent space while the tokenizer trains.

Speech and audio tokenizers keep this shape and add the codec's adversarial and spectral losses. VibeVoice's acoustic tokenizer is a sigma-VAE: the encoder predicts \( \mu \), and \( \sigma \) is a pre-defined distribution rather than learned. It is causal, downsamples 24 kHz audio by 3200x to 7.5 frames per second, and trains with DAC's discriminator and loss designs. Stable Audio Open's autoencoder has a VAE bottleneck of latent size 64, with the KL term down-weighted by \( 10^{-4} \); a diffusion transformer trains on 1024-step latent sequences of about 47 s.

Levers and optimisations

Lever What it changes Trade-off
KL weight How hard the posterior is held to the prior (Stable Audio Open: \( 10^{-4} \)) A small weight acts as a light regulariser, not a tight bottleneck. Inference Too large squeezes out detail.
Fixed or learned \( \sigma \) VibeVoice fixes \( \sigma \) to a pre-defined distribution Guards against variance collapse (a learned \( \sigma \) shrinking toward zero) in autoregressive use.
Latent size and rate 64 dimensions in Stable Audio Open; 7.5 Hz in VibeVoice Lower rates give shorter sequences. VibeVoice reports 80x better compression than EnCodec at comparable performance.
Causal encoder VibeVoice's tokenizer is causal Required for streaming; limits the context per frame.

Choosing between the two forms is a trade-off.

Discrete codes Continuous latents
Fidelity Quantization loss No quantization loss; higher ceiling
LM output Softmax over a vocabulary Needs a regression, diffusion or flow head
Sampling control Top-p over a categorical Weaker; temperature maps to noise injection in the head
Reinforcement learning Direct on tokens Harder; the per-step probability ratios that RL needs add overhead
Cost per step One LM step LM step plus a diffusion or flow pass

What goes wrong

  • Variance collapse. A learned \( \sigma \) can shrink toward zero, so the latent acts like a fixed number instead of a spread-out distribution; this is reported as a risk in autoregressive use, and VibeVoice's fixed \( \sigma \) exists to prevent it.
  • Per-step cost. A diffusion or flow pass on top of each LM step adds latency, which matters for duplex products.
  • Hard to align with RL. A flow-matching head produces each output through many small denoising steps, so policy-gradient methods must track a probability ratio at every step. How this is handled is covered in Diffusion and flow: rewards.
  • Over-regularised latents. Inference A heavy KL weight can remove the detail the decoder needs, which is why the weights used are small.

Inference Qwen3-Omni uses a codec plus a causal ConvNet decoder, which suggests continuous heads may stay niche for duplex products. For a duplex product I would start discrete and revisit.

A continuous tokenizer is the VAE version of a codec: a Gaussian latent with a small KL term, which removes quantization loss but needs a diffusion or flow head and makes RL harder.
