Look-ahead (right context) in streaming encoders

Look-ahead, also called right context, is how much future audio an encoder may see before it commits to an output for the current frame. Zero look-ahead means each output depends only on the past, so it can be emitted as soon as its frame arrives. N frames of look-ahead means each output waits for N more frames, which helps resolve ambiguous sounds and word boundaries but adds latency.

Reading "0LA" and "12LA"

Inference Shorthand such as 0LA and 12LA names a streaming configuration by its look-ahead in encoder frames: 0 frames or 12 frames. What 12 frames costs depends on the frame duration after subsampling, and on whether the look-ahead is applied per layer or per chunk (below).

Frame duration after subsampling 12 frames of look-ahead (derived)
40 ms (4× subsampling of a 10 ms hop) 480 ms
80 ms (8× subsampling of a 10 ms hop) 960 ms

For comparison, Speech ReaLLM uses 240 ms input frames with 960 ms of encoder right context (arXiv 2406.09569).

The latency and accuracy trade-off

NVIDIA's cache-aware streaming FastConformer paper measures the trade-off directly (arXiv 2312.17279). On LibriSpeech test-other with a hybrid model and chunk-aware look-ahead, the authors report:

Look-ahead latency RNNT WER (%) CTC WER (%)
0 ms 9.5 10.6
40 ms 9.0 10.1
240 ms 7.8 8.8
520 ms 7.5 8.4
680 ms 7.3 8.0
1,360 ms 6.3 7.1
Offline (full context) 5.0 5.7

Zero look-ahead costs about 50% relative WER against the 1,360 ms setting (derived, RNNT), and most of the gain arrives within a few hundred milliseconds. In the same paper, buffered (non-cache-aware) streaming reaches only 11.3% WER at 2,000 ms with RNNT, worse than cache-aware streaming with zero look-ahead.

Regular against chunk-aware look-ahead

How look-ahead is implemented matters as much as how much is allowed (arXiv 2312.17279).

Regular look-ahead Chunk-aware look-ahead
Mechanism Each self-attention layer sees M future steps Input split into chunks of size C; every frame sees its whole chunk, plus earlier chunks as cached left context, but never the next chunk
Effective look-ahead M × number of layers From C−1 frames (first frame in a chunk) to 0 (last frame)
Paper's example 17 layers, one frame each, 80 ms per frame (8x subsampling of a 10 ms hop): 17 × 80 ms = 1,360 ms Average latency is half the look-ahead size
RNNT WER at 1,360 ms 7.1% 6.3%

So "12LA" is cheap or expensive depending on whether it means 12 frames once at the chunk level, or 12 frames per layer that compound through the stack. Inference

One model, several look-aheads

The same paper trains a single model with multiple look-ahead settings and compares it to separate single-setting models on LibriSpeech test-other (arXiv 2312.17279). With RNNT, the multi-look-ahead model reports 6.2%, 5.5% and 5.2% WER at 40, 240 and 520 ms, against 6.4%, 5.9% and 5.4% for single-look-ahead models. That lets a product pick the latency at inference time.

Look-ahead in encoders built to stream

The FastConformer results retrofit look-ahead onto a standard design. Three newer releases put the dial into the architecture or the training recipe.

Model Look-ahead mechanism Reported results
Moonshine v2 (arXiv 2602.12241) Sliding-window self-attention at 50 Hz: 16 frames of left context in every layer, 4 frames of right context in the first two and last two layers, 0 in the middle layers; 4 × 20 ms = 80 ms look-ahead Open ASR average WER 12.01 (tiny, 33.6M parameters), 7.84 (small) and 6.65 (medium, 244.9M); on an Apple M3, response latency 258 ms for medium against 11,286 ms for Whisper Large v3
Voxtral Realtime (arXiv 2602.11298) A delay τ is embedded and injected through Ada RMS-Norm in every Transformer block; training samples it from 80 to 2400 ms in 80 ms steps (1 to 30 adapter frames) LibriSpeech clean / other WER 2.08 / 5.54 at 480 ms and 1.96 / 4.59 at 960 ms, against 1.84 / 3.66 for offline Whisper
Nemotron Speech streaming (NVIDIA model card) Cache-aware FastConformer-RNNT, 24 encoder layers, about 600M parameters; att_context_size sets the right context in 80 ms frames Chunk 0.08 s: average WER 8.43; 0.16 s: 7.67; 0.56 s: 7.07; 1.12 s: 6.93 (Open ASR datasets, without punctuation and capitalisation)

A later paper argues that the structural delay τ is a weak proxy for perceived latency, and measures a word-level emission delay instead (arXiv 2609.18333; details). Inference The curve has the same shape as the FastConformer table: the first few hundred milliseconds buy most of the accuracy. What differs is that one trained model can sit anywhere on it.

NVIDIA's Parakeet realtime end-of-utterance model card lists a cache-aware streaming FastConformer-RNNT with 80 to 160 ms latency.

Inference: where each setting fits in a voice agent

Zero look-ahead suits barge-in detection, end-of-turn signals and the fastest partials, where reacting early matters more than a perfect word. Larger look-ahead suits final transcripts handed to the reasoning LLM. A multi-look-ahead encoder can serve both from one model.

In the interview, say:

Look-ahead is the future audio the encoder waits for before committing a frame, so it trades latency for accuracy. NVIDIA's cache-aware FastConformer shows RNNT WER going from 9.5% at zero look-ahead to 6.3% at 1,360 ms, and chunk-aware look-ahead beats per-layer look-ahead at the same latency because it does not compound with depth. I would train one encoder with several look-aheads (or a delay input, as in Voxtral Realtime) and use zero for turn-taking and a larger one for final transcripts.
