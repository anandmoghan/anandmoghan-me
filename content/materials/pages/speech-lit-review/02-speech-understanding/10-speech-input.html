Speech input for LLMs

Three ways to feed speech to an LLM: continuous encoder embeddings, discrete semantic tokens, or codec tokens. Most models use the first.

Option What it is Used by
Continuous embeddings ASR-pretrained encoder, then a small projector into LLM space Voxtral (arXiv 2507.13264), Voxtral Realtime (arXiv 2602.11298), Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765), Qwen3.5-Omni (arXiv 2604.15804), Granite-speech (arXiv 2505.08699), Phi-4-multimodal (arXiv 2503.01743), Step-Audio 2 (arXiv 2507.16632), Canary-Qwen
Discrete semantic tokens Single-codebook VQ on an ASR encoder, built for AR generation GLM-4-Voice (12.5 Hz, 175 bps; arXiv 2412.02612)
Codec tokens Multi-codebook RVQ, first layer distilled toward a semantic teacher Moshi's Mimi (12.5 Hz, 8 codebooks, WavLM-distilled first layer; arXiv 2410.00037)
Hybrid Discrete and continuous summed Kimi-Audio (12.5 Hz semantic tokens from a VQ layer in the Whisper encoder, plus continuous Whisper features downsampled to 12.5 Hz; arXiv 2504.18425)

Per-model paths

Model Encoder and adapter Rate to LLM
Voxtral (arXiv 2507.13264) Whisper large-v3 encoder, 30 s chunks, MLP adapter with 4x downsample 12.5 Hz
Voxtral Realtime (arXiv 2602.11298) Causal encoder trained from scratch (970M, 50 Hz, 15 s sliding window), 4x MLP adapter, 3.4B decoder initialised from Ministral 3B 12.5 Hz
Qwen3-ASR (arXiv 2601.21337) / Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765) AuT encoder, 8x conv downsample, 1-8 s dynamic attention window 12.5 Hz
Qwen3.5-Omni (arXiv 2604.15804) AuT trained from scratch on 40M hours of audio-text pairs, four Conv2D blocks, 16x downsample (about 160 ms per frame) 6.25 Hz
Granite-speech (arXiv 2505.08699) CTC-trained Conformer plus windowed Q-Former; 100 Hz log-mel reduced 2x at the encoder input, then 5x in the Q-Former 10 Hz
Step-Audio 2 (arXiv 2507.16632) Frozen encoder at 25 Hz, adaptor with 2x downsampling 12.5 Hz
Qwen2-Audio (arXiv 2407.10759) Encoder initialised from Whisper-large-v3, plus a stride-2 pooling layer 25 Hz (40 ms per frame)

Why about 12.5 Hz is common, and where it is not

Whisper emits 50 Hz frames. Voxtral notes that 30 minutes of audio at 50 Hz is a sequence of 90k tokens, which is why it adds a 4x downsampling adapter (arXiv 2507.13264). Moshi's Mimi codec also runs at 12.5 Hz (arXiv 2410.00037).

The rate is a per-model choice and not a convergence point. Qwen3.5-Omni trains its AuT encoder from scratch at 6.25 Hz, half the 12.5 Hz of Qwen3-Omni, with 16x downsampling (arXiv 2604.15804). The Voxtral Realtime encoder is trained from scratch too (causal, 50 Hz, 15 s sliding window), then adds the same 4x adapter to reach 12.5 Hz (arXiv 2602.11298). Inference Encoders trained for the purpose set their own rate, so 12.5 Hz reflects the Whisper-descended and codec-descended designs.

Trade-offs

  • Continuous: richest acoustic and paralinguistic signal, easy to train as ASR, but the output side needs a separate path.
  • Discrete: one vocabulary for input and output, enables self-supervised next-token training, loses detail.
  • Codec: the model hears and speaks the same tokens, but multi-codebook streams cost more per frame. Inference This suits duplex.
  • Inference Downsampling to 10-12.5 Hz is the main sequence-length lever. It costs fine acoustic detail, which motivates hybrids like Kimi-Audio.
In the interview, say:

"Input is mostly an ASR-pretrained encoder, a small projector and about 12.5 Hz, though encoders trained from scratch pick their own rate (6.25 Hz in Qwen3.5-Omni). Discrete tokens appear when the same vocabulary must also be generated."
