Whisper and the Whisper encoder

Whisper is an encoder-decoder Transformer trained on 680,000 hours of weakly supervised audio. Its encoder has been the default front end for speech LLMs, though the newest streaming models train their encoders from scratch instead. Source: arXiv 2212.04356 unless stated.

Architecture

  • Confirmed Input is 16 kHz audio turned into an 80-channel log-magnitude mel spectrogram (25 ms window, 10 ms stride), in 30 s segments.
  • Confirmed The stem is two conv layers (width 3, GELU); the second has stride 2, so one encoder frame covers 20 ms.
  • Derived: the 10 ms hop gives 100 mel frames per second (100 Hz). The stride-2 conv halves that to one frame per 20 ms (50 Hz). A 30 s window is therefore 30 s / 10 ms = 3,000 mel frames and 3,000 / 2 = 1,500 encoder frames. Short audio is padded to 30 s, so compute is paid for the full window.

  • large-v3: same architecture, 128 mel bins instead of 80, and a Cantonese token. Reported OpenAI reports a 10% to 20% error reduction over large-v2 (OpenAI model card).
  • large-v3-turbo: a fine-tuned, pruned large-v3 with the decoder cut from 32 to 4 layers (OpenAI model card). The card lists it at 809M parameters.

Multitask token format

The decoder prompt carries the task as tokens: start-of-transcript, language, task (transcribe or translate), then timestamps or no-timestamps. One model therefore does ASR, X-to-English translation and language ID. Timestamps are quantised to 20 ms.

Training data and recipe

Model Data
v1 / v2 680,000 h weakly supervised: 117,000 h of non-English transcription across 96 other languages, 125,000 h of X-to-English translation, the rest English (paper).
large-v3 1 million hours of weakly labelled audio and 4 million hours of audio pseudo-labelled with large-v2 (OpenAI model card).
  • Filtering: drop machine-generated transcripts (all caps or all lower, no punctuation). Compare audio language ID (VoxLingua107-trained) with CLD2 on the transcript and drop mismatches; English transcripts of other-language audio are kept as translation data.
  • Optimiser: AdamW, gradient-norm clipping, linear decay after 2,048 warmup steps, batch 256, FP16 with activation checkpointing, 2 to 3 passes over the data.
  • Augmentation: none for the first models. Large-v2 trained 2.5x more epochs with SpecAugment, stochastic depth and BPE dropout.

Long-form decoding

Whisper slides a 30 s window over long audio and decodes with heuristics (paper section 4.5).

  • Beam search with beam 5.
  • Temperature fallback from 0, in steps of 0.2 up to 1.0, when average log-probability is below -1 or the gzip compression ratio exceeds 2.4.
  • Condition on the previous window's text only when temperature is below 0.5.
  • No-speech threshold 0.6 to skip silent windows.

Limitations

  • The model card warns that predictions may include text that was not spoken (hallucination) and that the sequence-to-sequence architecture is prone to repetitive text. It also states that the models cannot be used for real-time transcription out of the box.
  • The fixed 30 s window, non-causal encoder and autoregressive beam decoder make streaming hard.
  • A 2026 study that masked parts of the audio counted 51,797 insertions for Whisper-small, 86% of them repetition loops, and 10,365 for large-v3, 35% repetition. Decoders built on an explicit LLM produced 38x fewer insertions than Whisper-small, but a Q-Former encoder brought repetition back (arXiv 2604.21276).
Traps

Hallucination and repetition are documented limitations on the model card. Do not rank checkpoints on hallucination without your own measurement.

Inference Three issues recur. Padding to 30 s wastes compute on short turns. 50 Hz is too long a sequence for an LLM, so every system adds an adapter (50 Hz / 4 = 12.5 Hz). Bidirectional attention blocks streaming, so teams either make the encoder causal (GLM-4-Voice) or replace it (AuT, Voxtral Realtime).

Making the Whisper encoder stream

Whisper does not stream for three reasons.

  • The encoder takes a fixed 30 s window (arXiv 2212.04356), and Voxtral describes it as a fixed receptive field of 30 s (arXiv 2507.13264).
  • Streaming wrappers pad every buffer to the full context length, so each partial result pays for 30 s (arXiv 2506.12154).
  • Encoder attention is bidirectional, so every frame sees later audio (GLM-4-Voice replaces it with block-causal attention, arXiv 2412.02612). The model card states the models cannot do real-time transcription out of the box.

Four families fix this, with different cost; a fifth, training a streaming encoder with a delay input, is described after the table.

  • Causal fine-tuning. CarelessWhisper fine-tunes Whisper with LoRA so each encoder frame attends only to its own chunk and earlier chunks, and it caches encoder keys and values instead of re-encoding padded buffers (arXiv 2508.12301). It trains on 960 h of LibriSpeech with chunk sizes of 40 to 300 ms. The U2-style variant puts a CTC head on the Whisper encoder, trains with dynamic chunk masks (0.1 to 1.0 s) on inputs shorter than 30 s, and rescores with the original decoder (arXiv 2506.12154). GLM-4-Voice makes the encoder causal inside its tokenizer: causal convolution before the Transformer and block-causal attention, then pooling and vector quantisation give 12.5 Hz tokens (arXiv 2412.02612).
  • Replace the encoder. Qwen3-Omni trains AuT (Audio Transformer) from scratch on 20 million hours of supervised audio, with 8x Conv2D downsampling to 12.5 Hz and block-wise window attention for real-time prefill caching (arXiv 2509.17765). Qwen3-ASR reuses AuT with a dynamic flash-attention window of 1 s to 8 s, so one model serves streaming (short chunks) and offline (long queries) (arXiv 2601.21337).
  • Wrap the offline model. whisper_streaming re-runs Whisper on a growing buffer and commits words once consecutive runs agree (LocalAgreement), at 3.3 s latency (arXiv 2307.14743). Simul-Whisper uses the cross-attention alignment to guide decoding on chunks, with a truncation detector for cut words (arXiv 2406.10052). The U2 authors note that wrappers reprocess the same audio and leave a training-inference mismatch (arXiv 2506.12154).

The fifth family does not retrofit Whisper at all. Voxtral Realtime trains a causal encoder from scratch with the decoder conditioned on a delay sampled from 80 to 2400 ms; at 480 ms its LibriSpeech clean / other WER is 2.08 / 5.54, against 1.84 / 3.66 for offline Whisper (arXiv 2602.11298). Moonshine v2 uses a sliding-window encoder with 80 ms of look-ahead (arXiv 2602.12241).

In the interview, say:

"Whisper streams in three ways: wrap it and pay for re-encoding, fine-tune it causal with a chunked mask and an encoder cache, or replace the encoder. Wrappers need no training but cost latency; causal fine-tuning gets sub-second latency; a new windowed encoder like AuT costs a from-scratch run but serves streaming and offline from one model."

Streaming audio encoders beyond Whisper

Most streaming encoders do one of two things: restrict attention to a causal or windowed context, or cache past activations.

  • Qwen3-ASR numbers: the paper reports 2.69 average WER offline vs 3.33 streaming for the 1.7B model, and 3.48 vs 4.40 for 0.6B, with streaming run at a 2 s chunk size (arXiv 2601.21337). It also reports an average time to first token as low as 92 ms for the 0.6B model.
  • DSM-ASR: feeds Mimi tokens (12.5 Hz) and delays the text stream by a default 2.5 s, with timestamp precision of 80 ms (arXiv 2509.08753).
  • Cache-aware Conformer: with a regular look-ahead, latency compounds with depth (1,360 ms for 17 layers and one token of look-ahead); chunk-aware look-ahead does not. On LibriSpeech test-other with an RNNT decoder, the cache-aware model reaches 6.3% WER at 1,360 ms, against 11.3% for buffered inference at 2,000 ms (arXiv 2312.17279).
  • The model card lists a 120M-parameter cache-aware FastConformer-RNNT with 17 layers and latency of 80 to 160 ms.

Interview insights

Inference, not sourced fact
  • Whisper got the recipe right: scale plus weak supervision gives robustness, and multitask tokens put ASR, translation and language ID in one model.
  • It is wrong for voice products as shipped: fixed window, non-causal encoder and beam decoding hurt latency, and silence hallucination matters for agents.
  • Newer encoders address this: the Open ASR Leaderboard authors find Conformer encoders with Transformer decoders give the best average WER (arXiv 2510.06961), and Qwen3-ASR uses a dynamic 1 to 8 s attention window to cover both streaming and offline (arXiv 2601.21337).
  • AuT is the synthesis: Whisper-scale data with a streaming-friendly encoder.
  • The newest streaming systems (Voxtral Realtime, Moonshine v2) no longer use Whisper as the starting point, so "start from Whisper" is the pragmatic default and not the field's direction.
In the interview, say:

"Whisper's recipe is right, but the architecture is wrong for voice agents: a fixed 30 s window and a bidirectional encoder. That is why GLM-4-Voice makes it causal, Qwen3-Omni replaces it with AuT, and Voxtral Realtime trains a causal encoder from scratch with a delay knob, and why I would start from a streaming encoder."
