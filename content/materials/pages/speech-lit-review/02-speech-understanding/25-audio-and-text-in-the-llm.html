How encoder audio and ASR text meet inside the LLM

A speech LLM must decide where audio positions and text positions sit in one sequence. That choice sets when text can be emitted, whether the model streams, and what alignment the training data needs. This section lays out six layouts from the papers; encoder rates are in Speech input, the Whisper front end is in Whisper, and the full streaming-ASR taxonomy is in Streaming ASR families.

Six layouts

1. Prefix (soft prompt). [audio e1 … eT] <asr prompt> → text tokens Qwen2-Audio models each text token given the previous text and the encoded audio, \( P(x_t \mid x_{<t}, \mathrm{Encoder}(a)) \), with the objective stated over the next text token (Section 2; arXiv 2407.10759). Its encoder is initialised from Whisper-large-v3, and a stride-2 pooling makes each frame about 40 ms (arXiv 2407.10759). The official repo prompt for the pretrained model is <|audio_bos|><|AUDIO|><|audio_eos|>Generate the caption in English:, and its chat example puts the audio item before the text item (QwenLM/Qwen2-Audio). Voxtral passes encoder output through an MLP adapter that downsamples 4x (50 to 12.5 Hz), and its decoder predicts text conditioned on the audio and text inputs (arXiv 2507.13264). Its pretraining uses an audio segment followed by its transcript (<repeat>), or alternating audio and text segments (<next>), and a special token selects transcribe mode with no text prompt (arXiv 2507.13264). Neither paper states whether the loss is masked to text positions, and neither describes the audio positions beyond conditioning. Inference The <|AUDIO|> placeholder suggests encoder frames replace those positions in the input embeddings.

2. Chunk-interleaved streaming. [audio chunk 1] [text 1] $ [audio chunk 2] [text 2] $ … SpeechLLM-XL splits the audio into 1.28 s chunks and trains the LLM to emit each chunk's text plus an end-of-chunk token $ (Sections III and IV-A; arXiv 2410.03752). The target transcript is segmented per chunk, with a $ after each chunk's tokens, and the audio encodings are interleaved with text embeddings by audio-text alignment (Section III, Fig. 1; arXiv 2410.03752). When reference alignments are missing, CTC forced alignment between audio encodings and text tokens gives each token's end time (Section III; arXiv 2410.03752). Self-attention caches the previous \( b \) chunks, and at inference each chunk's text is generated until $ is predicted, then $ is dropped from the transcript (arXiv 2410.03752).

3. Per-frame wait or blank. e1 BLANK e2 w w BLANK e3 BLANK … In Speech ReaLLM an input embedding arrives every 240 ms, the model appends it and keeps predicting tokens until it predicts BLANK (Section 2, Algorithm 1; arXiv 2406.09569). A frame can therefore yield zero, one or several words. Training targets come from fixed alignments produced by an external CTC "alignment-teacher", with cross-entropy loss; the paper found it better to give the encoder 960 ms of right context than to delay the labels (Sections 3 and 4.2; arXiv 2406.09569).

4. Transducer with an LLM predictor. LLM(text history) + encoder frame → joint network → token or blank Transducer-Llama uses the LLM as the non-blank predictor at decoding; encoder output enters through the joint network, not the LLM input (Sections II and III; arXiv 2412.16464). The non-blank distribution combines both scores:

\[ P_{nb} = (1 - P_b)\,\operatorname{softmax}(\log P_{ac} + \log P_{ilm}) \]

Here \( P_b \) is the blank probability, \( P_{ac} \) comes from the acoustic encoder and \( P_{ilm} \) from the internal LM, which the LLM replaces after RNN-T training (Sections II and III-C; arXiv 2412.16464). The LLM is frozen apart from new embedding and output layers for the ASR vocabulary (Section III-A; arXiv 2412.16464). The paper does not state the predictor's input. Inference As in any transducer predictor, it sees the previous non-blank labels, not audio.

5. Time-aligned parallel streams. step t: emb(text token) + emb(audio tokens) → one vector → text out Moshi gives each stream its own embedding table and sums them: the Temporal Transformer takes "the sum of K learnt embedding tables" for the previous step, with \( K = 2Q + 1 = 17 \) streams for \( Q = 8 \) (Section 3.4; arXiv 2410.00037). One stream holds the aligned text, with PAD between words and EPAD before each word, and PAD is about 65% of tokens in English conversation (Section 3.4.4; arXiv 2410.00037). Steps are 80 ms (12.5 Hz). DSM does the same, "the embeddings are summed before going into the backbone" (Section 3; arXiv 2509.08753). For ASR it teacher-forces the audio tokens, computes loss on the text stream including PAD and WORD, and delays the text by a default 2.5 s (Sections 3.2 and 4; arXiv 2509.08753). Three later papers build on this layout. Voxtral Realtime keeps DSM's time-aligned text stream, pairs it with a causal encoder trained from scratch, and injects a delay through Ada RMS-Norm so the delay can be chosen at inference (arXiv 2602.11298). Factorized DSM removes the word-start token and models "wait" as a separate sigmoid, so the vocabulary softmax is skipped on waiting steps (74.70% of Corpus of Spontaneous Japanese steps and 66.89% of LibriSpeech steps); at 480 ms, LibriSpeech WER is 6.17 against 6.52 for DSM (arXiv 2610.04333). A duplex model can place a lightweight ASR head beside the agent text head on the same backbone, with user and agent embeddings time-aligned and added before the decoder, so one decoding pass yields both texts (arXiv 2609.15759). More in Inner Monologue and who emits what per frame.

6. Hybrid continuous plus discrete input. per 12.5 Hz step: whisper_feature(t) + emb(semantic_token(t)) Kimi-Audio downsamples Whisper features from 50 to 12.5 Hz with an adaptor, then adds them to the semantic-token embeddings as the audio LLM input (Sections 2.2 and 4.1; arXiv 2504.18425). Confirmed The paper is inconsistent: its Introduction says it concatenates the two (arXiv 2504.18425). Inference The method sections are more specific, so treat it as addition.

Position encoding for audio

Qwen3-Omni uses TM-RoPE, which splits the rotary embedding into temporal, height and width components (24, 20 and 20 rotary angles) (Section 2.3; arXiv 2509.17765). For text, all three components share one position ID, which makes it equal to 1D RoPE. For audio, each temporal ID spans 80 ms, and the AuT encoder emits 12.5 Hz tokens (Sections 2.2 and 2.3; arXiv 2509.17765). Qwen3.5-Omni retrains AuT from scratch at 6.25 Hz (about 160 ms per frame), so the 80 ms figure is specific to Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2604.15804). Each modality starts at one plus the largest position ID of the previous one, and audio and video align through absolute-time IDs (arXiv 2509.17765).

Inference The Qwen2-Audio and Voxtral papers state no separate audio position scheme, so I assume ordinary sequential positions there. In layout 5 a position is a time step by construction, since all streams sum into one backbone input per step (arXiv 2509.08753).

Where ASR text leaves the speech model

Inference: the hand-off in the target design

Inference In the unified speech LLM design and in the cascade, the speech model's streaming transcript goes to the downstream reasoning LLM as plain text. The text would carry tags such as partial or final, and the reasoning LLM would start only on a stable transcript, as in partials, finals and when to start the LLM. Layouts 2 and 3 give that stream natively; a prefix model gives it only after the utterance ends; layout 5 gives it with a lag set by the delay (fixed in DSM, chosen at inference in Voxtral Realtime).

Supporting facts: SpeechLLM-XL drops $ from the final transcript (arXiv 2410.03752), and Speech ReaLLM's response after each embedding is often empty (arXiv 2406.09569). Streaming ASR trade-offs are in Streaming approaches and Look-ahead.

In the interview, say:

"Audio and text meet in the LLM in a few ways: offline models put projected audio frames first and decode text after, while streaming models interleave chunks with an end-of-chunk token, emit a blank after each frame, run a transducer, or sum time-aligned streams per 80 ms step. The trade-off is alignment: chunk and frame layouts need CTC alignments for training targets, time-aligned streams need word timestamps, and a prefix needs neither but waits for the whole utterance. For a voice agent I want text while the user is still speaking, so I would pick chunk interleaving or time-aligned streams."
