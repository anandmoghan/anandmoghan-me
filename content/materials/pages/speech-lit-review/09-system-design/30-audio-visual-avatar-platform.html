ML system design: an audio-visual avatar platform (Synthesia-style)

Inference

Inference Design choices and targets below are my opinion unless tagged Confirmed or Reported. Latency figures marked as targets are illustrative and have no source. The target voice model is in a unified speech LLM with a downstream reasoning LLM; the cascade baseline is in Baseline: a streaming ASR → LLM → TTS voice agent; the renderer and the two voice paths are in a real-time avatar that talks back. This section adds the platform around them: two products, shared assets, data, training, evaluation, serving and safety.

Requirements

One platform serves two products with different service levels. Batch video turns a script into a 1080p, 30 fps video in minutes, and dubs it into 30+ languages. Video Agents hold two-way conversations; Synthesia describes real dialogue as "typically under one second".

High-level architecture

  • Batch. Express-Voice is in-context cloning without fine-tuning, two transformers of about 800M parameters each conditioned on reference audio. Express-Animate makes motion, Express-Eval scores audio-motion agreement, and Express-Render synthesises frames, distilled to two diffusion steps. Confirmed Using Eval to select among motion candidates is my reading of its role. Inference
  • Dubbing. The chain is ASR, MT, cloned-voice TTS, lip-sync re-render. The TTS needs duration control so the translated line fits the original shot. Inference Synthesia's Dubbing 2.0 post (15 July 2026) says the translation engine "respects the original timing and length" and gives no metrics (post). Confirmed The research alternative is joint generation: JUST-DUB-IT (arXiv 2601.22143) adapts an audio-video diffusion model with a LoRA to write the translated audio and the facial motion together. Reported
  • Interactive. The voice is a unified speech LLM with a downstream text LLM, driving a causal renderer; the cascade is the launch fallback and baseline, and native full-duplex is the research track. Inference Perceiving the user needs a streaming encoder with a fixed-size KV cache (StreamingVLM reuses attention-sink states, a short window of recent vision tokens and a long window of recent text tokens; up to 8 FPS on one H100) (arXiv 2510.09608) or an omni model such as Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765). Confirmed

One timeline for voice and video

The two teams need a shared clock, or sync becomes a post-hoc patch. Ovi scales audio RoPE frequencies by 31/157 so 31 video latent frames align with 157 audio tokens (arXiv 2510.01284). Qwen3-Omni instead assigns absolute-time IDs (one temporal ID per 80 ms of audio) and adjusts video frame IDs to real timestamps, which allows streaming inputs of arbitrary duration (arXiv 2509.17765). Confirmed Inference I would use absolute-time positions across every model, so a TTS chunk, a motion chunk and a rendered block all carry the same timestamps. The audio stays the clock, as in the real-time avatar section. A speech LLM on an 80 ms frame clock gives the voice side of that timeline for free.

A stronger form is a shared token stream. Meta's research avatar has the voice model emit speech tokens that carry content and delivery; the same stream feeds the audio decoder and the avatar model, which keeps voice, lips and expression synchronised (Meta, 23 Sep 2026). Reported Inference That bears on a design with a text LLM behind a tool call: the renderer would read the speak head's semantic and prosodic tokens and state, not only a waveform, while the text LLM stays swappable. Wan-Streamer takes the opposite end: one Transformer over text, speech and video with 160 ms units (arXiv 2606.25041).

Data

  • Capture studio against webcam or photo. Synthesia supports personal avatars from a single photo and an optional voice sample (personal avatars docs). Confirmed Inference Studio capture gives clean, controlled full-body data for the premium tier and for training. Webcam and photo give scale and diversity but noisy lighting and audio; tier them by capture quality.
  • Curation. Ovi filters clips with SyncNet (absolute offset at most 3, confidence above 1.5, mean volume at least -60 dB) and uses MLLM captions (arXiv 2510.01284). Confirmed
  • Multilingual speech. Inference Dubbing needs paired speech across 30+ languages with accent labels and speaker identity; emotion2vec tags give emotion coverage. Long-tail languages need synthetic or licensed data.
  • Consent provenance. Inference Every training clip carries a consent record and licence scope. A person who withdraws consent must be removable from the training set and from future retrains.

Training stages

  1. Pretrain backbones. Start from an open video DiT (Wan is an open suite of video foundation models, arXiv 2503.20314; Ovi initialises its video tower from Wan2.2 5B, arXiv 2510.01284) and a speech AR model with a codec. Confirmed
  2. Audio-conditioning finetune. Add audio conditioning for lip and motion; FantasyTalking adds a second, frame-level lip-refinement stage (arXiv 2504.04842). Confirmed
  3. Distillation. Reduce to few steps for batch; build a causal student for interactive, with autoregressive rollouts in training to close the train-test gap (Self Forcing, arXiv 2506.08009). Confirmed
  4. Preference optimisation. FantasyTalking2 trains a reward model (Talking-Critic), curates a 410K-pair preference dataset and optimises with TLPO (arXiv 2508.11255). Confirmed For voice, use the RL recipes from the control-and-quality chapter: WER and SIM rewards plus an emotion term, and add per-axis timing rewards for the speech LLM's action channel.
Trap: reward hacking

Automatic rewards get gamed; GRPO on CER and NLL rewards collapsed prosody into monotone speech (arXiv 2509.18531). Confirmed Inference Guard with multi-metric rewards, a naturalness term, held-out human panels, and a check on motion diversity (a lip-sync reward alone can freeze the head).

Serving and cost

  • Pools. Inference Separate GPU pools for the speech LLM, text LLM, motion and render; cascade pools only as failover capacity. Interactive gets reserved capacity with a hard concurrency cap; batch fills the gaps from a queue and can be pre-empted or delayed.
  • Caching. Inference Cache identity features and voice conditioning per avatar, so repeat renders skip the encoders.
  • Steps against quality. Express-Render uses two diffusion steps, and Render-Turbo is described as coming later (Express-2 post). Confirmed Inference Treat step count as a per-tier knob: fewer steps for previews and interactive, more for final export.
  • Evaluations per chunk and sessions per GPU. Meta reports a teacher needing 120 model evaluations per video chunk against 2 for the distilled student (60x), and 12 concurrent real-time sessions on one GB200 after four-bit quantisation-aware training gave 8x capacity over the two-step BF16 baseline (Meta). Reported Inference Cost per session-minute is GPU cost divided by concurrent sessions; track it per tier next to the latency percentiles.
  • Autoscaling. Inference Scale batch on queue depth and interactive on session count, with warm pools because model load is slow.

Safety

  • Consent gate. Synthesia requires a live consent video (uploads not accepted) and the person must match the photo (personal avatars docs). Confirmed Inference Bind the voice store to the same verified identity, so a cloned voice cannot be used without it.
  • Moderation at creation. Synthesia says it moderates content at the point of creation and lists a Content Integrity Policy (AI governance page). Confirmed Inference Screen the script before generation and the output before delivery.
  • Labelling and provenance. The governance page states EU AI Act Article 50 labelling and lists Content Authenticity Initiative engagement. Confirmed The page does not mention C2PA or watermarking. For comparison, Meta's research avatar embeds an invisible Video Seal watermark in generated video with no added latency, and Google's Gemini 3.8 Audio card does not mention watermarking for Live Avatar.
  • Voice-clone abuse. Synthesia states that it strictly prohibits cloning voices without explicit consent (voice cloning page). Confirmed Inference Add speaker verification of the reference against the consenting person, and rate limits on cloning and on interactive agents.

Risks and what I would do first

  • Risks. Inference Audio and video teams drift on clocks and metrics; distilled causal renderers degrade under audio conditioning; action-channel data with real overlap is scarce; tags the text LLM cannot use; reward models get gamed.
  • Days 1-30. Inference Learn the current Voice stack and its failures. Build the shared benchmark and metric pins with the video team, including latency percentiles, and put the cascade on it as the baseline.
  • Days 31-60. Inference Stand up the unified speech LLM path: listen head with tags and end-of-turn, tool call to the text LLM, speak head with fillers, absolute-time chunks and listening cues, scored on the benchmark. Ship the cascade as launch fallback behind the same gates.
  • Days 61-90. Inference Train the first in-house version on the shared timeline, add timing and voice rewards with hacking guards, gate it against the cascade, and start the native full-duplex research track.
In the interview, say:

"I see two products with two SLOs on one set of consent-gated identity and voice assets. Interactive voice is a unified speech LLM that owns listening, turn-taking and speaking, with a swappable text LLM behind a tool call; the cascade is the launch fallback and baseline. I would share an absolute-time clock between voice and video and build one internal benchmark first, because published sync numbers do not compare."
