ML system design: a unified speech LLM with a downstream reasoning LLM (the target)

Inference

Inference The overall design is my opinion; the component facts are tagged and cite arXiv papers or Synthesia's own pages. Latency and size figures marked as targets are illustrative targets, not measurements, and have no source. The idea: one speech model owns everything that touches audio (listening, turn-taking, speaking), and a separate text LLM owns content, reached through a tool-call style interface. Native full-duplex (Moshi style) is the evolution of this design, and a cascade of independent ASR, LLM and TTS models is the baseline.

Inference The honest costs of the target: an extra hop of LLM time to first token; paralinguistic information must be flattened into the call, so what the tags omit the LLM never sees; and two models to serve, version and evaluate together.

Precedents for each piece. Kyutai DSM applies one architecture to both jobs: ASR delays the text behind the audio (2.5 s default), and TTS delays the audio behind the text (1.28 s), with the delay set per stream Confirmed (arXiv 2509.08753). The DSM authors report a 150 ms first-chunk latency for TTS at batch size 1 Reported. Step-Audio 2 calls external tools such as web search against hallucination and audio search to switch timbres Confirmed (arXiv 2507.16632). Qwen3-Omni uses a Thinker-Talker design, the in-model analogue of this split (arXiv 2509.17765). Freeze-Omni keeps the LLM frozen and adds a state classifier (keep listening, interrupted, turn ended) (arXiv 2411.00774). MinMo adds an 18M-parameter Full Duplex Predictor beside the LLM (arXiv 2501.06282). Both show a small decision module beside a text LLM works. Moshi's Inner Monologue shows text and audio can share one clock (arXiv 2410.00037). Confirmed

Architecture

  • Listen head. A causal streaming encoder at 12.5 Hz feeds a text-LLM-initialised backbone. The head emits streaming text (partials and stable finals), paralinguistic tags (emotion, rate, language, confidence) and an action token per frame. The user stream is ingested continuously, including while the agent speaks.
  • Tool call. On an end-of-turn decision the head emits a structured call: the stable transcript, the tags, the turn state (end-of-turn confidence, whether it interrupted a reply) and a pointer to what was actually played last. Start on the end-of-turn candidate and cancel on a resumed turn, as in the cascade baseline.
  • Downstream LLM. Any text LLM with tools and retrieval. It streams text back with style cues (emotion, pace) the speak head can use. It never touches audio, so it can be swapped or upgraded without retraining the speech model.
  • Speak head. It turns text chunks plus style and the voice prompt into codec tokens at 12.5 Hz (semantic then acoustic codebooks through a depth transformer or multi-token head), decoded by a causal codec decoder. A small first chunk cuts time to first audio; later chunks carry more lookahead for prosody.
  • Latency cover. While the call runs, the speak head says a short acknowledgement or filler, and the listen head can backchannel during long user turns. Neither waits for the LLM.
  • One clock. Every output chunk carries an absolute timestamp (Qwen3-Omni assigns one temporal ID per 80 ms, arXiv 2509.17765). Confirmed The renderer and client play audio on that clock and shed video frames first.

Conversational behaviours: who decides what

Inference The speech LLM owns timing and the floor; the text LLM owns content. The action channel is one token per frame or chunk: listen, end-of-turn, backchannel, speak, yield. DuplexSLA is the precedent for such a channel (a textual action channel on a shared 160 ms chunk clock; 98.33% backchannel accuracy, self-reported; arXiv 2605.20755), and Freeze-Omni and MinMo show the same decision made by a small module beside a text LLM. Data

  • Listen head. Large unlabelled audio with pseudo-transcripts (Moshi uses 7M h, arXiv 2410.00037; Kimi-Audio over 13M h, arXiv 2504.18425), plus ASR data to align the encoder. Confirmed Paralinguistic tags come from pseudo-labellers (Synthesia names emotion2vec in its Express-Voice evaluation), checked on a human-labelled subset. Inference
  • Speak head. TTS-grade, speaker-diverse, consented speech with style labels; interleaved synthetic speech-text (1T tokens, 600B synthetic interleaved; spoken QA rose from 13% to 31% in arXiv 2411.17607). Confirmed
  • Action channel. Dual-channel conversation with real overlap: Fisher, about 2,000 h of phone conversations (arXiv 2410.00037), MinMo's 4,000 h of duplex data (arXiv 2501.06282), and Seamless Interaction conversations (used by Kyutai for RL, arXiv 2606.11167). Confirmed Synthetic dialogue has few real overlaps and regular backchannels, so mix real and synthetic.
  • Tool-call traces. An LLM writes dialogues with interruptions; a harness renders both sides with many TTS voices and logs the call, the reply and the truncation. Inference The baseline cascade is the generator and the first labeller.
  • Consent. Every voice clip carries a consent record and licence scope; withdrawn voices are removable from retrains. Inference

Training stages and rewards

  1. Align the encoder and adaptor on ASR with the LLM backbone frozen.
  2. Pretrain listen and speak on unlabelled and interleaved synthetic data, with a high text fraction.
  3. Train the listen head on streaming text, tags and the action channel; the speak head on text-to-speech with style.
  4. Spoken SFT on tool-call traces, including fillers, truncations and re-plans.
  5. DPO on text-token probabilities with timing pairs (the Moshi alignment work used 93,490 pairs and alignment over text tokens, arXiv 2506.21463).
  6. RL on windows around events with per-axis timing rewards (turn onset, backchannel, yield) and voice rewards (WER, speaker similarity, quality), plus a text-quality term against drift.

Inference The staging follows the proposed recipe. Rewards come from online RL and rewards: DuplexPO decouples when to speak from what to say (arXiv 2607.07148), and Kyutai's RL on Moshi and PersonaPlex adds a response-quality reward against semantic degradation (arXiv 2606.11167). Reported The forgetting guard: speech input erodes LLM knowledge Confirmed (arXiv 2509.14930), so the downstream text LLM stays untouched by design, and the speech backbone is gated at every stage on ASR WER, strict text QA and timing.

Evaluation

  • Latency and interaction. Time to first audio from gold end of turn at a fixed false-yield rate, floor-transfer offset against the human baseline, stop latency, false yields, premature takeovers, backchannel rate. Suites: Full-Duplex-Bench v1, v1.5 and v2 (arXiv 2503.04721, 2507.23159, 2510.07838) and TurnBench (arXiv 2608.25218); see conversational evaluation.
  • Per component. Listen head: WER on domain audio, tag accuracy, end-of-turn recall at fixed false-positive rate. Speak head: second-ASR WER, speaker similarity, UTMOS, native-speaker panels. Tool call: does the LLM answer correctly with and without tags (an ablation of the interface). Inference
  • Intelligence in speech mode. Spoken-dialogue suites (VoiceBench, arXiv 2410.17196; URO-Bench, arXiv 2502.17810) against the same LLM on text. DuplexPO's authors note that duplex gains often come with weaker reasoning (arXiv 2607.07148); here the LLM is unchanged, so I expect a small gap. Inference
  • Safety. Moderate the transcript and the reply text; check voice-clone abuse and consent binding; label output (Synthesia states EU AI Act Article 50 labelling on its governance page). Inference

Serving

  • Per-frame deadline. The speech LLM must finish each 80 ms frame, silent or not, and the worst frame matters, not the mean. Size it as a real-time loop; the LLM is a normal streaming text service. Inference
  • Memory and sessions. A Moshi-sized backbone (temporal transformer of 32 layers and 4096 dimensions, arXiv 2410.00037) needs about 512 KiB of KV per frame, about 6.3 MiB per second and 23 GB per hour (derived, assuming full attention and bf16). Cache, not weights, sets session capacity; use grouped-query attention, a rolling window and session caps. Batch sessions to the largest \( B \) with \( t_{\text{step}}(B) < 80 \) ms. Inference
  • The hop. Keep the LLM in the same region, use prefix caching for the system prompt, route simple turns to a small model, and let fillers cover the first-token wait. Inference
  • Echo. Browser AEC plus the playback reference; test stop latency after cancellation.
  • Fallback. If the speech model misses deadlines or is down, route new sessions to the cascade, and hand over mid-session at a turn boundary using the transcript. Inference

Evolution to full duplex, the cascade, and risks

Inference The research track is a native full-duplex model (Moshi and PersonaPlex style: user audio, agent text, agent audio and an action token on one 80 ms clock) that replaces the two heads. The tool-call contract survives, so the downstream LLM still supplies content while the duplex model keeps the floor. The DuplexPO authors describe a tension between interactive dynamics and reasoning ability in full-duplex models (arXiv 2607.07148), which is why I keep reasoning outside it for now. The cascade of independent models stays as the baseline everything is measured against, the launch fallback, and the first generator of synthetic dialogue. It is not the target.

Commercial end-to-end models are moving the same way. Google DeepMind's Gemini 3.8 Audio model card (15 September 2026) covers the Live and Live Extended Thinking models, and describes an optional Live Avatar variant whose output is "Audio, video, and text, with 24K token output" and which supports "a few minutes of continuous interaction, rather than extended hours" (model card). Confirmed The card gives no latency and no avatar-specific evaluation. A model that emits video from the same stack as speech is the strongest form of the shared-stream idea in the avatar platform section. Inference

  • Risks. Inference Tags the LLM cannot use; the hop erasing the latency gain; reward hacking (monotone prosody, an agent that never yields); scarce dual-channel data with real overlap; two models drifting apart across versions.
In the interview, say:

"I would build one speech LLM that owns everything audio: streaming ASR with emotion tags, turn-taking, backchannels, barge-in, and expressive TTS on one 12.5 Hz clock. Reasoning stays in a swappable text LLM behind a tool call, so we keep tool use, grounding and text-level safety, and fillers cover the extra hop. A cascade of independent models is the baseline and fallback, and native full-duplex is where the speech model goes next."
