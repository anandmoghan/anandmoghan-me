Continuous autoregression and next-token diffusion

A smaller, newer branch drops discrete tokens. An AR model predicts continuous latents, and a small diffusion or flow head samples each next frame or patch.

Model Design Notes
MELLE (arXiv 2407.08551) Regresses continuous mel frames; spectrogram flux loss; latent sampling replaces top-p The authors report a 47.9% relative WER reduction over VALL-E on continuation
DiTAR (ICML 2025; arXiv 2502.03930) LM over aggregated patch embeddings, bidirectional-attention diffusion transformer (LocDiT) generates each patch Temperature maps to the noise-injection point in the reverse ODE
VibeVoice (arXiv 2508.19205) LLM hidden states condition a light diffusion head predicting continuous VAE features; 7.5 Hz The authors report 80x better compression than EnCodec at comparable performance; up to 90 min of speech in a 64K context
dots.tts (arXiv 2606.07080) 2B continuous autoregressive TTS in a learned AudioVAE latent space; flow-matching head conditioned on full history The authors report first-packet latencies of 85 / 54 ms (output streaming / dual-streaming) after MeanFlow distillation
VoxCPM (arXiv 2509.24650) Tokenizer-free: a text-semantic LM and a residual acoustic LM guide a local diffusion decoder, trained end to end with a diffusion objective The authors report 1.8M h of bilingual training data for VoxCPM-0.5B
Continuous Audio Language Models, CALM (arXiv 2509.06926) Large Transformer backbone; its embedding conditions a small MLP head that generates the next VAE frame by consistency modelling Releases Pocket TTS, 100M parameters, faster than real time on a laptop CPU
CuteTTS (arXiv 2608.08638) Patch-level AR over causal VAE latents; explicit speaker conditioning; bidirectional flow-matching head Guidance-step distillation folds CFG and multiple solver steps into one student: first-audio latency down 23.3%, RTF down 40.8% vs the base model
VoxCPM2 (arXiv 2606.06928) 2B hierarchical diffusion-AR model on continuous latents, no external discrete tokenizer; 30 languages, trained on over 2M h AudioVAE encodes at 16 kHz and reconstructs at 48 kHz; average WER 1.68% on the authors' internal 30-language set

A consistency head and a distilled flow head are the same idea: a small network that produces the next frame in one step or very few. CALM trains it as a consistency model; dots.tts and CuteTTS distil a flow head.

Inference The two lists below are design analysis, not results from the papers.

The case for

  • No quantization loss, so higher ceiling on naturalness.
  • Very low frame rates shorten the AR sequence (VibeVoice runs at 7.5 Hz).

The case against

  • Per-frame diffusion or flow cost on top of the LM step.
  • RL is harder without discrete tokens. GROW notes that trajectory-level policy-gradient RL for flow-matching TTS must track per-step likelihood ratios, which adds overhead (arXiv 2608.03215). CosyVoice 3's DiffRO optimises the speech tokens directly with a differentiable reward, and targets discrete-token models (arXiv 2505.17589).
  • Sampling control is weaker than top-p over a categorical.
  • Inference Qwen3-Omni uses a codec plus a causal ConvNet decoder (arXiv 2509.17765), which suggests continuous heads may stay niche for duplex products.
Say this in the interview:

"Continuous AR removes the quantization bottleneck, but you pay in per-step sampling cost and lose easy RL. For a duplex product I would start discrete and revisit."
