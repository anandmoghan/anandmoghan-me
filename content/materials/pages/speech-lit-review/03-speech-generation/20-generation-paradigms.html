Generation paradigms

There are five ways to generate speech from text or from an LM state. The dominant high-quality design is the hybrid; block diffusion LMs are the newest entry.

Paradigm Examples Core idea
AR codec LM Llasa (arXiv 2502.04128), Spark-TTS (arXiv 2503.01710), CSM, Moshi (arXiv 2410.00037) One Transformer predicts tokens left to right
NAR masked SoundStorm (arXiv 2305.09636), MaskGCT (arXiv 2409.00750) Parallel iterative filling of tokens
Block diffusion LM Luna-TTS Realtime (arXiv 2608.11593), DLLM-TTS (arXiv 2608.00011), Chatterbox-Flash (arXiv 2605.30748) AR across blocks of tokens, parallel masked denoising inside each block
NAR flow/diffusion Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Seed-TTS DiT (arXiv 2406.02430) Generate the whole utterance by ODE or diffusion
Hybrid AR + flow CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Qwen3-TTS 25 Hz (arXiv 2601.15621), FlashTTS (arXiv 2606.09141) LM for content and prosody, flow for acoustics

AR codec LMs

  • Llasa: a single Llama-style Transformer over X-Codec2 tokens, 1B/3B/8B, with inference-time search using speech-understanding verifiers (arXiv 2502.04128).
  • Spark-TTS: Qwen2.5 LLM plus BiCodec (arXiv 2503.01710).
  • CosyVoice 2: an LLM predicts speech tokens, then a chunk-aware causal flow-matching model decodes them (arXiv 2412.10117).
  • Newer entries: IndexTTS2 (arXiv 2506.21619), Step-Audio-EditX (LLM-based audio editing with reinforcement learning; arXiv 2511.03601), Higgs Audio v2 (pretrained on over 10 million hours of audio, per the Boson AI repo).

Multi-codebook handling

An RVQ frame has several codes, so the model needs a trick.

  • Sesame CSM: a Llama backbone models the zeroth Mimi codebook from interleaved text and audio, and a smaller audio decoder with a linear head per codebook models the rest; Mimi runs at 12.5 Hz (Sesame blog).
  • Moshi: \( K = 2Q + 1 = 17 \) streams per step with \( Q = 8 \) codebooks (8 user audio, 8 own audio, 1 text). Its RQ-Transformer splits temporal and per-codebook (depth) modelling (arXiv 2410.00037).
  • Qwen3-Omni's Talker uses an MTP module to emit codec frames (arXiv 2509.17765).

NAR notes

SoundStorm uses bidirectional attention and confidence-based parallel decoding to generate codec tokens, and the authors report 30 s of audio in 0.5 s on a TPU-v4 (arXiv 2305.09636). MaskGCT is two-stage (text to semantic tokens, then semantic to acoustic tokens), mask-and-predict in both, with no explicit alignment or phone-level duration model, trained on 100K h (arXiv 2409.00750).

Trap:

MaskGCT is a masked generative model (mask-and-predict). Do not cite it as a flow model. The same applies to the diffusion-LM systems above: they unmask discrete tokens and have no ODE.

Diffusion language models and block diffusion

A diffusion LM is a masked-token model in the MaskGCT family, built on a pretrained text LLM. Block diffusion splits the sequence into blocks, runs masked denoising inside each block and generates the blocks left to right. That keeps streaming and a KV cache while decoding many tokens per step.

  • Luna-TTS: a 0.6B family trained on 1M h of Chinese, English, Japanese and Korean speech, adapted from a pretrained AR text LLM by moving from causal to bidirectional to block-causal attention. Luna-TTS is fully non-autoregressive and generates the whole RVQ token grid in a fixed number of parallel refinement steps; zero-shot cloning and editing come from infilling. Luna-TTS Realtime is AR over blocks of 32 codec frames (1.28 s) with KV-cached, parallel denoising inside each block. It reports RTF 0.0240 and 41.6 ms local first-block latency, and 1.49 WER / 76.8 SIM on Seed-TTS-Eval test-en. A GRPO stage computes policy ratios over the realised denoising trajectory (arXiv 2608.11593).
  • DLLM-TTS: conditional block discrete diffusion over X-Codec2 tokens, masked diffusion within each block and blocks in sequence. A 0.6B model trained on 20K h reaches RTF 0.15 and is competitive on Seed-TTS-eval (arXiv 2608.00011).
  • Chatterbox-Flash: fine-tunes an AR TTS decoder into a block-diffusion decoder. Standard block-diffusion decoding hurts discrete speech tokens because a long-tail token distribution pushes parallel selection toward a few frequent tokens. The fix is inference-only: subtract the block-level marginal token distribution from the scores (prior-calibrated scoring) and stop the iterations early based on calibrated confidence (arXiv 2605.30748).
  • OmniVoice: a single-stage, non-autoregressive diffusion-LM model that maps text straight to multi-codebook acoustic tokens, with full-codebook random masking during training and initialisation from a pretrained LLM. It covers 600+ languages and trains on 581k h of open-source data (arXiv 2604.00688).
  • DeMaR: asks whether the gain of mask-and-replace training (randomly swapping some tokens, not only masking them) comes from inference-time self-correction. Trained from scratch on LibriTTS, it keeps lower WER than mask-only diffusion and AR baselines even when each token stays fixed once unmasked, so replacement helps through noisier training context (arXiv 2610.09448).
Inference:

Block diffusion sits between the AR codec LM and the fully masked NAR model: it trades some of the AR model's token-by-token control for parallel decoding, while the block-causal order keeps streaming possible.

The "LM for content, flow for acoustics" split

  • Inference Why the LM: text streaming, interleaving with text, reuse of LLM serving stacks, in-context learning.
  • Inference Why flow: it can restore acoustic detail lost to quantization, and CosyVoice 2 shows it can decode chunk by chunk (arXiv 2412.10117).
Inference:

The hybrid may be transitional, since Qwen3-Omni replaces block-wise diffusion with a lightweight causal ConvNet over a multi-codebook codec (arXiv 2509.17765).

Say this in the interview:

"The LM decides what is said and how, the renderer decides how it sounds. That split is why I can stream the LM and distil the renderer separately."
