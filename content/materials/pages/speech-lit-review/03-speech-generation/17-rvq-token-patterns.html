Training an LM on RVQ tokens: flattening, parallel, delay, depth

Once the codec exists, the LM must model \( K \) codebooks per frame. MusicGen defines a codebook pattern as a partition of all (time, codebook) pairs into steps, and the model predicts every position in a step in parallel (arXiv 2306.05284). Its two reference patterns, with frame \( t \) and codebook \( k \), are below.

\[ \text{parallel: } P_s = \{(s, k)\}, \qquad \text{delay: } P_s = \{(s - k + 1,\, k) : s - k \ge 0\} \]

Source: MusicGen, Eqs. 5 and 6 (arXiv 2306.05284). In the delay pattern, codebook \( k \) is shifted by \( k - 1 \) steps. Step \( s \) therefore predicts codebook 1 of frame \( s \), codebook 2 of frame \( s - 1 \), and so on. Each token sees the lower codebooks of its own frame as context, and the sequence stays near \( T \) steps long. Inference From Eq. 6, the delay pattern needs \( T + K - 1 \) steps for \( T \) frames. MusicGen's 30 s ablation reports 1500 steps for delay and parallel. Positions absent from a step get a special token, and the first input is the sum of special tokens (arXiv 2306.05284).

Inference The latency column is my reading of the step counts, except the NAR and SoundStorm figures, which the papers state. FAD values are MusicGen's 30 s ablation (lower is better).

  • MusicGen reports that flattening improves generation but costs far more, and that delay reaches similar performance at a fraction of the cost. Its ablation table lists 1500 steps for delay, 6000 for flattening, and FAD of 0.96 and 0.86 respectively (arXiv 2306.05284).
  • VALL-E uses an autoregressive model for the first of 8 quantizers and a non-autoregressive model for the other seven, called once per quantizer. The authors note that early quantizers recover acoustic properties such as speaker identity and later ones learn fine acoustic detail (arXiv 2301.02111).
  • Moshi's RQ-Transformer runs a Temporal Transformer over \( S \) steps and a smaller Depth Transformer over at most \( K \) sub-tokens per step, instead of one model over \( K S \) steps. With 8 codebooks at 12.5 Hz, a flat sequence would be 100 steps per second of audio (arXiv 2410.00037).
  • Moshi also delays the acoustic tokens against the semantic token by \( \tau \) steps: \( V_{s,1} = A_{s,1} \) and \( V_{s,q} = A_{s-\tau,q} \) for \( q > 1 \). The authors found a delay of 1 or 2 steps greatly improves quality, since it lets the larger Temporal Transformer model the semantic-acoustic dependence (arXiv 2410.00037). For how Moshi's streams are timed, see Timing logic.
  • Qwen3-Omni's Talker predicts one codec frame per step: a linear head for the zeroth codebook, then an MTP module for all residual codebooks (arXiv 2509.17765).
  • Fish Audio S2 uses the same split under another name. Its model card describes a Dual-AR design: a 4B slow AR along the time axis predicts the primary semantic codebook, and a 400M fast AR generates the remaining 9 residual codebooks at each time step (Fish Audio S2 Pro model card; technical report arXiv 2603.08823).
  • OmniVoice skips the AR walk down the codebooks. A non-autoregressive diffusion-LM model maps text straight to multi-codebook acoustic tokens in one stage, trained with a full-codebook random masking strategy (arXiv 2604.00688). It belongs to the block and masked-diffusion family in Generation paradigms.
  • SoundStorm sums the embeddings of all tokens in a frame, so the sequence length equals the number of frames, whatever the number of quantizers. It uses a bidirectional Conformer with one head per RVQ level, and fills in levels coarse to fine (arXiv 2305.09636).
In the interview, say:

"With K codebooks per frame, flattening is best but K times longer; MusicGen's delay pattern gets close at T plus K minus 1 steps. For streaming speech I would model codebook 0 per frame and let a small depth or MTP head, or a delay pattern, handle the residuals."
