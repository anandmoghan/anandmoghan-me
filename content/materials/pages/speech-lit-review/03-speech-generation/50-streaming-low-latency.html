Streaming and low-latency generation

Streaming TTS needs a causal, low-frame-rate codec on the AR side, and a chunkable, few-step decoder on the acoustic side.

Latency levers

Lever How it works
Causal, low-rate codec Fewer AR steps per second of audio, no future context needed
Chunk-aware causal flow CosyVoice 2 samples one of four attention masks (non-causal, full-causal, chunk-M, chunk-2M) uniformly in training, so one model serves streaming and offline (arXiv 2412.10117)
Look-ahead Kimi-Audio uses chunk-wise causal masking with a look-ahead mechanism to fix chunk-boundary issues (arXiv 2504.18425); CosyVoice 2 also uses a look-ahead pre-convolution (arXiv 2412.10117)
Few-step distillation ZipVoice flow distillation reduces sampling steps and removes the CFG overhead (arXiv 2506.13053); MeanFlow learns average velocity over an interval (arXiv 2505.13447); Int-MeanFlow distils for speech (arXiv 2510.07979); FlashTTS uses an X-pred mean-flow decoder at 2 NFE (arXiv 2606.09141); DMOSpeech distils a 128-step teacher to 4 steps with distribution matching (arXiv 2410.11097); flow maps reach 1.80% WER at 1 NFE against 1.76% for a 32-NFE teacher (arXiv 2609.36324); see the table in Few-step distillation beyond MeanFlow
Mel-space prediction for chunked decoding X-Pred MeanFlow predicts a generalized mel field and derives the average velocity analytically, with layer-selective block-wise attention for chunked generation with bounded context; it improves on Direct-u MeanFlow at few steps (arXiv 2609.12728; the abstract gives no numbers)
Guidance folding CuteTTS guidance-step distillation lowers first-audio latency 23.3% and RTF 40.8% (arXiv 2608.08638); unified guidance removes CFG's extra pass for nearly 3x faster inference (arXiv 2607.00363)
Multi-token prediction VITA-Audio emits several audio tokens per forward pass (arXiv 2505.03739); FlashTTS adds parallel MTP modules
Sampling schedule F5-TTS's Sway Sampling is an inference-time flow-step strategy that needs no retraining (arXiv 2410.06885)

MeanFlow's headline FID 3.43 is an ImageNet one-step result (arXiv 2505.13447). IntMeanFlow reports 1-NFE token-to-spectrogram and 3-NFE text-to-spectrogram inference for speech (arXiv 2510.07979).

Inference:

For flow decoders, step distillation is the biggest latency lever. For AR models, lower token rate plus a causal codec is.

Say this in the interview:

"I budget first-packet latency per stage. For the renderer the lever is NFE, so I would distil to 2 steps. For the LM it is token rate and multi-token prediction."
