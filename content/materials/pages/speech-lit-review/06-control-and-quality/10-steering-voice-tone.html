Steering voice, tone and emotion

Five control channels, usually combined: a reference clip, a natural-language instruction, inline tags, duration targets, and style guidance at sampling time.

Disentanglement

IndexTTS2 separates emotion from timbre: timbre comes from one prompt and emotional tone from a style prompt, so the two can come from different speakers (arXiv 2506.21619). Its text-based emotion control comes from fine-tuning Qwen3. The authors report better word error rate, speaker similarity and emotional fidelity than other zero-shot TTS models. Step-Audio-EditX takes a different route: large-margin synthetic data instead of representation-level disentanglement (arXiv 2511.03601).

In CosyVoice's own tests, the instruct model raised the share of generated speech that an emotion2vec classifier labelled sad, angry, surprised and disgusted, compared with the base model (arXiv 2407.05407, Table 10).

Style planning and non-verbal control

Speech-Rewarded Style Planning (arXiv 2610.11461) trains a text style planner through a frozen downstream TTS. The planner reads dialogue history and the response text, writes candidate style instructions, and is optimised with GRPO. The reward is the teacher-forced likelihood of the target speech tokens. The authors find that speech-text alignment only weakly predicts downstream acoustic similarity among candidate instructions for the same utterance, so a caption faithful to the audio can still fail to steer a given synthesiser. On an English subset of the ISCSLP 2026 CoT-TTS corpus it improves style and emotion similarity and lowers mel-cepstral distortion against a base LLM and target-audio-informed captioning baselines Reported.

NVAlign (arXiv 2609.31892) targets non-verbal vocalisation tags in continuous autoregressive flow-matching TTS. It fine-tunes a TTS model and an NVV-aware recogniser on NVV-annotated speech, then freezes the recogniser as the reward. A gradient surrogate passes the reward back through the flow-matching sampler to update the autoregressive backbone and the flow head, with fidelity penalties and reference-velocity regularisation to protect speaker similarity and quality. On NVV-SuperBench and in listening tests it improves tag-following over SFT and Flow-GRPO baselines; the abstract gives no numbers Reported. This is the post-training answer to the tag-reliability pitfall noted in prompting pitfalls.

Duration control and lip-sync

Evaluation

emotion2vec (arXiv 2312.15185) is a self-supervised speech-emotion representation, usable as the basis for emotion similarity. Synthesia reports measuring Express-Voice with WavLM for speaker similarity and emotion2vec for emotional similarity.

Synthesia context

Synthesia's Express-2 (4 Sep 2025) pairs Express-Voice with Express-Video. The post reports a blind test with 100 native English-speaking evaluators across 17 accents, in which Express-Voice rated highest for matching the original speaker's identity, rhythm and accent (Reported company claim). The post gives no scores.

Gap

In-context cloning internals, speaker embeddings and timbre/prosody/content disentanglement were not researched beyond IndexTTS2 and Express-Voice.

In the interview, say:

"I would combine a reference clip for identity, an NL or tag channel for emotion, and a duration mode for sync, with emotion kept disentangled from timbre so style transfers across speakers."
