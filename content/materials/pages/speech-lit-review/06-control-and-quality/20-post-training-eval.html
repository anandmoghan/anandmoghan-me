Post-training and evaluation for speech generation

Post-training uses ASR and speaker-verification rewards. The recurring failure is reward hacking.

Methods

Method Idea
Seed-TTS RL (arXiv 2406.02430) RL for robustness, speaker similarity, controllability
DiffRO (CosyVoice 3; arXiv 2505.17589, 2507.05911) Computes rewards on neural codec tokens rather than synthesized audio; Gumbel-Softmax makes the reward differentiable; a multi-task reward model adds emotion and quality control
F5R-TTS (arXiv 2504.02407) Converts flow outputs to Gaussians so GRPO applies; WER and SIM rewards; 29.5% relative WER reduction, 4.6% relative SIM gain (authors)
Flow-GRPO (arXiv 2505.05470) ODE-to-SDE conversion that keeps the model's marginals, enabling the sampling that online RL needs for flow-matching models (text-to-image)
GROW (arXiv 2608.03215) Group-relative advantage-weighted on-policy RL that acts on the standard flow-matching objective, avoiding the ODE-to-SDE conversion and per-step likelihood ratios; on DiTAR the authors report WER 2.016 to 1.558 and SIM 0.676 to 0.715
SwanVoice (arXiv 2605.30993) DiffusionNFT post-training with phone-level and speaker-similarity rewards
FlowTTS-GRPO (arXiv 2606.23190) Online RL for flow-matching TTS: ODE-to-SDE conversion, weighted multi-objective reward; tested on CosyVoice 3.0 and F5-TTS
Subjective-reward GRPO (arXiv 2608.31035) GRPO on codec language models with learned rewards for speaking style, naturalness, likability and arousal and a CER zone constraint. Each reward mainly lifts its own metric; human transfer is uneven; best-of-8 reranking performs about as well as GRPO perceptually
NVAlign (arXiv 2609.31892) Direct-gradient post-training of non-verbal tag-following, with a frozen NVV-aware recogniser as the reward, passed through the flow sampler; beats SFT and Flow-GRPO on tag-following
Step-Audio-EditX (arXiv 2511.03601) Large-margin synthetic data for iterative emotion and style editing, with no embedding priors or auxiliary modules
Preference optimisation EASPO: stepwise preference optimisation for diffusion TTS, scoring noisy intermediate states (arXiv 2509.25416)

Reward hacking

Trap: automatic rewards get gamed
  • RRPO reports that DiffRO-style reward models can be exploited with acoustic artifacts that earn spurious reward but degrade perceptual quality (arXiv 2512.04552).
  • A prosody study found GRPO on CER and NLL rewards lowered error rates but collapsed prosody into monotone speech; adding speaker similarity destabilised training. Its fix was iterative DPO with a few hundred human preference pairs per round (arXiv 2509.18531).
  • FlowSE-GRPO (speech enhancement): a single reward gives fast metric gains but degrades audio fidelity; multi-metric rewards mitigate this (arXiv 2601.16483).
Inference

Inference Always pair automatic rewards with a naturalness term and listening tests. Run best-of-N under the same reward as the baseline first: arXiv 2608.31035 finds best-of-8 about as good as GRPO perceptually, so RL has to justify its training cost.

Validating reward models

MOS-RMBench (arXiv 2510.00743) converts existing MOS datasets into preference-comparison tasks and tests scalar, semi-scalar and generative reward models. Scalar models perform best overall, with accuracy above 74%; most models do worse on synthetic than on human speech; all struggle with pairs whose MOS differs little. The authors propose a MOS-aware generative reward model that scales its reward by the MOS difference, narrowing the gap with scalar models on the hardest pairs Reported. EmoS (arXiv 2608.09189) is a GRPO-trained evaluator for emotional intelligence in spoken language models, with a bilingual response-pair dataset and 83.8% accuracy on its benchmark, which the authors call approaching human level Reported. The practical rule: measure a judge on close pairs and synthetic audio from your own domain before it supplies a training reward. Inference
