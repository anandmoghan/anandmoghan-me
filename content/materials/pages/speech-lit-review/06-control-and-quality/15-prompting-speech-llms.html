Prompting speech LLMs to shape the output speech

A speech LLM takes several prompts at once, and each one controls a different property of the output. Persona text sets what the agent is, a reference clip sets who it sounds like, an instruction sets how it delivers a line, and the user's own audio nudges its prosody. Steering voice, tone and emotion covers the TTS-side channels; this section covers the speech-to-speech level.

How the model consumes a prompt

  • Everything is context. PersonaPlex concatenates a voice segment and a text segment as a "hybrid system prompt" ahead of the dialogue, with delimiters marking where the system prompt ends. Loss is masked on the system prompt during training. The voice prompt comes first so it can be prefilled when no cloning is needed (arXiv 2602.06053).
  • Instruction in front, content behind. CosyVoice 3 prepends a description and an end token to the text to synthesise (arXiv 2505.17589). Its fine-tuning instructions name speaker and style (for example "You are Speaker A. Please talk to me happily."), and these fields are randomly masked.
  • Edit with an instruction. Step-Audio-EditX takes the original audio plus a descriptive command, and can apply several editing rounds to one clip (arXiv 2511.03601).
  • Inference Classifier-free guidance on the style or instruction condition would act as a strength knob: higher guidance exaggerates the style, lower guidance follows the voice's default. See the CFG material in the speech generation chapter; no source here measures it for prompts.

Where prompting sits in the unified design

In the unified speech LLM design, session setup and per-turn hints are separate concerns.

Inference (design, not sourced)
  • Fix persona text and voice at session start; OpenAI Realtime documents that the voice cannot change once the model has emitted audio.
  • Let the reasoning LLM return a short style field with each reply, such as style: "warm, slightly slower", and pass it to the speech LLM as a style instruction.
  • Keep the style field to a few words; Hume advises instructions of no more than 100 characters.
  • Let the speech LLM adapt prosody to the user's audio on its own; do not ask the reasoning LLM to guess it from a transcript.
  • Gate voice prompts on consent. Synthesia has the voice speaker read a randomly generated passcode (help article); marking and tracing outputs is in Safety and provenance.

Pitfalls

Pitfall Evidence
Tags the voice was not trained on ElevenLabs: "Tags land more readily when the delivery is already in the voice's training data" (docs)
Stage directions read aloud ElevenLabs notes the model may speak delivery guides in the text. Qwen3-Omni's README asks for output of spoken content only, with no descriptions of actions or emotions
Prompt cannot replace the voice OpenAI: prompting "can steer accent, pacing, and delivery, but it cannot fully replace voice design"
Emotion carried by the reference clip Inference A clip carries timbre and delivery together. IndexTTS2 adds a gradient reversal layer and a speaker classifier to disentangle them (arXiv 2506.21619)
Instruction gaps Instruct-FD: the best of six full-duplex systems reaches only 64.4% adherence on turn-taking instructions, scored by an LLM-based judge (arXiv 2607.20460)
Persona does not imply behaviour DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval: 1,038 cases from 240 conversations, eight roles, five conditioning setups, eleven models. Following an explicit floor-management policy did not reliably mean inferring it from a persona, and models that settled benign instruction conflicts often failed when a safety-relevant role should have overridden an explicit directive (arXiv 2609.03423)
Steerability gaps SteerDuplex (Moshi-based) taxonomises text- and audio-based steerability and finds significant gaps in current duplex models. SFT lifts the audio-steering average pass rate by 44.5 points over the strongest open baseline on SteerBench (390 prompts, 1,067 rubrics); RL raises source-clean interruption response from 72.5% to 82.5%, and its reward analysis found gaming through incomplete responses (arXiv 2609.12623)
Brittle adaptation Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 compares speech before and after an interruption. GPT-4o speeds up by 19.04 words per minute; Moshi by 59.55 with falling pitch, which the authors call hurried and brittle (arXiv 2507.23159)
Persona drift PersonaPlex trails Gemini on its GPT-4o-judged role-adherence benchmark; author-reported (arXiv 2602.06053)

To measure adherence, use emotion2vec for emotion similarity (arXiv 2312.15185) and audio-LLM judges for style. AudioJudge reports significant verbosity and positional biases that need mitigation (arXiv 2507.12705), so keep human listening in the loop.

In the interview, say:

"I treat prompting as separate channels: persona text and a voice clip fixed per session, a short style instruction per turn, inline tags for local effects, and the user's audio for adaptation. A reasoning LLM can emit a style hint with each reply, and the speech LLM applies it. I would measure adherence with emotion similarity and calibrated judges, because instruction following is still the weak spot."
