The S2S design space in one picture

Every speech-to-speech system answers the same five questions. The model zoo looks large only because the answers combine in many ways.

Decision Options Common pick Inference
1. Input representation Continuous encoder embeddings; discrete semantic tokens; codec tokens Continuous embeddings from an ASR-pretrained encoder at about 12.5 Hz
2. Output representation Multi-codebook codec tokens; single-codebook semantic tokens; continuous latents Semantic or codec tokens, rarely continuous
3. Text/speech coupling Cascade; text-first (half-cascade); Thinker-Talker; interleaved; parallel streams Text-first or interleaved
4. Streaming/duplex scheme Turn-based; chunked; time-multiplexed; dual-stream; state head Chunked, with a duplex layer on top
5. Acoustic renderer Codec decoder; flow-matching decoder plus vocoder; causal ConvNet AR LM for content, flow or ConvNet for acoustics

The core tension

Speech encoders emit frames at 50 Hz (Whisper, as used in Voxtral; arXiv 2507.13264), while text is far sparser. Every decision above is a way to give the LLM a sequence it can model at text-like cost. The goal is to do that without losing prosody, speaker identity or timing.

Why the LLM can be "downstream"

If the backbone sees speech as embeddings and emits tokens a separate renderer turns into audio, the reasoning model can be swapped, frozen or fine-tuned lightly. Freeze-Omni (arXiv 2411.00774) takes this to the limit by freezing the LLM.

The open problem

Keeping text-level intelligence once the model must speak. URO-Bench reports that open-source spoken dialogue models lag behind their backbone LLMs on instruction following and suffer from catastrophic forgetting (arXiv 2502.17810).

In the interview, say:

"I split any S2S system into five choices: input rep, output rep, text coupling, duplex scheme and renderer. Most new models differ in one or two of them, not all five."
