Multimodal, multilingual S2S

Multilingual S2S is lopsided: models understand far more languages than they speak. Text is the bridge that makes the rest possible.

Coverage asymmetry

Model Text Speech understanding Speech generation
Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765) 119 languages 19 10
Omnilingual ASR (arXiv 2511.09690) n/a 1,600+ languages (ASR only) n/a
SeamlessStreaming (arXiv 2312.05187) 96 target languages in text output 101 source languages 36 target languages

Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765) and Qwen3.5-Omni (arXiv 2604.15804) each report speech generation in 10 languages. Inference Generation is likely the constraint, because it needs clean TTS-grade data per language.

Translation and code-switching

  • Hibiki (arXiv 2502.03382): simultaneous speech-to-speech translation with multistream modelling. It uses per-word delays from text machine translation to build aligned synthetic data. The authors' model has 2.7B parameters and translates French to English (the pair they evaluate).
  • SeamlessStreaming (arXiv 2312.05187): direct streaming translation from 101 source languages into speech in 36 target languages.
  • JUST-DUB-IT (arXiv 2601.22143): the audio-visual case. It adapts a foundation audio-video diffusion model with a lightweight LoRA to dub a video, generating the translated audio and synchronised facial motion together, rather than translating, retiming and re-rendering lips in separate stages. The authors report better visual fidelity, lip sync and robustness than existing dubbing pipelines.
  • Synthesia Dubbing 2.0 (15 July 2026): the product side. The post says the translation engine "respects the original timing and length" and the lip-sync model stays "frame-accurate" through cuts and multi-speaker scenes; it publishes no metrics (post).
  • Video-to-speech (HiCoDiT, arXiv 2604.15923): the reverse direction, generating speech from a silent face. A hierarchical codec diffusion transformer conditions lower RVQ levels on lip motion and identity and higher levels on facial expression for prosody.
Code-switching gap

No dedicated study was found in the sources used here. Treat code-switching as an open question to test, not a solved capability.

How text streams serve S2S

Role Mechanism
Supervision ASR transcripts and TTS text give cheap aligned targets for speech understanding and generation, so unpaired audio can still be used (Kimi-Audio, over 13M h, arXiv 2504.18425)
Scaffold Text is generated first or alongside speech: Moshi's time-aligned Inner Monologue (arXiv 2410.00037), Thinker text before Talker (arXiv 2509.17765), Step-Audio 2 fixed-ratio interleaving (arXiv 2507.16632), ARIA alignment (arXiv 2604.15804)
Alignment Per-word delays (Hibiki, arXiv 2502.03382) and delayed streams (DSM, arXiv 2509.08753) turn transcripts into time-aligned training data
Capability transfer A frozen text LLM (Freeze-Omni, arXiv 2411.00774) or text-teacher distillation (arXiv 2509.14930) carries text-level multilingual knowledge into speech
Inference

Inference For a multilingual product, understand broadly with a multilingual encoder, generate in a short list of languages you can evaluate with native raters, and use the text stream to hold language identity.

In the interview, say:

"Understanding scales through ASR-style data; generation does not. The text stream is both the supervision signal and the scaffold that lets generation borrow the LLM's multilingual ability."
