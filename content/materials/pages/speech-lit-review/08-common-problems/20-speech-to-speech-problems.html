Speech-to-speech and duplex problems

End-to-end and full-duplex models add failures that cascades do not have. The model must listen while it speaks, decide when to talk, and keep its text intelligence while operating on audio tokens. Synthesia's Video Agents "talk, listen, and act in real time" inside a video Confirmed (Synthesia 3.0 post), so these failures reach its interactive product, not only research systems.

Intelligence drop and forgetting

Adding speech capability can degrade an LLM's knowledge and reasoning even when inputs stay textual, and performance drops further on spoken queries (arXiv 2509.14930) Reported. The SALAD authors name the resulting shortfall the "text-speech understanding gap" (arXiv 2510.13632). Two recipes exist: freeze the text LLM (Freeze-Omni, arXiv 2411.00774), or keep a high share of text in the training mix (Moshi trains half of the time on text-only batches, arXiv 2410.00037; Kimi-Audio pretrains on text-only, audio-only and interleaved data, arXiv 2504.18425).

Interleaved synthetic speech-text data lifted spoken question answering from 13% (Moshi) to 31% in one report (arXiv 2411.17607) Reported. Gate every stage on a strict text QA score so forgetting shows up early Inference. See streaming conversation.

Multi-turn degradation

Full-Duplex-Bench v2 tests daily, correction, entity-tracking and safety task families. Its authors report that the tested systems often get confused when people talk at the same time, struggle with corrections and sometimes lose track of who or what is being discussed (arXiv 2510.07838) Reported. FLEXI reports significant gaps between open-source and commercial models in emergency awareness, turn terminating and interaction latency (arXiv 2509.22243) Reported.

Backchannel misread as barge-in

A 2026 survey calls this the single most-cited failure mode in user feedback: the system mistakes the user's acknowledgement ("uh-huh") for a barge-in and stops (arXiv 2606.19453) Reported. Talking Turns finds that audio foundation models can interrupt too aggressively and rarely backchannel themselves (arXiv 2503.01174). The open problem is the trade-off between responding early and yielding falsely Inference.

DuplexSLA reports backchannel accuracy of 98.33%, against 40.00% for gemini-3.1-flash-live and 0.33% for gpt-realtime-1.5 with semantic VAD (arXiv 2605.20755, Table 6) Reported. These numbers are self-reported by the DuplexSLA authors on their own scenarios. Quote them as a claim, not a ranking.

Self-echo, latency spikes and quantisation

  • Self-echo. A model that ingests user audio while speaking can hear its own voice. Mitigate with acoustic echo cancellation and by conditioning on the model's own output stream Inference.
  • Chunk quantisation. SyncLLM alternates 160 ms chunks of user and assistant tokens (arXiv 2409.15594), and the survey calls it an example of "apparent (not substantive)" full duplex, since transitions occur only at chunk boundaries (arXiv 2606.19453) Reported. Turn-taking feel plausibly suffers when decisions are quantised at 80 to 240 ms, trained on heuristic labels, or starved of backchannels in the data Inference.
  • Latency spikes. Reported latencies are measured under different conditions and do not compare Inference. Track p95 and p99, because a conversation is judged by its worst pauses. See streaming and low latency.

Interruption recovery and truncation

On barge-in the agent must stop, and its context must record only the words actually spoken. Otherwise it believes it said a sentence the user never heard. Measure stop latency from the gold onset to the last audible agent sample on echo-cancelled output, plus the fraction of agent speech after onset Inference.

Long-session context and synthetic data

Token-level duplex models grow the KV cache continuously. A back-of-envelope for Moshi's temporal transformer uses its published shape of 32 layers and 4096 dimensions (arXiv 2410.00037) and assumes full multi-head attention in bf16: \( 2 \times 32 \times 4096 \times 2 \) bytes is about 512 KiB per frame, or roughly 23 GB per hour at 12.5 frames per second Inference. Grouped-query attention shrinks it.

Duplex training data is often synthetic: an LLM writes dialogue and TTS renders both sides. SyncLLM trains on 212k hours of synthetic dialogue and 2k hours of real speech (arXiv 2409.15594), and PersonaPlex on synthetic conversations from open LLMs and TTS (arXiv 2602.06053) Reported. Risks are TTS voice and prosody leakage, over-regular inserted backchannels, and circularity, since inserting backchannels needs dialogue that already contains them Inference. DuplexPO applies RL over selected windows of human conversations to learn when to speak separately from what to say (arXiv 2607.07148) Reported. See online RL rewards for per-axis timing rewards and the hacking risks.

Jailbreaks and multilingual safety

A survey of audio jailbreaks (arXiv 2605.30031) groups attacks by where they act (semantic, acoustic, signal and embedding layers) and defences as guard-based, training-free or training-based. It evaluates representative attacks and defences on ten open-source audio-language models and measures attack success rate, benign refusal rate and latency. The authors report that acoustic best-of-N reveals strong worst-case vulnerabilities, that narrative framing is an effective low-latency semantic attack, and that current defences trade robustness against benign usability. Reported They argue for cost- and utility-aware evaluation beside success-rate-only safety benchmarks.

SpeechJBB (arXiv 2606.06037) is a jailbreak dataset in English, French, German, Italian and Spanish, plus code-switched pairs that mix them. It also wraps safety-critical terms in invented, sound-plausible pseudo-words. The authors report high jailbreak success for code-switched harmful audio, strongest for non-English monolingual and non-English code-switched inputs, and refusals that fall as pseudo-word density rises. The failures do not appear to come from weak multilingual comprehension: some models with strong recognition and spoken reasoning are among the most vulnerable. Reported

RedVox (arXiv 2606.26968) is a safety and fairness benchmark built on real voices, with unsafe and stereotyped requests in five languages (English, French, Italian, Spanish, German). The authors found that only 8% of reviewed speech-model releases documented any multilingual safety analysis. On eight models, vulnerabilities persisted without adversarial prompts, were worse outside English and were amplified when requests were spoken. Reported The attack side of provenance (watermarks and detectors) is in Safety and provenance.

Trap:

"Full duplex" in a demo is not full duplex in an evaluation. Ask for false-yield rate, stop latency and multi-turn results, not a recorded conversation.
