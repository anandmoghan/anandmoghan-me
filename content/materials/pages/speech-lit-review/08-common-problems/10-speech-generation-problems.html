Speech generation problems

Thirteen failure modes recur in TTS and voice cloning. Most come from three places: weak text-to-audio alignment, conditioning on a flawed reference, and stochastic sampling. The table is the quick map; the subsections below cover the ones worth explaining aloud.

Grounding labels follow the rest of the page. Synthesia facts cite its Express-2 post and its docs; research claims cite arXiv. Mechanisms marked Inference are my reading, not a source. The Metric and Mitigation columns of the table are suggestions unless a subsection cites them.

A1 Hallucination and alignment

LLM-based TTS models can repeat words, drop words or produce misaligned speech Reported. The authors of arXiv 2406.17957 find that certain cross-attention heads implicitly learn the text-to-speech alignment, and that nothing in the loss forces that alignment to stay monotonic. Regotron adds a monotonic alignment loss to Tacotron 2 for the same class of problem (arXiv 2204.13437).

GOAT reports a strong positive correlation between hallucination and model uncertainty, and over 50% lower character error rate on challenging test cases (arXiv 2508.15442) Reported.

How to measure

  • WER and CER from one fixed, strong ASR model.
  • Optimal Alignment Score, a Viterbi measure of text-speech alignment quality (arXiv 2509.19852).

How to fix

  • CTC loss plus attention priors encourage monotonic cross-attention, with no new learnable parameters (arXiv 2406.17957).
  • Alignment-score training and attention-guided distillation of CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2509.19852).
  • Post-training distribution alignment with GFlowNets (arXiv 2508.15442).

Flow-matching TTS is non-autoregressive, so it has no free-running attention to stall (see diffusion and flow). The cost is that the total duration must be chosen before generation, and that choice can be wrong Inference.

A3 Mispronunciation

Names, acronyms, numbers and heteronyms fail when grapheme-to-phoneme rules or text normalisation guess wrong Inference. Express-Voice operates on graphemes directly, so any fix has to live in the model or in the script Confirmed (Express-2 post).

Synthesia's documented workaround is a per-word pronunciation entry Confirmed. The script docs give a respelling example, "sin-THEE-zhuh" for Synthesia, and offer two previews: an IPA version and a respelling that replaces the text before it reaches the voice model. Constraints from the docs and the help article:

  • A saved pronunciation does not automatically apply to instances of the word added to the script later.
  • "Apply to all" covers every instance in the current video.
  • "Add to glossary" applies across the workspace and is available on Enterprise plans only.
  • Pronunciation reliability varies by voice and language; regenerating the scene can help.

The pattern is a manual patch around a grapheme model. A production fix would add a per-customer dictionary applied before synthesis and a fixed name list for regression tests Inference.

A2 Long-form drift

Over minutes, autoregressive generation can lose speaker identity or flatten prosody because errors compound and nothing re-anchors the voice Inference. Measure speaker similarity in sliding windows against the first window, and plot it against time. Mitigate by synthesising in chunks and re-conditioning each chunk on the original reference.

A6 and A8 Reference leakage

Express-Voice clones in context: the reference audio conditions the model with no fine-tuning Confirmed (Express-2 post). The same mechanism that copies timbre can copy emotion, background noise and microphone channel Inference.

  • Detect leakage with emotion2vec similarity between prompt and output, and a noise-floor check.
  • Gate prompts at upload: reject clipped, noisy or reverberant audio, or denoise first.
  • Prefer neutral, clean references when the target style is set by the script.

A7 Metric pitfalls and A11 codecs

Synthesia names WavLM for speaker similarity and emotion2vec for emotional similarity in its voice evaluation, and publishes no scores Confirmed (Express-2 post). A similarity score depends on which speaker encoder computes it, so scores from different encoders do not compare Inference.

Express-Voice uses Descript's residual vector-quantised tokenizer Confirmed. A metallic or buzzy timbre that survives every sampling setting points at the tokenizer, not the LM Inference. See codecs and tokenizers for the rate trade-offs.

A9 Sampling instability and reranking

Higher temperature plausibly raises the hallucination rate of A1, so a few samples fail even when the average looks good Inference. The cheap fix is best-of-N: sample several candidates, score each, return the best. Score with ASR error plus a speaker-similarity term.

Synthesia applies the same idea to video: Express-Eval scores audio-motion alignment and picks the best motion candidate Confirmed (see audio-visual problems). Pushing the reranking signal into training is online RL, which invites reward hacking. One report finds that GRPO on transcription signals (CER and NLL) lowers error rates but collapses prosody into monotone speech (arXiv 2509.18531) Reported. See online RL rewards for composite rewards and KL control.

A4, A5, A10, A12, A13 in brief

  • Accents. Express-Voice training data was curated for accent coverage, including YODAS and LibriLight. Synthesia reports a blind test with 100 native English-speaking evaluators across 17 accents and top ratings, but names no comparison systems and gives no scores Reported (Express-2 post). Report per accent and per language pair Inference.
  • Prosody. Express-2 offers voice styles for the same voice (for example Excited and Neutral) Confirmed. The script docs add a pause tool with durations from 0.1 s to 99 s (default 1 s) (script docs). That is coarse control, and a flat read needs a script edit Inference.
  • Chunk seams. Streaming trades first-packet time against boundary quality Inference. See streaming and low latency.
  • Languages. Synthesia's own pages disagree on language counts. The Synthesia 3.0 post says dubbing covers 30+ languages, while the title of its AI dubbing page says 130+. Quote the number only with its page.
  • Misuse. Synthesia states that it strictly prohibits cloning voices without explicit consent Confirmed (voice cloning page), and its cloning flow has the speaker read a random passcode. Consent only controls creation. After release, a watermark such as AudioSeal (arXiv 2401.17264) lets a detector find the marked stretch in a longer clip. Speech enhancement can strip several published marks (arXiv 2609.29040), and a detector trained on marked fakes against unmarked real speech learns the mark instead of the fake: equal error rate rose from 16% to 75% in a white-box test (arXiv 2606.23335). Fingerprints survive DPO and GRPO more than some SFT changes (arXiv 2609.21738) Reported. Details are in Safety and provenance.
Trap:

Do not quote a similarity score from a paper next to Synthesia's. The speaker encoder changes the number, so only same-encoder comparisons mean anything.
