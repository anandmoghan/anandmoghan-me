Priorities, metrics and questions

Not every failure deserves equal effort. This ranking weighs how visible the failure is to a customer, how often it occurs in a voice and avatar product, and how cheaply it can be measured. The ranking, mitigations and metrics are my own judgement Inference, built on the sourced problems in the previous sections.

# Problem Mitigation Metric
1 Mispronounced names and brand terms Respelling, project dictionary, glossary Per-entity accuracy on a fixed name list, plus human rating
2 Hallucinated or skipped words in long scripts Monotonic alignment loss; alignment-score training WER on a stable ASR; Viterbi alignment score
3 Lip-sync on bilabials and fast speech AV sync expert; per-phoneme checks Human-rated sync on a phoneme-stratified set, not raw LSE-C
4 Identity and voice drift Windowed re-anchoring Speaker and face-embedding similarity per window vs first
5 Emotion and noise leakage from a bad reference Quality gate; denoising emotion2vec and noise floor on output
6 Voice-face expressiveness mismatch Emotion-conditioned motion; intensity controls Cross-modal emotion agreement
7 Hand and gesture artefacts Candidate reranking (Express-Eval style) Human plausibility; hand-artefact detector rate
8 Dubbing duration mismatch and lip re-render Duration-aware translation; retiming Fraction outside timing budget; sync after retiming
9 Streaming latency versus quality; A/V drift Faster renderer; seam smoothing Time-to-first-frame; A/V offset over duration
10 Duplex failures: barge-in misread, backchannels, multi-turn decay Echo cancellation; turn-taking evaluation Full-Duplex-Bench v1 and v2 interruption and multi-turn tracks
11 Voice misuse: unconsented clones, stripped watermarks, spoken jailbreaks Consent gate; multi-bit watermark tested against enhancement and codecs; detectors trained with marks on real and fake Consent-check pass rate; mark recovery after enhancement; per-language jailbreak success and benign refusal rate

Why this order

  • Items 1 and 2 are audible on first listen and cheap to test automatically, so they belong in every regression run.
  • Items 3 to 7 are the avatar-specific problems; they need human rating because the automatic metrics are weak.
  • Items 8 to 10 depend on product shape (dubbing, streaming, Video Agents) and rank higher if that product is the focus.
  • Item 11 ranks low on audible frequency but high on severity, and it is cheap to gate at creation; see Safety and provenance.
  • Any metric that is optimised in training needs a held-out judge, or reward hacking will hide regressions (see online RL rewards).

Questions to ask Synthesia about failure modes

  1. Which failure modes do you track in production, and which of them have an automatic metric?
  2. How do you evaluate lip-sync, given the known weaknesses of Sync-C, and does it differ for non-English dubbing?
  3. What does Express-Eval catch in practice: gesture plausibility, lip-sync, or both, and how often does it change the pick?
  4. For Express-Voice, how do you protect against emotion or noise leakage from customer reference audio?
  5. For Video Agents, what is the p95 first-frame latency, and how do you handle barge-in and backchannels?
In the interview, say:

"The Express-2 post publishes no failure metrics, so I would start by asking which ones you track. My first three would be per-entity pronunciation, alignment-based hallucination rate, and phoneme-stratified lip-sync with human raters, because papers report that Sync-C correlates weakly with human judgement."
