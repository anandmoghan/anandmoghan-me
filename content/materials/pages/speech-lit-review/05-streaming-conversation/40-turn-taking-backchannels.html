Turn-taking prediction and the human baseline

Turn-taking is a prediction problem: when does the other speaker stop, and should I start? The field moved from stand-alone predictors to end-of-turn detectors in cascades, then to semantic detectors that read the audio itself, and now to duplex models that fold the decision into generation.

Predictors: TurnGPT and VAP

  • TurnGPT (Ekstedt and Skantze, Findings of EMNLP 2020, arXiv 2010.10874): a transformer language model that predicts turn shifts in spoken dialogue from text.
  • Voice Activity Projection, VAP (Ekstedt and Skantze, Interspeech 2022, arXiv 2205.09812): a self-supervised predictive objective on the voice activity of both speakers, so turn-taking models train without labels.
  • Multilingual VAP (LREC-COLING 2024, arXiv 2403.06487): English, Mandarin and Japanese. A model trained on one language predicts poorly on the others; one multilingual model performs on par with the monolingual ones and learns to identify the input language.

Semantic end-of-turn detectors

Silence length alone cannot tell a hesitation from a finished thought. A family of 2025 and 2026 detectors decides from partial speech, usually without waiting for an ASR transcript.

CoDeTT (arXiv 2603.25434) is the benchmark built to compare such systems: it frames turn-taking as a structured decision problem with fine-grained decision categories and controlled context, and the authors observe substantial performance disparities across decision types and scenarios. The common design lesson is the same as the vendor models above: report false cutoffs and latency at a stated operating point.

The human baseline

On smooth transfers, humans start speaking a median of 151 ms before the other turn ends (TurnBench, arXiv 2608.25218). Define the floor-transfer offset as

\[ \Delta = t_{\text{agent onset}} - t_{\text{user end}}, \qquad \Delta_{\text{human, median}} = -151\ \text{ms}. \]

A cascade that waits for silence can only produce \( \Delta > 0 \). A negative offset needs prediction, which is what VAP and duplex models do. Inference

TurnBench (arXiv 2608.25218, Aug 2026) is an annotated benchmark of end-of-turn (EOT) and interruption detection. The authors report about 30 h of dual-channel dyadic speech in 154 dialogues, each triple-annotated. A segment is gold when at least 2 of 3 annotators give the same label with endpoints within \( \pm 200 \) ms. Results are recall, false-positive rate (FPR) and median latency, across 14 turn-taking systems.

VAP baseline Recall FPR Median latency
End of turn 0.845 0.055 368 ms
Interruption 0.945 0.107 994 ms

The TurnBench authors state the open problem: no current system matches human timing without excessive false positives.

In the interview, say:

"Humans start a median 151 ms before the other turn ends, so the target offset is negative, which needs prediction. I would report floor-transfer offset against that baseline, at a stated false-interruption rate."
