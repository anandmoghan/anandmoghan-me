Delay as a knob and interleaved decoders

Moshi's text-audio offset is a design choice, and other systems make the same choice differently. This section covers delay as a knob (DSM, Hibiki) and the fixed-ratio interleaving families.

DSM: delay as a knob

Delayed Streams Modeling (arXiv 2509.08753) pre-aligns streams and applies a fixed delay per stream. In the paper's framing, ASR corresponds to the text stream being delayed relative to audio, and TTS to the opposite.

Task Delayed stream Delay Authors' result
ASR Text, behind audio 2.5 s default, about 31 frames Short-form average WER 6.4 (Canary-Qwen-2.5B 5.6; Whisper large-v3 7.5)
TTS Audio, behind text 1.28 s, 16 steps Latency 150 ms at batch size 1

Inference Delay becomes a per-stream setting, not an architecture. ASR needs audio lookahead before it commits a word. TTS needs text lookahead before it commits prosody. For "text and voice at once", the same decoder with a chosen offset is the lever.

Hibiki: per-word delay

Hibiki (arXiv 2502.03382) is a multistream decoder-only model for speech translation. It sets a delay per word, using the perplexity of an off-the-shelf text translation model (MADLAD-3B) on source prefixes. The target is required to lag the source by at least 2 s. The authors report LAAL of 3.4 s on short-form and 5.0 s on long-form audio, against Seamless at 2.8 s and 4.2 s. On the same tables Hibiki scores ASR-BLEU 35.5 against Seamless at 33.8 (short-form) and 26.6 against 23.9 (long-form).

Inference Moshi aligns text to the clock (padding fills the gaps). Interleaving aligns text to the order (no clock, so no padding, but no free time axis). Thinker-Talker separates the two jobs: the Thinker can finish a thought before the Talker voices it. Reasoning and live speech together in one duplex model is now reported (Mind-Paced Speaking, arXiv 2510.09592; Chronological Thinking, arXiv 2510.05150), with the thinking placed in the listening window or paced against the voice; see the previous section.

Trap

A ratio is not a duration. "13:26" tells you the mix of tokens, and only the speech token rate turns it into seconds. "First chunk" latency is also not the same as steady-state delay.

In the interview, say:

"DSM shows the offset between streams is a setting: delay the text for ASR, delay the audio for TTS. Interleaving models fix an order instead of a clock, so a ratio like 13:26 only becomes a duration through the speech token rate."
