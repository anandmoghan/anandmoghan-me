Streaming decoders and the latency budget

The decoder streams too, and its lookahead is latency. This section ends with the end-to-end budget and a checklist.

Streaming codec decoding

The decoder must also stream, and any lookahead it needs is added latency.

System Streaming decode
Mimi (arXiv 2410.00037) Causal encoder and decoder; 80 ms frames
Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765) Lightweight causal ConvNet (Code2Wav) replaces block-wise diffusion
Qwen2.5-Omni (arXiv 2503.20215) Codec DiT with 2 blocks lookback, 1 block lookahead

Kimi-Audio (arXiv 2504.18425) Chunk-wise autoregressive detokenizer with look-ahead (example: 1 s chunk, 4 future semantic tokens)
GLM-4-Voice (arXiv 2412.02612) 0.8 s decoder blocks; at least 10 speech tokens needed for the first output

Inference One block of lookahead costs one block of delay. A fully causal decoder such as Mimi's pays none, which is part of why 160 ms is achievable.

Checklist

  • Report latency with its definition: theory, first packet, first chunk, turn-taking delay or total.
  • State batch size, GPU and whether the run was cold start.
  • Measure the worst frame, not just RTF; a missed 80 ms deadline is audible.
  • Compute the KV-cache cost per session before promising concurrency.
  • Test barge-in on echo-cancelled output and report stop latency.
  • For tool-using agents, report tool-call latency separately and say whether the call overlapped user speech.
  • Add the decision wait \( c/2 \) when comparing chunked and per-frame systems.
  • Run a session past the reported context (Moshi: 5 minutes) to see what happens at the limit.
In the interview, say:

"A duplex model has an 80 ms deadline every frame, silent or not. So I budget the worst frame, the cache per session and the decision wait, and I quote latency with its definition and load."
