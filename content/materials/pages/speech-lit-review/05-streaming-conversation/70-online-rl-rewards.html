Online RL and rewards for speech generation

The common pattern: the policy is a codec or discrete-token LM (sometimes flow matching), sampled in groups. Rewards come from the decoded audio: ASR error for intelligibility, speaker-verification (SV) cosine for similarity, a quality or preference predictor for naturalness. The main failure is reward hacking on any single optimised score. Duplex timing is the newest axis, with per-axis rewards derived from VAD events. The same loop now trains recognisers (an acoustic-fidelity or delay term in the reward) and voice agents (per-tool-call rewards); see Bridge from GRPO on streaming ASR.

Evidence quality

Every claim below cites a paper or an official repository or model card. Kyutai publishes RL-tuned Moshi and PersonaPlex checkpoints on its Hugging Face account. Numbers are the authors' own and come from different setups, so they are not comparable across rows.

The TTS-side summary (DiffRO, F5R-TTS, hacking signatures, listening gates) is in Post-training and evaluation for speech generation. This section adds reward implementations, the duplex rewards, and infrastructure.

Duplex timing rewards

  • DuplexPO (arXiv 2607.07148). The Factorized Conversational Dynamics Reward covers turn initiation, backchanneling, yielding and regularised participation. It optimises only windows around annotated agent events, GRPO-style.
  • Kyutai multi-faceted alignment (arXiv 2606.11167). Reported RL post-training of Moshi and PersonaPlex with one reward per axis (pause handling, turn-taking, backchanneling, user interruption) plus an LLM-based response-quality reward against semantic degradation. Per the official model card, GRPO uses rule-based rewards, segments come from Meta's Seamless Interaction corpus via VAD, and evaluation uses Full-Duplex-Bench v1 and v2. Checkpoints: kyutai/moshika-rl-seamless and kyutai/personaplex-rl-seamless.
  • ASPIRin (arXiv 2604.10065). GRPO with a binary speaking-or-silence mapping of text tokens. The authors report over 50% fewer duplicate n-grams than standard GRPO.
  • SteerDuplex (arXiv 2609.12623). Reported A Moshi-based model with two-stage RL on hybrid rewards that combine verifiable interaction checks and judge-based semantic feedback. RL raised source-clean interruption response from 72.5% to 82.5% and cut synthetic pause barge-in from 26.5% to 9%, with steering and aggregate task scores comparable or higher. The reward analysis found the model gaming the reward through incomplete responses, so timing gains need a completeness check.
  • Multi-reward DPO for blockwise duplex. Semantic, audio-quality and emotion-consistency rewards aggregated in one DPO objective, studied on blockwise duplex models (arXiv 2601.19063).

Algorithms

GRPO dominates for codec LMs: sample a group of \( G \) rollouts per prompt, normalise rewards within the group, and keep a KL penalty to the SFT reference. Standard notation for the advantage of rollout \( i \):

\[ \hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \operatorname{mean}(r_1,\dots,r_G)}{\operatorname{std}(r_1,\dots,r_G)} \]

With several rewards, a common choice is to normalise each reward within the group before summing. Inference GROW (arXiv 2608.03215) standardises its rewards within the group; I have not checked other papers.

Trap: the train reward is not the metric

If the reward ASR is also the evaluation ASR, WER improvements may be hacking. Evaluate with a second ASR, a second SV model, and a listening panel.

Infrastructure and cost

Every rollout must be decoded to a waveform, then passed through an ASR and an SV model. Inference A SpeechJudge-style reward model dominates the cost. Cheaper options are token-level rewards, cached reference embeddings and a small ASR for the reward.

  • FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice: GRPO training support and an RL model, per the README.
  • zai-org/GLM-TTS: multi-reward GRPO TTS README.
  • AmphionTeam/SpeechJudge: the generative reward model.
  • Kyutai RL checkpoints: kyutai/moshika-rl-seamless, kyutai/personaplex-rl-seamless.

Bridge from GRPO on streaming ASR, and RL for voice agents

The same GRPO loop now has recognition and agent versions, which show how the reward changes while the machinery stays.

Paper Task Reward change and result
Grounded in Sound (arXiv 2609.14455) ASR with a 7B speech LLM Adds an acoustic-fidelity reward from a frozen, separately pretrained wav2vec2-CTC character-level judge, used only in training. Insertion errors fall 28.3% on AMI-IHM and 22.3% on AMI-SDM; WER on AMI-SDM goes from 35.89% to 34.71%. No detectable WER difference on the other five tiers against a schedule-matched WER-GRPO baseline. Inference uses one model with greedy decoding. The authors call it a mechanism paper shown in one setup (Reported)
Look Less, Hear Better (arXiv 2609.18333) Streaming ASR, delayed-streams recogniser One GRPO reward scores accuracy and emission delay (AWED, a word-level delay relative to each word's acoustic end). WER falls 30.8% relative at 80 ms structural delay and 5.7% at 480 ms; median AWED goes from 1.17 s to 1.04 s. The authors find the structural delay is a weak proxy for perceived latency (Reported)
SpeechGym (arXiv 2608.26432) Voice agents with tools, audio only Two omni-modal models talk in native audio on a text agent benchmark's unchanged tasks, tools and success checks. Outcome-only GRPO gets too little signal because nearly every group fails alike; a per-turn process reward crediting each successful tool call restores it. The abstract says task success more than doubles on an independently built voice benchmark, with no exact figure (Reported)

Inference: what transfers
  • Group sampling, per-group advantage normalisation, KL to a reference and clip monitoring are the same machinery.
  • A zero-advantage group (every rollout scores alike) is the failure SpeechGym reports; watch its fraction for any sparse reward.
  • WER is a reward I already know how to shape; adding an acoustic or delay term is how the ASR papers fix its blind spots.
  • New in TTS: rollouts need waveform decode, SV and quality rewards compete, and hacking shows up as monotone prosody rather than a bad transcript.
  • New in duplex: rewards are timing events on audio, not text metrics, so windows around events replace whole-sequence rollouts.

Practical recipes

Inference: starting points
  1. Zero-shot TTS. WER or CER from a fixed ASR that differs from the evaluation ASR; SV cosine with cached references; a small-weight UTMOS or DNSMOS composite; GRPO with KL; per-group normalisation per reward; a duration-ratio penalty; held-out judges (second ASR, second SV, listening panel).
  2. Expressive TTS. Add a reasoning RM or audio-LLM judge with a rubric, or an emotion classifier, gated by an ensemble; track F0 variance, energy and rate against human references.
  3. Baseline first. Run best-of-N under the same reward before training; if GRPO does not beat it perceptually (arXiv 2608.31035), RL only moves the cost from inference to training.
  4. Full-duplex. Per-axis timing rewards from VAD on generated audio against annotated events (DuplexPO, Kyutai); a text-level LLM-quality reward against content drift; train on windows around events.

Monitor: per-reward mean and variance, fraction of zero-advantage groups, held-out metrics, F0 spread, silence ratio, speaking rate, duration distribution, KL, clip fraction and end-of-sequence behaviour. Stop when held-out metrics degrade even if the train reward rises, and spot-check audio.

In the interview, say:

"I would run GRPO with a composite of ASR error, speaker cosine and a quality model, normalised per group, with KL and held-out judges. For duplex I would add per-axis timing rewards from VAD events plus a text-quality term so timing gains do not cost content. Single-score rewards get hacked, so I would validate every reward against held-out judges and listeners."
