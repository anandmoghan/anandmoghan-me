Wiring the decision and the turn-taking feel

The same families can be read as three wirings of one decision. The wiring explains most of what makes a duplex model feel like, or unlike, a person talking.

Three ways to wire the decision

  • Dual-stream: no separate decision; speaking and listening are both tokens on every frame.
  • Flattened: one sequence, so who speaks is decided by which role's tokens come next.
  • Control head: a small classifier reads hidden states and gates a mostly unchanged LLM. This is why Freeze-Omni can keep its LLM frozen.

The 2026 survey (arXiv 2606.19453) calls SyncLLM an L2-shaped but "apparent (not substantive)" full-duplex system.

What causes the turn-taking feel

Inference Two causes follow from the sources, both about the decision and not about speech quality:

  1. Decisions quantised at chunk boundaries. A decision waits for the next boundary, \( c/2 \) on average for chunk length \( c \) (derived). That is 40 ms for an 80 ms frame, 120 ms for a 240 ms chunk and 500 ms for a 1.0 s chunk.
  2. Scarce interaction data. The 2025 survey names synchronous data scarcity (arXiv 2509.14515). The 2026 survey finds that observed behaviour is constrained by the interaction patterns in training and evaluation (arXiv 2606.19453).

A related finding: audio foundation models miss when to speak, interrupt too aggressively and rarely backchannel (Talking Turns, arXiv 2503.01174). Inference My reading: the speech path is not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the duplex decision, plus ingesting user audio while the model is speaking its own.

Fit for a Synthesia voice agent

The closest patterns are DSM-style delay streams plus an action channel (DuplexSLA), or a token-level L2 design like Moshi. For reasoning and speech together, name the options: think in the listening window (Chronological Thinking, arXiv 2510.05150; FLAIR, arXiv 2603.17837), pace the voice with a reasoner (arXiv 2510.09592), or pair the duplex model with a bigger thinker (KAME, arXiv 2510.02327; MoshiRAG, arXiv 2604.12928). Their reported gains are on different benchmarks, so compare them only with that in mind.

In the interview, say:

"The speech path is not the hard part. The hard part is the decision, which is quantised by chunk size and limited by the interaction patterns in the training data. So I would compare duplex systems on interaction metrics at a fixed operating point, not on a single latency number."
