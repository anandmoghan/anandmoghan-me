Producing text and speech together: Moshi

Voice-to-voice models that "reason and talk" must emit text and audio at the same time. The sources show three coupling patterns. This section covers Moshi, the delayed parallel stream, and then the work that uses the same streams to think while listening or while speaking. The next section covers delay as a knob and fixed interleaving. The control head that decides when to speak is in the full-duplex sections.

Pattern How text and audio line up Models
Delayed parallel stream Same clock; text and audio are separate token streams offset by a chosen delay Moshi (arXiv 2410.00037), DSM (arXiv 2509.08753), Hibiki (arXiv 2502.03382)

Thinker-Talker The Talker uses the Thinker's hidden representations to produce audio tokens Qwen2.5-Omni (arXiv 2503.20215), Qwen3-Omni (arXiv 2509.17765)

Moshi: the codec and the clock

Moshi (arXiv 2410.00037) models speech with Mimi tokens at 12.5 Hz. Mimi has 8 RVQ levels of 2048 codes. The first level is a semantic code distilled from WavLM; the other seven are acoustic. The codec is causal, with an 80 ms frame. Confirmed

Each 80 ms frame holds 17 streams: 1 text token, 8 Moshi audio tokens and 8 user audio tokens (\( K = 2Q + 1 = 17 \) with \( Q = 8 \)). The user stream is just another input: the model hears the user continuously while it speaks.

Two transformers per frame

  • Temporal transformer: 4096-dim, 32 layers, run once per frame over time. It is initialised from the Helium text LM (about 2.1T training tokens) and outputs a context vector.
  • Depth transformer: 1024-dim, 6 layers, random initialisation. It turns the context vector into up to 17 sub-tokens inside the frame.
  • The split keeps the big model at 12.5 steps per second. Only the small model runs 17 times per step. Inference

Inner Monologue: text, PAD and delays

Moshi predicts a text token for the agent's own speech on the same 12.5 Hz grid. Frames with no new word carry PAD. The paper also defines an EPAD token, inserted before the next word to mark the end of the padding. Padding is about 65% of text-stream tokens in English conversational speech.

Text is the first sub-step of each frame, so that frame's audio tokens are conditioned on it (derived from the sub-step order: text, Moshi audio, user audio). Inference

Latency follows from the delay: 160 ms theoretical with acoustic delay 1, about 200 ms in practice. The paper also gives 640 ms for acoustic delay 2. The recipe: Helium text LM, then 7M h of audio transcribed with Whisper large-v3 (single stream), then multi-stream post-training on diarised audio, a Fisher fine-tune (2,000 h of phone conversation), and instruction tuning on more than 20k h of synthetic speech (arXiv 2410.00037). Thinking while listening and while speaking

Moshi's text lane gives a model room to plan, but its text is the words it is about to say. Several 2025 and 2026 papers use the idle or overlapping time differently: they reason during the user's turn, or they let a slower reasoner pace the speaker.

Approach When the model thinks Mechanism Reported
Chronological Thinking (arXiv 2510.05150) While the user speaks Replaces repeated silence-token prediction with strictly causal incremental reasoning, using only past audio. Thinking halts when the user stops, so it adds no delay Better response quality on objective metrics and human evaluation; competitive full-duplex metrics. The abstract gives no numbers
FLAIR (arXiv 2603.17837) While the user speaks Latent thinking: each step feeds its latent output into the next. Trained with an Evidence Lower Bound objective and teacher forcing, so no reasoning annotations are needed Competitive on speech benchmarks and full-duplex metrics; no added latency. The abstract gives no numbers
LTS-VoiceAgent (arXiv 2601.19952) On meaningful prefixes of the user's speech Cascaded, not end to end. A Dynamic Semantic Trigger picks when to think; a background Thinker keeps state and a foreground Speaker solves speculatively Better accuracy-latency-efficiency trade-off than serial cascades on VERA, Spoken-MQA, BigBenchAudio and the authors' Pause-and-Repair benchmark
Mind-Paced Speaking (arXiv 2510.09592) While speaking A Formulation Brain reasons and sets the pace; a separate Articulation Brain speaks before the reasoning finishes. The split removes mode-switching 92.8% on Spoken-MQA and 82.5 on URO-Bench in a zero-latency configuration; underlies Step-Audio R1.1
KAME (arXiv 2510.02327) In a back-end LLM, in parallel with speech The S2S model answers at once; the LLM's text answer is injected in real time to guide the speech Correctness approaches a cascade on speech-synthesized MT-Bench; latency on par with the S2S baseline
MoshiRAG (arXiv 2604.12928) During the gap before the answer's core content A compact duplex model flags knowledge-demanding queries and retrieves asynchronously; retrieval methods are swappable without retraining Factuality comparable to the best released non-duplex speech LMs
DuplexOmni (arXiv 2606.09186) In a pluggable thinking layer, asynchronously An interaction layer handles streaming audio and video and speaks; the thinking layer provides reasoning and tool use Strong results on public benchmarks; the abstract gives no numbers

Inference The first four rows differ in who pays for the thinking. Chronological Thinking and FLAIR spend the listening window and pay nothing at the turn boundary. Mind-Paced Speaking spends time while talking, so the voice must be paced by the reasoner. KAME, MoshiRAG and DuplexOmni move the thinking to a second model, which keeps the duplex model small and adds a hand-off. Model-by-model comparison of the tandem family: S2S architectures.

In the interview, say:

"Moshi keeps text and audio on one 12.5 Hz clock and pads the text stream, about 65% PAD. A small depth transformer expands each 80 ms frame into 17 sub-tokens, so the big model runs only 12.5 times a second. Recent work spends the listening window on causal or latent reasoning, or pairs the small duplex model with a bigger thinker."
