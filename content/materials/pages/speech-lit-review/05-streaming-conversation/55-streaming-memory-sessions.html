Streaming inference: memory and many sessions

Memory, not compute, often sets how many duplex sessions one GPU can hold. This section covers the cache, long sessions and batching.

KV-cache growth

Inference This arithmetic is derived under stated assumptions: full multi-head attention, bf16 storage, and only the temporal transformer counted (32 layers, model dimension 4096, from Moshi's Table 1, arXiv 2410.00037). The depth transformer's cache can be discarded each frame.

\[ 2 \times 32 \times 4096 \times 2\ \text{B} = 524{,}288\ \text{B} \approx 512\ \text{KiB per frame} \]

The leading 2 is keys plus values; the trailing 2 is bytes per bf16 value. At 12.5 frames per second that is 512 KiB \( \times 12.5 \approx 6.3 \) MiB per second, or about 23 GB for one hour (derived). Grouped-query attention would shrink it.

The cache grows linearly with session length and with the number of sessions. Inference For many concurrent sessions, cache memory, not weights, sets capacity.

Long sessions

  • The Moshi paper states the model follows multi-turn conversations across 5 minutes (arXiv 2410.00037). Ask what happens beyond that before claiming more.
  • One windowing pattern for audio is block-local attention in Qwen2.5-Omni's streaming codec DiT: 2 blocks of lookback and 1 of lookahead (arXiv 2503.20215).
  • A learned pooling of the speech KV cache inside the LLM (SpeechKV) compresses speech to about text-level granularity. The authors, working on speech LLMs more broadly and not a duplex model, report performance on par with or slightly better than uncompressed (relative gains of 6.6% on out-of-domain entity recognition and 2.3% on OpenASR) and at least 1.49 times decoding speedup that grows with audio length (arXiv 2607.06827).
  • A NeurIPS 2026 listing proposes acoustic-to-text KV consolidation for full-duplex models. A side channel transcribes incoming speech into compact text during idle listening time; when the cache exceeds its budget, older acoustic states are evicted while the transcript and recent audio are kept. It is trained with LoRA and distillation. On ten-minute LongSpeech sessions with MiniCPM-o 4.5 the authors report a 64.6% smaller peak streaming KV cache than the same model without eviction, with comparable pause handling, turn-taking and interruption on Full-Duplex-Bench (NeurIPS listing; no arXiv id found). Reported
  • For video, Self Forcing (arXiv 2506.08009) uses a rolling KV cache for autoregressive video extrapolation at inference. It is a related pattern for unbounded streams, but it is a video model.

Batching many duplex sessions

Inference A turn-based server pays nothing for an idle chat. A duplex session is always active: it needs one frame every 80 ms, including silence. Batching sessions into one GPU step helps throughput, but the whole batched step still has to finish inside 80 ms. The sustainable batch size is the largest \( B \) with \( t_{\text{step}}(B) < 80 \) ms.

DSM-TTS reports 150 ms latency at batch size 1 and about 380 ms at batch size 32 (arXiv 2509.08753, Table 6), so latency figures depend on load.
