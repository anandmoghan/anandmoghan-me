Latency in frames, video alignment and vocabulary

Latency is easiest to reason about in frames. The same clock arithmetic also aligns audio with video, and fixes the vocabulary for the interview.

Seconds to frames, and Moshi's latency

Divide any delay by 0.08 s to count frames.

Moshi's 160 ms is one 80 ms frame plus one 80 ms acoustic-delay step: \( 2 \times 80 = 160 \) ms (derived decomposition). The paper reports about 200 ms in practice. With acoustic delay 2 the paper states a theoretical latency of 8 timesteps, 640 ms (arXiv 2410.00037). Confirmed

Trap

Do not generalise the 160 ms into a formula such as "(1 + delay) frames". Delay 2 would give 240 ms, not the 640 ms the paper quotes, and this chapter does not derive how the 8 timesteps decompose. Quote the two numbers as stated and say which acoustic delay each belongs to.

Video alignment

Video frames run at 25 or 30 fps, against 12.5 Hz for audio tokens. At 25 fps there are exactly 2 video frames per audio frame. At 30 fps there are \( 30 / 12.5 = 2.4 \), which is not an integer, so the two streams cannot share a frame grid (derived).

Video latents are coarser than pixel frames. Wan 2.1's causal VAE has a latent length of about \( 1 + T/4 \) for \( T \) frames (arXiv 2503.20314), so 25 fps becomes 6.25 latent frames per second and 30 fps becomes 7.5 (derived).

Ovi aligns the two token rates without resampling. It runs RoPE on both streams and scales the audio RoPE frequencies by \( 31/157 \approx 0.197 \), so 31 video latent frames line up with 157 audio tokens (arXiv 2510.01284). That is about \( 157 / 31 \approx 5.06 \) audio tokens per video latent frame (derived).

Say it right in the interview

Term What it counts Example Common slip
Sample rate Waveform samples per second (Hz, kHz) 24 kHz audio into Mimi Calling it the frame rate
Hop Spacing between STFT windows (ms) 10 ms hop gives 100 Hz mel frames Confusing it with the LLM's step
Frame rate Latent vectors per second after an encoder or codec Mimi 12.5 Hz, 80 ms per frame Treating a frame as one token
Token rate Discrete ids per second, summed over codebooks Mimi 100 tokens/s Saying "12.5 Hz" for it
Bitrate Bits per second of the code Mimi 1.1 kbps Quoting it without codebook size
Chunk size Audio processed or emitted per step; a multiple of frames LLaMA-Omni 2: 400 ms; DuplexSLA: 160 ms Using "chunk" and "frame" interchangeably
Stride How far the input advances per step DuplexSLA user stream: 80 ms Equating it with the chunk
Delay (lookahead) Offset between streams, or future context a model waits for Moshi acoustic delay 1; DSM ASR text delay 2.5 s Equating it with end-to-end latency
Latency Time from an event to the response Moshi 160 ms theory, about 200 ms practice Comparing theory with measured numbers
In the interview, say:

"I keep four numbers apart: sample rate, frame rate, token rate and chunk size. Mimi is 24 kHz in, 12.5 Hz frames, 100 tokens a second, and chunks are whatever the system batches on top."
