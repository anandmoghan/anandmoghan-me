Rates, frames and clocks

A speech system runs several clocks at once, and "the frame rate" can mean any of them. Keep the clocks apart and every number in this chapter becomes easy to check. The anchor to remember is 12.5 Hz, which is one frame every 80 ms.

How numbers are labelled

Every number carries its source. Arithmetic done for this chapter is marked derived. The 16 kHz input, 25 ms window, 10 ms hop and Whisper's stride-2 convolution come from the Whisper paper (arXiv 2212.04356); see Whisper and the Whisper encoder.

The ladder from waveform to LLM

Stage Rate Period Worked arithmetic

The whole chain for one Whisper-style input path:

\[ 16000\ \text{Hz} \xrightarrow{\ \text{hop } 160\ } 100\ \text{Hz} \xrightarrow{\ \div 2\ } 50\ \text{Hz} \xrightarrow{\ \div 4\ } 12.5\ \text{Hz} \]

Other encoders reach 12.5 Hz by other routes. Step-Audio 2 runs a frozen audio encoder at 25 Hz and a 2x adaptor, giving 12.5 Hz (arXiv 2507.16632).

The reason to stop at 12.5 Hz is sequence length. Thirty minutes is 1,800 s, so 50 Hz gives \( 1800 \times 50 = 90{,}000 \) positions (derived). At 12.5 Hz the same audio is \( 1800 \times 12.5 = 22{,}500 \) positions (derived).

Codec frame rates, tokens per second, bitrate

A codec frame is a vector in time. A token is one integer id. A residual quantiser (RVQ) turns each frame into one id per codebook, so token rate and frame rate differ.

\[ \text{tokens/s} = f_{\text{frame}} \times N_{\text{codebooks}} \qquad \text{bitrate} = f_{\text{frame}} \times N_{\text{codebooks}} \times \log_2 V \]

Mimi has \( f = 12.5 \) Hz, \( N = 8 \) and \( V = 2048 = 2^{11} \), so \( 12.5 \times 8 = 100 \) tokens/s and \( 100 \times 11 = 1100 \) bps. That matches the 1.1 kbps in the Mimi paper (arXiv 2410.00037). Confirmed

Trap

"Mimi runs at 12.5 Hz" is the frame rate. The LM sees 100 tokens per second on the audio side alone, 200 for a user stream plus an agent stream (derived), and that is why the depth transformer exists (next section). Single-codebook codecs avoid this: their frame rate is their token rate.

In the interview, say:

"Mimi runs at 12.5 Hz, so one frame is 80 ms. With eight codebooks that is 100 tokens a second, and 1.1 kbps at eleven bits per token. Text and both audio streams share that one clock, which is why Moshi has no explicit turn state."
