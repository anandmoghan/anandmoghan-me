Evaluating conversational systems

Evaluation moved from demos to automated overlap benchmarks (Full-Duplex-Bench) and annotated dual-channel benchmarks (TurnBench, arXiv 2608.25218, Aug 2026). Latency alone is a weak number: it only means something at a stated false-interruption rate. Fixed binary timing windows are a second weakness: TACT (arXiv 2609.27372) scores turn-taking against the speaker's inferred intent instead. Timing is also only half the picture, so capability and instruction-following suites (URO-Bench, DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval, M3-DuplexBench) sit beside it. The open problem, per the TurnBench authors, is that no current system matches human timing without excessive false positives.

Latency definitions and operating points

LiveKit's eot-bench (LiveKit blog) reports two views of the same detector: the false-cutoff rate at a fixed latency budget (300 or 600 ms), and the mean latency at a fixed false-cutoff target (5% or 10%). Reported For LiveKit's own v1, the blog gives 9.9% false cutoffs at 300 ms and 4.5% at 600 ms, and 543 ms mean latency at 5% and 295 ms at 10%. It is a vendor benchmark, but the operating-point framing is reusable for any turn-taking system.

Vendor latency claims are not comparable. Deepgram's Flux docs state "~260ms end-of-turn detection" inside the STT model, without a stated benchmark. That cannot be compared with the eot-bench operating points above.

Benchmarks

Benchmark What it measures Notes
Full-Duplex-Bench v1 (arXiv 2503.04721) Pause handling, backchanneling, turn-taking, interruption Automatic, reproducible metrics; released with code
Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 (2507.23159) User interruption, user backchannel, talking to others, background speech Categorical behaviour, stop and response latency, prosodic adaptation; on five agents the authors find "responsive" and "floor-holding" strategies (ICASSP 2026)
Full-Duplex-Bench v2 (2510.07838) Multi-turn streaming interaction Automated examiner with staged goals under Fast and Slow pacing; task families daily, correction, entity tracking and safety; supports commercial APIs and open models
FLEXI (2509.22243) Latency, response quality and conversational effectiveness across six scenarios, including emergency interruption The authors report gaps between open-source and commercial models in recognising emergencies, ending turns and latency
TurnBench (2608.25218) End-of-turn and interruption detection ~30 h dual-channel, 154 dialogues, triple-annotated, plus a 104 h training set and public leaderboard; gold is 2 of 3 annotators within ±200 ms; 14 systems scored on recall, false-positive rate, median latency
TACT (2609.27372) Intent-conditioned turn-taking 9,728 episodes (73.2 h) from five dyadic corpora, each with dialogue history, a per-speaker memory profile and an annotator posterior over six intent classes. Scored with a strictly proper, threshold-weighted continuous ranked probability score whose weights come from intent-conditioned timing kernels fitted to human floor-transfer offsets
DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval (2609.03423) Implicit instruction following in full-duplex agents 1,038 cases from 240 controlled conversations, eight assistant roles, five conditioning setups (default, explicit instructions, persona-implied behaviour, persona plus rule, instruction conflict). Deterministic Instruction Adherence Score for turn-taking plus an LLM-judged Persona Adherence Score for content; 11 real-time speech models
M3-DuplexBench (2607.29125) Multi-turn, multilingual, multidomain duplex Timing and content jointly, in English and Japanese, over casual conversation and multi-turn question answering from a common source corpus; an optional reference-conditioned setting diagnoses sensitivity to dialogue history. The abstract gives no numbers; code is public
URO-Bench (2502.17810) End-to-end spoken dialogue: understanding, reasoning, oral conversation Basic and pro tracks of 20 test sets each, covering multilingualism, multi-round dialogue and paralinguistics. Open models do well on daily QA, trail their backbone LLMs on instruction following, show catastrophic forgetting, and stay weak on paralinguistics and audio understanding
SteerBench (in 2609.12623) Steerability of duplex models 390 spoken prompts and 1,067 human-authored binary audio and text rubrics covering tone, persona, style or accent, and speed or length
Talking Turns (ICLR 2025, 2503.01174) When to speak, interrupt, backchannel Audio foundation models sometimes miss when to speak, interrupt too aggressively and rarely backchannel; supervised turn-event model as judge; benchmarks curated from Switchboard

Reported numbers

  • TurnBench. Confirmed The VAP baseline scores end-of-turn recall 0.845, false-positive rate (FPR) 0.055, median latency 368 ms; interruption recall 0.945, FPR 0.107, median 994 ms. Humans on smooth transfers start a median 151 ms before the current turn ends.
  • DuplexSLA-Bench (arXiv 2605.20755). Reported Authors' backchannel accuracy, context-prefill setting: gpt-realtime-1.5 0.33% (semantic VAD), gemini-3.1-flash-live 40.00%, DuplexSLA 98.33%.
  • Instruct-FD (arXiv 2607.20460). Reported The authors report that the best of six full-duplex systems achieves only 64.4% adherence to turn-taking instructions.
  • TACT (arXiv 2609.27372). Reported Across 11 systems the best scores 0.47 against a human topline of 0.86, scores barely change across speaker profiles, and Spearman agreement with human judgement is 0.81 against 0.46 for binary metrics.
  • DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval (arXiv 2609.03423). Reported Following an explicit floor-management policy did not reliably mean a model could infer it from a persona, even for frontier models. Models that resolved benign instruction conflicts often failed when safety-relevant role behaviour should have overridden an explicit directive.
  • Qwen-Audio-3.1-Realtime (arXiv 2609.25176). Reported The response rate to background speech on Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 fell from 73.0% to 13.0%.
  • PersonaPlex (arXiv 2602.06053). Reported The authors report 0.170 s smooth turn-taking and 0.240 s user-interruption latency for the released 7B checkpoint, against 0.265 s and 0.257 s for Moshi.
Trap: self-reported numbers and unvalidated judges

DuplexSLA-Bench, Instruct-FD, TACT, DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval, Qwen-Audio-3.1-Realtime and PersonaPlex numbers come from the authors, and systems are measured under different conditions. LLM and audio judges need validation against human labels before their scores gate a release.

Ten proposed latency-aware and interaction-based metrics

Inference This list is my proposal, built on the benchmarks above. Every timing metric uses gold event labels and echo-cancelled agent output, never the system's own VAD.

# Metric Definition and how to compute
1 Floor-transfer offset Distribution of agent onset minus gold EOT, compared with a human baseline (anchor: 151 ms before the turn ends, TurnBench)
2 Time to first audio Gold EOT to first agent audio; report p50, p90, p95. Use gold EOT, not the VAD stop
3 Latency at fixed false-yield rate Threshold sweep; read mean latency at 5% and 10% false yield (eot-bench framing)
4 Stop latency after barge-in Gold onset to last audible agent sample on echo-cancelled output; plus the fraction of agent speech after onset
5 False-yield rate per minute Count of stops caused by backchannels, laughter or background speech, divided by conversation minutes
6 Premature-takeover rate Fraction of user mid-turn pauses in which the agent starts speaking
7 Backchannel rate and placement Rate versus human; share within a window after a prosodic boundary; Jensen-Shannon divergence of inter-event intervals
8 Overlap quality Cooperative versus competitive overlap share, and the overlap-duration distribution
9 Interrupted-thread recovery Does the agent resume or address the interrupted thread? LLM judge, validated on a human-labelled subset
10 Turn-taking naturalness MOS Human ratings of 10 s clips, paired against human-human conversation

The set separates timing from content, in line with DuplexPO, and ties latency to an operating point.

In the interview, say:

"I would never report latency alone. I would report time to first audio from gold end-of-turn at a fixed false-yield rate, stop latency after barge-in on echo-cancelled output, and false yields per minute, anchored to the human 151 ms offset. Judge-based metrics get validated on a human subset first."
