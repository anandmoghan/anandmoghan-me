Streaming inference: deadlines and first packets

A duplex model is a real-time loop, not a request-response call. It must produce a frame on a fixed clock for as long as the session lasts, even when nobody is talking. This section turns the earlier rates into engineering budgets.

The per-frame deadline

Inference Moshi (arXiv 2410.00037) runs at 12.5 Hz, so it has a hard deadline of 80 ms per frame: one temporal pass plus up to 17 depth sub-steps (the paper's K = 17 streams). A missed deadline is an audible gap, because the audio must keep flowing.

The standard check is the real-time factor (RTF), compute time divided by audio time:

\[ \text{RTF} = \frac{t_{\text{compute}}}{t_{\text{audio}}} \]

RTF below 1 is necessary and not sufficient: the worst frame must still fit in 80 ms. MiniCPM-o 4.5 reports RTF 0.21 (INT4) to 0.27 (FP16) with llama.cpp-omni on an RTX 4090 (arXiv 2604.27393). Reported That is 210 to 270 ms of compute per second of audio (derived).

LLaMA-Omni 2 (arXiv 2505.02625) shows the chunk version of the same arithmetic. Speech tokens run at 25 Hz, so each is 40 ms and a 10-token chunk is 400 ms. The authors report a 542.7 to 663.3 ms first chunk, so roughly 140 to 260 ms of overhead sits on top of the chunk (derived). Inference

The deadline can be relaxed by waking the backbone less often than once per frame. DiffuPlex (arXiv 2610.12214) predicts several future user and assistant frames in one backbone wake, using rolling masked diffusion over an 80 ms interaction interval. The authors report 1.46× (LISTEN) and 1.59× (SPEAK) deployment wall-clock speedups, and 1.61× and 1.80× for the core LM alone.

MiniCPM-o's ablation (arXiv 2604.27393) finds 1.0 s gives the best balance. The authors report that shorter chunks leave less modelling budget for control and generation, and that too-short chunks lack enough information per window, causing substantial degradation. Inference Shorter chunks cut the average wait for a decision (\( c/2 \), derived) but pay the per-step cost more often. Smaller is not automatically better.

Tool calls inside the budget

A tool call is the longest step a voice agent can add, so the sources try to overlap it with something else.

  • Stream RAG predicts tool queries in parallel with user speech, before the user finishes. The authors report QA accuracy from 11.1% to 34.2% absolute and tool-use latency down 20% on AudioCRAG (arXiv 2510.02044).
  • MoshiRAG starts retrieval after flagging a knowledge-demanding query and finishes inside the natural gap before the answer's core content arrives (arXiv 2604.12928).
  • NemotronLabs VoiceChat emits function calls on a stream parallel to agent text (arXiv 2609.21967).
  • Full-Duplex-Bench-v3 measures the end result: Gemini Live 3.1 is fastest at 4.25 s but turn-takes only 78.0% of the time, the cascade has a perfect turn-take rate and the highest latency at 10.12 s, and GPT-Realtime leads Pass@1 at 0.600 (arXiv 2604.04847).
  • First-party docs: Gemini 3.8 Live defaults to non-blocking function calls with SILENT, WHEN_IDLE and INTERRUPTED scheduling (model page); gpt-realtime-2 says higher reasoning effort can increase latency (model page).
