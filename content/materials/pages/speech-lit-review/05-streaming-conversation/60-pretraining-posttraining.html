Pre-training and post-training speech-to-speech models

Nearly every open speech-to-speech model follows one recipe. Start from a strong text LLM, add speech tokens, pretrain on ASR, TTS and interleaved speech-text, run spoken-dialogue SFT, then apply DPO or RL on the text side and sometimes the speech side. The real choice is whether the text backbone stays frozen or trains on a mixed corpus.

Every recipe below is the authors' own account in the cited paper. For the TTS-only post-training view (WER and SIM rewards, hacking), see Post-training and evaluation for speech generation. The reward details for online RL are in the next section.

Frozen backbone or mixed text: fighting forgetting

Freeze the LLM

  • Freeze-Omni and MinMo keep the text capability by construction.
  • LLaMA-Omni 2 freezes the LLM in stage II; Qwen2.5-Omni and Baichuan-Audio freeze it in stage 1.
  • Inference The cost is a ceiling on how deeply speech can shape the representations.

Train on a mixed corpus

  • Moshi keeps half the pretraining batches text-only; Kimi-Audio uses 585B text tokens beside 585B audio tokens; Step-Audio 2 mixes text and audio tokens.
  • Speech and text share one set of weights, so the mix ratio is a tuned hyperparameter.
  • An unbalanced mix is the main risk.

Confirmed Adding speech capability can degrade knowledge and reasoning even for text inputs, and more for spoken queries (arXiv 2509.14930). SALAD (arXiv 2510.13632) reports that speech-adapted LLMs underperform their text counterparts and attributes the gap to forgetting and cross-modal misalignment.

Interleaved synthetic pretraining and spoken instruction data

Interleaved pretraining (arXiv 2411.17607) synthesises interleaved speech tokens from text spans with a text-to-token model and a 12.5 Hz supervised tokenizer. It used 1T tokens including 600B synthetic interleaved. Spoken QA rose from 13% (Moshi) to 31%.

Spoken instruction data is synthetic almost everywhere: an LLM writes the dialogue and TTS renders it. Moshi uses over 20k h of synthetic speech (arXiv 2410.00037), and Step-Audio 2 adds synthesised speech-to-speech data in its cooldown (arXiv 2507.16632). Inference The risk is TTS voice and prosody leakage into the model's own speaking style; mix in real recorded turns and many TTS voices.

Preference, RL and distillation

  • DPO on text tokens. Moshi-Aligned (arXiv 2506.21463) applies length-normalised DPO to text-token probabilities only, because audio-token probabilities are unstable. It uses 93,490 pairs (27% timing, 73% content), judged by Mistral Large 2. Averaged QA moved 36.1 to 39.2 and safety 70.9 to 77.8 (Moshi-Instruct to Moshi-Aligned).
  • Talker DPO. Qwen2.5-Omni ranks preference triplets by WER and punctuation-pause error. Qwen3-Omni runs DPO on multilingual pairs in a four-stage Talker.
  • RL. Step-Audio 2 runs PPO with a binary conciseness reward on the thinking trace (60 iterations), PPO with a learned reward model (120), then GRPO for audio perception (400). Qwen3-Omni uses GSPO with rule-based and model-based rewards.
  • Semantic-channel-only preference (WavAlign, arXiv 2604.14932). The authors argue that sparse preference supervision conflicts with dense speech generation when parameters are shared. Their adaptive hybrid recipe limits preference updates to the semantic channel, anchors acoustic behaviour with explicit supervision, and adjusts the mix of signals from rollout statistics to avoid unreliable gradients. The abstract reports consistent gains in semantic quality and expressiveness across benchmarks and architectures, with no numbers. This agrees independently with Moshi-Aligned's choice to optimise text tokens only. Reported
  • Agent RL in audio (SpeechGym, arXiv 2608.26432; Qwen-Audio-3.1-Realtime, arXiv 2609.25176). SpeechGym trains voice agents with GRPO in an audio-only environment, where outcome-only reward gives too little signal and per-tool-call rewards restore it; see Bridge from GRPO on streaming ASR. Qwen-Audio-3.1-Realtime reports its response rate to background speech falling from 73.0% to 13.0% on Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5, and task success on a half-duplex speech-to-text adaptation of τ-Voice rising from 78.4% to 82.0%. Reported
  • Distillation. Qwen3-Omni distils strong to weak: off-policy, then on-policy KL against Qwen3-32B and 235B teachers.

Duplex post-training and evaluation gates

DuplexPO (arXiv 2607.07148) runs GRPO-style RL on selected windows from long human conversations. Its four-part reward (FCDR) covers turn initiation, backchanneling, yielding and regularised participation, with a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone. Reported Intelligence gains are small (Llama Questions 75.3 versus 72.0 for the SFT baseline, Table 4). The point is that timing is optimised without touching content.

Post-training also changes a model's forensic fingerprint. GenTraceBench (arXiv 2609.21738) finds DPO and GRPO mostly preserve it, while some SFT and pre-training data changes shift it; see Tracing a clip across training stages.

Proposed recipe for a full-duplex text+voice model

Inference: my recipe, assembled from the sources above
  1. Freeze a trusted text LLM; train the encoder and adaptor on ASR.
  2. Train a semantic-first 12.5 Hz supervised tokenizer plus codec; freeze after a reconstruction gate.
  3. Unfreeze the LLM with a high text fraction: interleaved synthetic speech (2411.17607) plus unlabelled speech with Whisper pseudo-labels.
  4. Spoken SFT on LLM-written dialogues rendered with many TTS voices, plus real recorded turns.
  5. DPO-LN on text tokens, with timing pairs (WavAlign makes the same split and anchors the acoustics with supervision).
  6. A small duplex head on frozen states (MinMo style), then gated GRPO duplex RL on event windows (DuplexPO style).
  7. Gates at every stage: ASR WER, text QA (strict), duplex timing.
Trap: the audio-token DPO shortcut

Applying DPO to audio-token probabilities looks natural but Moshi-Aligned found them unstable, so it optimised text tokens only, and WavAlign gives a mechanism: shared parameters let sparse preference labels fight dense audio generation. Reward signals also miss timing: the Dual-Axis Generative Reward Model (arXiv 2604.14920) argues existing metrics are superficial proxies, so it scores semantic quality and interaction timing separately.

In the interview, say:

"I would keep the text LLM frozen through alignment, unfreeze it with a high text fraction and interleaved synthetic data, then do DPO on text tokens and GRPO on duplex timing windows. Every stage is gated on ASR WER, strict text QA and timing, because speech training erodes text ability."
