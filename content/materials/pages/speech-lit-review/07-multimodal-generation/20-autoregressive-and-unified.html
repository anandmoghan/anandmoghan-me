Autoregressive and unified models

The field has moved from a discrete-versus-continuous fight to hybrids. Next-token AR dominates unified stacks, but the per-token head is increasingly diffusion or flow over continuous latents. Video is moving to chunked, causal AR with diffusion for streaming.

Discrete-token AR

  • VAR (arXiv 2404.02905): next-scale prediction. The authors report ImageNet 256 FID improving from 18.65 to 1.73 against an AR baseline, with around 20x faster inference.
  • Infinity (arXiv 2412.04431): bitwise token prediction with an infinite-vocabulary tokenizer and bitwise self-correction. The authors report GenEval 0.62 to 0.73 over SD3-Medium, and a 1024 px image in 0.8 s, 2.6x faster than SD3-Medium.
  • Emu3 (arXiv 2409.18869): one transformer trained from scratch with next-token prediction over discrete image, text and video tokens. The authors claim it surpasses SDXL and LLaVA-1.6.
  • Emu3.5 (arXiv 2510.26583): world-model framing on interleaved vision-language data, over 10T tokens, RL post-training. Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) turns token-by-token decoding into bidirectional parallel prediction, accelerating per-image inference by about 20x (authors).

Continuous-token AR with diffusion heads

  • MAR (arXiv 2406.11838, NeurIPS 2024): no vector quantisation; each token's distribution is modelled with a diffusion procedure (Diffusion Loss), including masked autoregressive variants.
  • Fluid (arXiv 2410.13863): continuous tokens give clearly better visual quality than discrete; random order beats raster on GenEval. At 10.5B: zero-shot FID 6.16 on MS-COCO 30K, GenEval 0.69.
  • NOVA (arXiv 2412.14169, ICLR 2025): non-quantised AR video, frame by frame in time and set by set within a frame; 0.6B parameters.
Bridge to speech

MELLE, DiTAR and VibeVoice are the audio analogue of MAR and Fluid: an AR model over continuous latents with a small diffusion or flow head per step. Inference The same trade-offs likely apply (no quantisation loss, but per-step sampling cost and harder RL). See Speech generation.

Chunked AR video diffusion

  • MAGI-1 (Sand AI, arXiv 2505.13211): autoregressively predicts fixed-length video chunks, trained to denoise per-chunk noise that increases monotonically over time, which supports streaming generation. The largest variant has 24B parameters and supports context lengths up to 4 million tokens (authors).
  • Self Forcing (arXiv 2506.08009, NeurIPS 2025): trains on its own rollouts with a rolling KV cache. The authors report 17 FPS with sub-second latency on one H100. Inference It is the most relevant entry for interactive avatars.
  • Joint audio-video students (Hallo-Live, arXiv 2604.23632; Omni-LiveAvatar, arXiv 2608.13602): the same chunked causal recipe applied to a joint teacher (Ovi, LTX-2), so sound and picture stream together. Figures and mechanisms are in streaming and real-time.

One related observation from the Kelix report: most VLMs pair discrete text tokens with continuous ViT features, which biases them toward understanding (arXiv 2602.09843).

Discrete diffusion language models

  • LLaDA (arXiv 2502.09992): 8B masked-diffusion LM trained from scratch; the authors report it is competitive with LLaMA3 8B in in-context learning.
  • MMaDA (arXiv 2505.15809): a multimodal diffusion language model with UniGRPO, a unified policy-gradient RL algorithm for post-training across reasoning and generation.

The convergence debate

  • Continuous tokens with a diffusion or flow head give better per-token quality (Fluid, MAR). Discrete tokens give a simpler LM interface and a single cross-entropy (Emu3, LLaDA) Inference.
  • Random-order or bidirectional-within-block generation is winning for images. Raster order is kept where causal streaming matters Inference.
  • Chunked, causal AR suits streaming video (MAGI-1, Self Forcing). Full-clip diffusion remains the offline quality leader Inference.
In the interview, say:

"For images the question is no longer discrete or continuous. It is where the diffusion sits: as a per-token head, as a chunk-level denoiser, or as a separate decoder. For a streaming avatar I would take chunked causal generation, and for speech the same logic gives an AR model with a flow head."
