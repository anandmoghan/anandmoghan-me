Tokenizers beyond speech

Across images, video and audio, the tokenizer matters more than the generator. The common design is a causal, heavily compressed latent, often regularised toward semantic features Inference. MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer (arXiv 2602.10934) is a counterexample for audio: a Transformer-only causal codec trained end to end with no pretrained encoder and no semantic distillation, which its authors report improves predictably with scale. Discrete codecs stay the default for audio LMs; continuous VAE latents stay the default for diffusion and flow.

Image and video tokenizers

  • MAGVIT-v2 (arXiv 2310.05737, ICLR 2024) uses lookup-free quantisation (LFQ). With this tokenizer, the authors show LLMs outperforming diffusion models on ImageNet and Kinetics, and title the paper "Tokenizer is Key to Visual Generation" Reported.
  • FSQ (arXiv 2309.15505) quantises each latent dimension to a small set of fixed values. The codebook is the implicit product of those sets, so the authors report no codebook collapse and no need for commitment losses or codebook reseeding Confirmed.
  • TiTok (arXiv 2406.07550) is a 1D tokenizer. It reduces a 256x256x3 image to 32 discrete tokens, with gFID 1.97 on ImageNet 256 using the same generator framework Reported.
  • The NVIDIA Cosmos tokenizer outputs either continuous latents or discrete tokens for images and video. Its README states total compression of up to 2048x (8x16x16) and running up to 12x faster than the best available tokenizers Reported.
  • Causal 3D VAEs compress time and space without peeking at future frames. Wan's VAE replaces GroupNorm with RMSNorm to preserve temporal causality, which enables a feature cache (arXiv 2503.20314) Confirmed.

FSQ in standard notation (not taken from the sources): bound each latent dimension, then round.

\[ \hat z_i = \operatorname{round}\!\left( \left\lfloor L_i / 2 \right\rfloor \tanh(z_i) \right), \qquad |\mathcal{C}| = \prod_i L_i \]

Here \( L_i \) is the number of levels in dimension \( i \), and \( |\mathcal{C}| \) is the implicit codebook size.

Semantic-aligned VAEs

A recent trend is to align the latent with, or build it directly on, vision foundation model features, then decode.

Tokenizer Idea Reported result
VA-VAE (arXiv 2501.01423) Vision-foundation-model alignment for high-dimensional tokenizers FID 1.35 on ImageNet 256 generation with LightningDiT
VFM-VAE (arXiv 2510.18457) Frozen foundation model used directly as the tokenizer encoder, with a new decoder gFID (w/o CFG) 2.22 in 80 epochs; 1.62 after 640 epochs
DINO-Tok (arXiv 2511.20565) Continuous and discrete tokenizers on a frozen DINO encoder ImageNet 256 rFID 0.28 (continuous) and 1.10 (discrete)

General audio and music codecs

Codec Type Key facts
DAC (arXiv 2306.06546) Discrete RVQ One universal model for speech, environment and music; about 90x compression of 44.1 kHz audio at 8 kbps
Stable Audio Open AE (arXiv 2407.14358) Continuous 156M-parameter autoencoder with a VAE bottleneck of latent size 64, for 44.1 kHz stereo audio
MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer (arXiv 2602.10934) Discrete, Transformer-only 1.6B parameters trained on 3M hours of general audio; CAT is learned end to end with no pretrained encoder or semantic distillation; the authors report it outperforms prior codecs across speech, sound and music over a range of bitrates and improves predictably with scale
WavTokenizer (arXiv 2408.16532, ICLR 2025) Single quantiser One quantiser with 40 or 75 tokens per second of 24 kHz audio; evaluated on speech, audio and music (ICLR 2025)
Trap

Best reconstruction is not best downstream. The DASB benchmark (arXiv 2406.14294) reports that discrete audio representations are less robust than continuous ones, and that semantic tokens generally outperform acoustic tokens.

Unified and joint audio-visual tokenizers

  • UniCodec (arXiv 2502.20067): one codec with a single codebook across speech, music and sound, using a partitioned domain-adaptive codebook and domain Mixture-of-Experts.
  • MelTok (arXiv 2510.01903): a 2D mel-spectrogram tokenizer that compresses 44.1 kHz audio into a single codebook, with a token-based vocoder.
  • EntangleCodec (arXiv 2606.02739): a unified discrete audio tokenizer with caption-aligned semantic-acoustic representations and a flow-matching diffusion decoder.
  • AVTok (arXiv 2606.30811): a 1D joint audio-video tokenizer with a shared encoder-decoder, modality-specific learnable queries and a unified codebook Reported.
  • VideoPoet (arXiv 2312.14125) is the precedent: it concatenated MAGVIT-v2 and SoundStream vocabularies into one unified vocabulary rather than learning a joint tokenizer.

Bridge to speech codecs

Speech codecs (Mimi, X-Codec2, BiCodec, CosyVoice 2 FSQ tokens, dynamic-frame-rate codecs such as FlexiCodec, predictability-aware codecs such as LLM-Codec and ReLMCodec) are covered in chapter 03, with the evaluation measures in Evaluating tokenizers. The same logic applies there: low rate and semantic distillation help the LM, continuous latents win back fidelity. Inference For a Voice team the new point is that audio-visual tokenizers may be converging on the same recipe as speech ones.

In the interview, say:

"The tokenizer sets the ceiling more than the generator does. The pattern is causal, compressed, semantically aligned latents: discrete for audio LMs, continuous with a flow head for quality."
