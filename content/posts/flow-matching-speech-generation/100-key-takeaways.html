Key Takeaways

  1. Straight training paths cut steps, not to one: the learned field averages crossing paths, so trajectories still bend.
  2. Masked infilling gives prompting for free: scoring only the hidden frames turns the reference clip into the voice condition, with no speaker encoder.
  3. Alignment and length are the hard part: phones need an aligner, padded characters converge slowly, and the large stacks hand both to an AR token model.
  4. Cut cost in order: a warped or pruned schedule with Euler is usable to about 7 steps and breaks below about 6, and past that a distilled student with guidance folded in runs several-fold faster.
  5. Retune guidance when you cut steps: the best strength shifts with step count, and weaker guidance wins at 4 steps or fewer.
  6. Benchmark where you serve: RTF does not transfer across papers, and the decoder that wins at one stream can lose at six.
  7. WER and SIM saturate, and rewards get hacked: hold evaluators fixed, keep listening tests, and use human preferences to protect prosody.
  8. Watermarks are not enough: they protect only outputs of models that apply them, which is why at least one lab withholds its weights.
