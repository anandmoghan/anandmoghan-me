From Diffusion to Flow Matching

The original diffusion sampler spends a thousand network calls on one sample. A real-time TTS system can afford tens. Several recent open and shipped TTS systems closed that gap with flow matching. It regresses a velocity along a straight path from noise to data. Voicebox, F5-TTS and CosyVoice 2 all use it and sample with classifier-free guidance.

Time runs the other way:

In DDPM, \( x_0 \) is data and noise sits at \( t = T \). In flow matching, \( x_0 \) is noise at \( t = 0 \) and \( x_1 \) is data at \( t = 1 \). Convert before mixing equations or code.

Denoising diffusion

Denoising diffusion learns to undo a fixed noising process.

The standard setup uses \( T = 1000 \) steps [2]. Sampling applies the learned reverse step once per level: a thousand sequential calls. The noise predictor is also a score model. The score \( \nabla \log p_t \) points uphill in the noisy data density. A short derivation gives \( \epsilon_\theta \approx -\sigma_t\,\nabla \log p_t \).

The SDE view and the probability flow ODE

Take \( T \to \infty \) and the noising chain becomes a stochastic differential equation (SDE). DDPM's chain is a discretisation of the variance-preserving (VP) SDE. Every such SDE also has a deterministic twin with the same marginals \( p_t(x) \). It is the probability flow ODE:

\[ \mathrm{d}x = \big[f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g(t)^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t \]

Here \( f \) is the drift and \( g \) the noise scale of the forward SDE. Same marginals means you can use any ODE solver, with error set by the step count. My read is that the ODE removes randomness, not curvature. Diffusion trajectories bend, and the conditional path can overshoot the sample and backtrack. Every bend forces the solver into short steps.

Diffusion can also regress \( x_0 \) or \( v = \alpha_t\epsilon - \sigma_t x_0 \). Noise prediction ignores data errors at pure noise, so distillation uses v- or x-prediction. This \( v \) is a derivative along an angle on a circle, not the flow-matching velocity.

Flow matching

Flow matching starts from the ODE. It picks a path from noise to data and regresses the velocity field that generates it. The field of the whole distribution is intractable, so training regresses per-sample fields. That gives the same gradient, with no ODE simulation. The straight path gives conditional flow matching. Voicebox trains with exactly this path and target [1]. Fewer than 10 steps already gives it high quality, though it defaults to 32. Time-step schedules covers where to spend the steps.

Classifier-free guidance

Every system above samples with guidance.

Settings vary: Voicebox uses \( w = 0.7 \) [1], F5-TTS \( w = 2 \) at 16 or 32 steps [3]. The cost of guidance covers serving and removal.

Rectified flow and straight paths

Rectified flow reaches the same loss from another side. It is conditional flow matching with \( \sigma_{\min} = 0 \), regressing onto \( x_1 - x_0 \). Straight paths matter because one Euler step follows a straight, constant-speed flow exactly. The evidence is on ImageNet-32 images, against a 1000-step reference solution. The straight path reaches the same error with about 60% of the solver steps [4].

The regression has a closed-form minimiser, \( v(x, t) = \mathbb{E}[x_1 - x_0 \mid x_t = x] \). That is the average direction of every training line through \( x \). Random pairs cross, the field averages them where they do, and trajectories bend. Learned flows come out straighter than diffusion, not straight.

Reflow straightens them by re-pairing. Run the trained ODE, record each noise sample with its output, and fit a new rectified flow. Those pairs come from one deterministic ODE, so their lines cross less. Straightness is the mean squared gap between velocity and the endpoint direction. The guarantee covers the best of \( K \) rounds: its straightness is bounded by a \( 1/K \) term. On images, one round gives nearly straight flows that work with one Euler step [5]. Tradeoff: each round fits the last model's outputs, so errors accumulate. The authors advise against many rounds. Few-step generation covers the alternatives.

How the two families relate

Stochastic interpolants make both families one framework. Each path comes with an ODE and a family of SDEs, all with the same marginals.

Aspect Diffusion (DDPM, VP SDE) Flow matching (OT path)
Path Variance-preserving; reaches noise only asymptotically Straight; reaches noise in finite time
Target \( \epsilon \), \( x_0 \) or \( v \) \( x_1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\,x_0 \)
Loss weighting Set by the noise schedule and the target None in the loss
Sampler Reverse SDE or probability flow ODE ODE
Trajectories Curved; can overshoot Less curved

Tradeoff: the straight path buys fewer steps and a target with a stable scale. It gives up the stochastic sampler, which plain flow matching does not have. Stochastic interpolants restore one, but it needs the score as a second learned field. Several recent open and shipped TTS systems take the deterministic side of that trade. Guidance, solvers and distillation built for diffusion carry over to it.
