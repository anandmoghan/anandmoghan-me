Introduction

PaLM 540B hit about 20 loss spikes even with gradient clipping on. Each time, the team restarted from a checkpoint about 100 steps before the spike and skipped 200 to 500 batches of data [1]. Three years later Kimi K2 reports 15.5T tokens of pretraining without a single loss spike [2]. K2 differs from PaLM in architecture and optimizer as well as recipe, so this is not a like-for-like comparison. Still, my read is that stability, more than compute, now sets how long a run can go and how high its learning rate can sit, because every spike costs rolled-back steps, lost tokens and someone watching the loss curve.

This post starts with how long runs fail and the signals that move before the loss does. It then covers the fixes labs ship today, from the transformer block and the optimizer to schedules, low precision and routing, and ends with the operations side of a job on thousands of GPUs. None of these fixes is free. Each one costs throughput, adds a hyperparameter to tune, or constrains the inference architecture, and I name the cost next to each fix.
