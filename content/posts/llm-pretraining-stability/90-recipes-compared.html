Recipes compared

The table lines up the runs that shaped today's recipe, from PaLM's hand-managed spikes to recent runs. Each row lists what the team says it did for stability and what went wrong or stood out.

Run Ref Stability choices What went wrong or stood out
PaLM 540B [1] Z-loss on output logits, no biases, clipping at 1.0, step-indexed deterministic data About 20 spikes despite clipping; restart and skip batches; replayed batches did not spike
OPT-175B [7] Dynamic loss scaling, clipping cut from 1.0 to 0.3, Pile subsets dropped for spiking Divergences tracked a crashing loss scale; fixed by rewinding and lowering the learning rate
GLM-130B [11] DeepNorm post-LN, embedding gradient shrink, fp16 kept for hardware portability Pre-LN and Sandwich-LN crashed in trials; embedding gradients far larger than other layers
Llama 3 405B [18] Low peak learning rate, long warmup, batch ramp, fp32 gradient accumulation Few spikes, no divergence interventions; hardware dominated the interruptions
OLMo 2 [10] QK-norm with reordered norm, z-loss, plain small init, epsilon 1e-8, no decay on embeddings, n-gram filter QK-norm and reordered norm helped only together; two z-loss implementations agreed forward but differed backward, forcing a retrain
Gemma 2 [13] Tanh soft-capping on attention and final logits, RMSNorm before and after each sub-layer No spike story published; soft-capping was gone one generation later
Gemma 3 [14] QK-norm replaces soft-capping; pre and post norm kept Cites ViT-22B, small-scale proxies and Chameleon as motivation [4] [5] [6]
Qwen3 [28] QK-norm added and QKV bias removed; global-batch balance loss for the MoE models QK-norm added "to ensure stable training"; no spike story
DeepSeek-V3 [16] FP8 with fine-grained scaling and high-precision islands, bias-based load balancing, AdamW beta2 0.95 No irrecoverable spikes and no rollbacks over 14.8T tokens
Kimi K2 [2] Muon with per-head QK-Clip; MLA rules out QK-norm Vanilla Muon logits passed 1,000 at mid scale; no loss spikes reported over 15.5T tokens
DeepSeek-V4 [17] Muon for most weights, AdamW elsewhere, RMSNorm on queries and KV entries instead of QK-Clip Spikes from MoE-layer outliers; rollbacks alone failed, fixed with anticipatory routing and SwiGLU clamping
Marin 32B [12] Tighter clipping, update-norm clipping, step skipping, then a warm start into a QK-norm architecture Heuristics only softened persistent spikes; the architecture switch removed them

A few choices are common in recent runs: pre-norm RMSNorm blocks, QK-norm wherever the attention design allows it, AdamW with a lowered beta2, small plain initialisation, and bf16 compute with fp32 islands for accumulation and master weights. They are not universal, as the table shows: Llama 3 stayed stable without crediting any of the architectural fixes, GLM-130B went with post-LN, and DeepSeek-V3 trained in FP8. My read is that they spread because each fixes a named failure at a small, predictable cost, but the cost is real: QK-norm does not fit MLA, and the fp32 islands spend memory and bandwidth. The lab-specific choices each answer a constraint the others do not have. QK-Clip exists because MLA cannot take QK-norm and Muon grows logits faster; fine-grained FP8 scaling and bias-based balancing come from DeepSeek's push on MoE efficiency; soft-capping lasted one Gemma generation. Z-loss stays lab-specific, used by PaLM, Chameleon, OLMo 2 and Marin 8B, which added it after its output head blew up in cooldown [8]; removing weight decay from embeddings is mostly an OLMo 2 choice. Llama 3 shows that a low learning rate, long warmup and batch ramp can carry a large dense run with few spikes; Marin 32B, with Llama-style settings, shows that they do not always.
