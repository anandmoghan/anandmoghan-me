Architecture-level stability fixes in LLM pretraining

Research note, 2026-10-14. Which architecture changes keep long, large runs from diverging, what they cost, and which named models report them.

How to read this note

  • confirmed The primary source was read in full text for this note.
  • reported Secondary source only (blog, news roundup, or a citation inside another paper).
  • inference My own reasoning, not stated by a source.

Costs that no source quantifies are marked as inference and carry no numbers.

The two failure modes the fixes target

  • Attention logit growth: query/key norms grow, logits get large, softmax weights become near one-hot (near-zero entropy), and the loss diverges. Seen at about 8B parameters in a ViT. Source: Scaling Vision Transformers to 22 Billion Parameters, arXiv 2302.05442. confirmed
  • Output logit divergence: the final softmax normalizer drifts away from zero in log space. Source: Small-scale proxies for large-scale Transformer training instabilities, arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Small models can show attention logit growth too, so it is not only a large-scale problem. The authors hypothesise a quadratic dependence of logits on parameter norms. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Softmax is translation invariant, so with weights shared across modalities each modality can inflate norms to "compete". Chameleon saw divergences after up to 20-30% of training and found that growing output norms predicted them. Source: Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models, arXiv 2405.09818. confirmed
  • PaLM 540B had about 20 loss spikes despite gradient clipping, at irregular intervals, and not on smaller models. The mitigation was operational, not architectural: restart from a checkpoint about 100 steps before the spike and skip roughly 200 to 500 batches. Re-running the same batches from an earlier checkpoint did not spike, so the cause is a data-batch and parameter-state combination, not bad data alone. Source: PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways, arXiv 2204.02311, section 5.1. confirmed

QK-norm

What it fixes

  • LayerNorm (or RMSNorm) on queries and keys before the dot product, so logit magnitude is bounded by construction. In ViT-22B it prevented divergence in an 8B-parameter model. Source: arXiv 2302.05442, which credits Gilmer et al. (2023) for the approach. confirmed
  • Without it, the learning rate at which models diverge shrinks as model size grows. With it, LR sensitivity is considerably lower, and a 1.2B model trained at learning rate 0.3. Loss diverged without it at two scales three orders of magnitude apart. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • LR sensitivity still increases with scale with or without QK-norm, so it narrows the problem and does not remove it. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • It does not fix the final softmax: Chameleon still needed z-loss on top. Source: arXiv 2405.09818. confirmed

Cost

  • Two extra normalizations per attention layer; small FLOPs, but they are extra memory-bound ops on the critical path. No source in this note quantifies the throughput cost. inference
  • Incompatible with multi-head latent attention (MLA) as used by DeepSeek-style models, because MLA keys are not fully materialized at inference. Moonshot cites this as the reason it built QK-Clip instead. Source: Kimi K2 technical report, arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed
  • In OLMo 2 ablations, QK-norm and reordered norm each gave poor results in isolation but improved gradient norm growth and spikiness together. Source: 2 OLMo 2 Furious, arXiv 2501.00656, section 3.3.2. confirmed

Models that report it

  • ViT-22B (arXiv 2302.05442). confirmed
  • Chameleon 7B and 34B; the model table lists QK-norm for both, and 7B diverged after about 20% of an epoch without it (arXiv 2405.09818). confirmed
  • OLMo 2, replacing OLMo-0424's "clip QKV to 8" (arXiv 2501.00656, Table 1). confirmed
  • Gemma 3, which says it replaces Gemma 2 soft-capping with QK-norm (arXiv 2503.19786). confirmed
  • Qwen3: "we remove QKV-bias used in Qwen2 and introduce QK-Norm to the attention mechanism to ensure stable training" (arXiv 2505.09388). confirmed
  • DBRX is cited by OLMo for QKV clipping, a different mechanism (arXiv 2501.00656). reported

Logit soft-capping and QK-Clip

  • Gemma 2 caps logits with soft_cap * tanh(logits / soft_cap), using 50.0 for attention layers and 30.0 for the final layer. Source: Gemma 2, arXiv 2408.00118. confirmed
  • Gemma 3 states it replaces soft-capping with QK-norm, "inspired by" ViT-22B, Small-scale proxies and Chameleon. The report gives no measured reason for the swap. Source: Gemma 3 Technical Report, arXiv 2503.19786. confirmed
  • Moonshot's argument against soft-capping: the cap acts after the query-key dot products, which can still grow excessively before capping is applied. Source: arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed
  • Plausible additional reason for Gemma 3's swap is kernel compatibility, since a tanh inside the softmax input complicates fused attention kernels. Not stated in the Gemma reports. inference
  • QK-Clip (Kimi K2): after each optimizer step, rescale W_q and W_k of any head whose max logit exceeds a threshold tau; forward and backward of the current step are untouched. Applied per head, because only a small subset of heads explode. For MLA it scales the head-specific q and k components, and leaves the shared rotary key alone. Source: arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed
  • Motivation: attention logit explosion happens more with Muon than with AdamW; a mid-scale Muon run exceeded logits of 1000. Kimi K2 with tau = 100 pre-trained on 15.5T tokens "without a single loss spike", and max logits decayed to a stable range after about 30% of training steps. Source: arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed
  • Cost: a max-logit statistic per head must be computed each step, and it is a training-only fix, so inference is unchanged. inference

z-loss on output logits

  • PaLM adds an auxiliary loss of 1e-4 * log^2(Z), where Z is the softmax normalizer, to push log Z toward 0. It reports this "increases the stability of training". Source: arXiv 2204.02311. confirmed
  • Small-scale proxies shows output logit divergence and that z-loss at coefficient 1e-4 mitigates it. With qk-layernorm and z-loss, models tolerate weight decay, warmup and muP changes without changing stability much, and mainly change LR sensitivity. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Chameleon uses 1e-5 times log^2 Z; z-loss alone sufficed for the 34B model together with QK-norm and norm reordering, while the 7B needed dropout as well. Source: arXiv 2405.09818. confirmed
  • OLMo 2 adopts z-loss, citing PaLM, Chameleon and Wortsman et al. Source: arXiv 2501.00656. confirmed
  • Implementation hazard: OLMo found the FlashAttention library's fused cross-entropy z-loss had different backward behaviour from a plain PyTorch implementation, suspected to be a precision difference. They abandoned the fused fork and re-trained from the point of divergence, and avoid switching implementations mid-run. Source: arXiv 2501.00656, section 3.3.3. confirmed
  • Cost: one extra term on logits you already have; negligible compute. It biases the objective slightly, which the sources do not report as harmful. inference

Pre-norm, post-norm, sandwich and reordered norm

  • Pre-LN is the de facto GPT-style standard because it trains more stably than original post-LN when stacking many layers. Source: Spike No More, arXiv 2312.16903, citing Xiong et al. (2020). confirmed
  • Pre-LN gradients at lower layers are larger than at higher ones, and it can underperform post-LN; DeepNorm was proposed to get post-LN quality with pre-LN stability. Source: DeepNet, arXiv 2203.00555. confirmed
  • Gemma 2 and Gemma 3 normalize both the input and the output of each sub-layer with RMSNorm ("pre and post norm", a sandwich-style layout). Gemma 2 says it does this "to stabilize training". Sources: arXiv 2408.00118, arXiv 2503.19786. confirmed
  • OLMo 2 normalizes only the outputs of attention and MLP before the residual add: h = x + RMSNorm(Attn(x)). It attributes the idea to Swin Transformer V2 (Liu et al.) and notes Chameleon picked it up. Source: arXiv 2501.00656. confirmed
  • OLMo ablation: reorder plus QK-norm reduced the gradient spike score from 0.108 to 0.069. Source: arXiv 2501.00656, Figure 7. confirmed
  • Chameleon 34B needed the reorder on top of QK-norm. Reason given: it bounds the norm growth of the feed-forward block, which is worse with the multiplicative SwiGLU. No perplexity difference from reordering until the Llama-2 layout diverged. Reordering worked poorly with dropout, so 34B dropped dropout. Source: arXiv 2405.09818. confirmed
  • Swin V2 introduced res-post-norm because activation values grew sharply in deeper layers of the pre-norm design. Source: Swin Transformer V2, arXiv 2111.09883. confirmed
  • Sandwich-LN (CogView, arXiv 2105.13290) did not stabilize GLM-130B test runs; DeepNorm did. Source: GLM-130B, arXiv 2210.02414. confirmed
  • In the Gated Attention study, adding sandwich norm to a baseline restored convergence at higher learning rate but gave negligible improvement. Source: arXiv 2505.06708. confirmed
  • Cost: extra norm ops per layer (post-norm and sandwich add one per sub-layer); no inference change beyond those ops. Quantified costs are not given in the sources. inference

RMSNorm and removing biases

  • PaLM: no biases in dense kernels or layer norms, "found to result in increased training stability for large models". Source: arXiv 2204.02311. confirmed
  • ViT-22B followed PaLM for QKV projections and LayerNorms (no bias, no centering), which improved accelerator utilization by 3% without quality loss. Unlike PaLM it kept biases on the MLP dense layers because it saw better quality and no speed cost. Source: arXiv 2302.05442. confirmed
  • OLMo 2: biases none, RMSNorm without bias replacing non-parametric LayerNorm. Ablations showed no difference between the two norms; the switch was for safety and because library bugs were fixed. Source: arXiv 2501.00656. confirmed
  • Qwen3 removed the QKV bias that Qwen2 used and uses RMSNorm with pre-normalization. Source: arXiv 2505.09388. confirmed
  • RMSNorm itself is mostly an efficiency and simplicity choice; the stability claims attach to bias removal in PaLM only. Whether RMSNorm is stabilizing by itself is not shown in these sources. inference

Embeddings: scaling, norm, weight decay, tying, gradient shrink

Scaling and embedding norm

  • Spike No More: stable pre-training needs small sub-layer parameters and embedding standard deviation close to 1 (large shortcut). Modern implementations dropped the original Transformer's embedding scaling. Fixes: scale embeddings by sqrt(d) ("Scaled Embed") or apply LayerNorm to embeddings ("Embed LN"). Both avoided spikes in their experiments, while the vanilla small-init model spiked. Source: arXiv 2312.16903. confirmed
  • That paper's small-init baseline uses sigma = sqrt(2/(5d)); it also reports that Scaled Embed permits a comparatively larger learning rate and better results. Source: arXiv 2312.16903. confirmed
  • BLOOM's embedding LayerNorm is cited there as prior art (Le Scao et al. 2022). Source: arXiv 2312.16903. reported
  • PaLM initializes input embeddings from N(0, 1) because no layer norm is applied to them, and scales pre-softmax logits by 1/sqrt(n) since embeddings are shared. Source: arXiv 2204.02311. confirmed
  • Cost of Scaled Embed: one constant multiply, effectively free. Embed LN adds one norm. inference

No weight decay on embeddings

  • OLMo 2: weight decay on token embeddings drives their norm down, and with a layer norm after them small embeddings produce large gradients in early layers (Takase et al. analysis). Excluding embeddings from weight decay let norms settle in a healthy range. Remedies from Takase et al. slowed convergence, so they took the simple route. Source: arXiv 2501.00656, section 3.4.2. confirmed
  • The paper describes the effect of decaying embeddings as "modest" on stability, so this is a smaller lever than the init or norm changes. Source: arXiv 2501.00656, Figure 10 caption. confirmed

Embedding gradient shrink

  • GLM-130B: gradient norm spikes preceded collapses by a few steps and came from abnormal embedding-layer gradients, several orders of magnitude larger than other layers early on. Shrinking the embedding gradient with alpha = 0.1 removed most spikes, with negligible latency. Originally from CogView. Source: arXiv 2210.02414. confirmed
  • The final run still had three late-stage loss divergences, handled by shrinking the embedding gradient further. Source: arXiv 2210.02414. confirmed

Tied vs untied

  • Tied: PaLM (shared input-output embeddings), all Gemma 2 sizes (2B, 9B, 27B), and the small Qwen3 models (0.6B, 1.7B list "Tie Embedding: Yes"). Sources: arXiv 2204.02311, arXiv 2408.00118, arXiv 2505.09388. confirmed
  • Tying saves parameters, which matters most with a 256k vocabulary at small size. None of the sources I read present tying as a stability fix; it is a parameter-budget choice, and tying is what forces PaLM's 1/sqrt(n) logit scaling. inference

Initialization

  • OLMo 2 moved to a single N(0, 0.02) for every parameter, from OLMo-0424's depth-scaled scheme that shrank later layers (output projections scaled by roughly 1/sqrt(2 * d_model * layer_idx)). The new scheme better preserved activation and gradient scale across layers and showed hyperparameter-transfer properties across widths. In a short-warmup stress test the new init had no loss spikes, and gradient-norm spike score fell from 0.40 to 0.03. Cost: converges slightly slower, recovered by other hyperparameters. Source: arXiv 2501.00656, section 3.2. confirmed
  • PaLM: fan-in variance scaling for kernels, N(0, 1) for input embeddings. Source: arXiv 2204.02311. confirmed
  • Spike No More: small sub-layer init (the widely used GPT-style scheme satisfies it) plus unit-scale embeddings; methods meeting both conditions avoided spikes, while some previously recommended fixes that miss a condition still spiked. Source: arXiv 2312.16903. confirmed
  • DeepNorm: post-LN with residual scaled up, LayerNorm(alpha * x + Network(x)), plus down-scaled init of selected weights; DeepNet reports training to over 1000 layers. GLM-130B used alpha = (2N)^(1/2), Xavier normal init scaled by (2N)^(-1/2) on ffn, v_proj and out_proj, and zero biases. Sources: arXiv 2203.00555, arXiv 2210.02414. confirmed
  • GLM-130B's "Lesson 3": DeepNorm, a type of Post-LN, was the option that stabilized it, after more than 30 failed preliminary 100B-scale trials with other layouts. Source: arXiv 2210.02414. confirmed
  • I did not find DeepNorm reported by a frontier open model after GLM-130B in the sources read; pre-norm plus QK-norm and z-loss appears to have won in practice. inference

Parallel attention and MLP blocks

  • Formulation: y = x + MLP(LN(x)) + Attn(LN(x)). PaLM reports about 15% faster training at large scale because the MLP and attention input matmuls fuse. Ablation: small quality degradation at 8B, none at 62B; they extrapolated quality neutrality at 540B. Source: arXiv 2204.02311. confirmed
  • ViT-22B uses parallel layers for the same fusion reason and cites PaLM's 15% figure. Source: arXiv 2302.05442. confirmed
  • This is an efficiency change, not a stability fix; neither source claims stability gains from it. Newer models in this note (OLMo 2, Qwen3, Gemma 2 and 3) use sequential blocks. confirmed

Gated attention and attention sinks

  • Gated Attention for Large Language Models (Qwen, arXiv 2505.06708): compared 30 variants of 15B MoE models and 1.7B dense models on a 3.5T-token dataset. A head-specific sigmoid gate after scaled dot-product attention (G1) works best: up to 0.2 PPL reduction and 2 MMLU points. confirmed
  • Stability claim in the paper's words: it "nearly eliminates loss spikes", tolerates larger learning rates and batch sizes, and improves scaling. Baselines hit convergence issues at higher max LR; gated models improved instead. confirmed
  • Attention sink: the baseline sent on average 46.7% of attention scores to the first token across layers, and 83% in layer 21; the gated models show no sink. Gating also lowers massive activations, and gave over 10 points on RULER length generalization. Gating only after the value projection lowers massive activations but not the first-token attention, so massive activations are not a prerequisite for sinks. confirmed
  • Cost: wall-time latency from gating is under 2% and the parameter and FLOP additions are small; FFN width was reduced to keep parameter count matched. confirmed
  • Caveat on stability evidence: the loss-spike comparison is a single 1.7B dense run pair over 3.5T tokens (Figure 1), not a many-seed study. inference
  • Qwen3-Next uses the output gate in its full-attention layers alongside Gated DeltaNet in a 3:1 layout, with a zero-centered QK-norm variant. The Qwen account quoted in secondary roundups says it helps eliminate attention sink and massive activation, and that weight decay is applied to norm weights. Sources: Raschka's LLM architecture gallery (sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/gated-attention/), smol.ai roundup (news.smol.ai/issues/25-09-11-qwen3-next). reported
  • Attention sinks were first framed in Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks (arXiv 2309.17453). reported

Differential Transformer and other proposals

  • Differential attention subtracts two softmax attention maps, with headwise GroupNorm and a fixed multiplier (1 - lambda_init) after it to align gradient flow with a standard Transformer. Source: Differential Transformer, arXiv 2410.05258. confirmed
  • Its stability-adjacent claim is fewer activation outliers (lower top activations in attention logits and hidden states than a baseline Transformer), framed as a quantization benefit. It does not claim fewer loss spikes in the text I checked. Source: arXiv 2410.05258, section 3.7. confirmed
  • The GroupNorm ablation in that paper is a quality ablation, not a stability one. inference
  • Other stability levers outside this note's scope but seen in the sources: dropout (Chameleon 7B), FP32 gradient accumulation (Llama 3), data filtering of repeated n-grams (OLMo 2), embedding gradient shrink (GLM-130B), optimizer-side QK-Clip (Kimi K2). Sources: arXiv 2405.09818, arXiv 2407.21783, arXiv 2501.00656, arXiv 2210.02414, arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed

Open questions and conflicting sources

  • OLMo 2's z-loss weight conflicts inside the same paper: Table 1 lists 10^-5, while section 3.3.3 and the summary text describe adding 10^-4 times log^2 Z. Check the released config before quoting a value. Source: arXiv 2501.00656. confirmed
  • Why Gemma 3 dropped soft-capping is not explained in the report; only the inspiration for QK-norm is cited. Moonshot's critique (cap applies after the dot product) and kernel compatibility are plausible, not sourced to Google. inference
  • Gemma 3 lists "post-norm and pre-norm", while OLMo 2 uses output-only norm; Chameleon says its layout follows Swin V2. These are different layouts that all get called "reordered" or "sandwich" in the literature, so the post should name the exact formula. inference
  • The OLMo 2 init change was tested under a deliberately short warmup to reproduce spikes; transfer of that result to a normal warmup is not shown. Source: arXiv 2501.00656. confirmed
  • OLMo 2 says QK-norm and reordered norm alone each gave poor results, while Chameleon says QK-norm was essential and reordering was needed only for 34B. So the interaction between the two is not settled across models. Sources: arXiv 2501.00656, arXiv 2405.09818. confirmed
  • Small-scale proxies reports qk-layernorm reduces LR sensitivity but sensitivity still grows with scale, so no source here shows that a given fix is sufficient at 1T+ tokens or frontier scale. inference
  • Not verified: whether Llama 3 or DeepSeek-V3 use QK-norm. A keyword search of the Llama 3 report text found no QK-norm mention, and I did not read DeepSeek-V3's architecture section for it. inference
  • Not verified from primary sources: Qwen3-Next gate details (secondary only), and the exact first-token attention drop figure quoted by blogs. The paper's own numbers (46.7% baseline) are in the gated attention section above. reported
  • Throughput costs of QK-norm, sandwich norm and soft-capping are not quantified in any source read; do not state numbers for them in the post. inference
