10-introduction.html: Introduction (about 200 words)

Stability, not compute, is what limits how long and how hot a pretraining run can go. PaLM restarted around its loss spikes by hand; Kimi K2 reports none over 15.5T tokens. The post explains what changed between those two runs.

  • No subsections.

Notes: failure-modes.html, lab-recipes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: Every fix costs something (throughput, a hyperparameter, an inference constraint); say so up front.

20-how-runs-fail.html: How long runs fail (about 950 words)

Spikes and slow divergence have a handful of mechanisms, and each has a signal that shows up before the loss does.

  • Attention logit growth
  • Output logit drift
  • When Adam's statistics go stale
  • Data and model state together

Notes: failure-modes.html, optimizers-schedules.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: Recovering from a spike (rollback, skip batches) works but hides the cause; spikes come back at larger scale.

Diagram: Anatomy of a spike: timeline with grad norm rising first, then max attention logit, then loss; rollback point marked.

30-architecture.html: Fixes in the architecture (about 1150 words)

Most of today's stability comes from a few cheap changes to the block: normalise queries and keys, keep output logits small, place norms where they bound each sub-layer, and treat embeddings carefully.

  • QK-norm and the end of soft-capping
  • Z-loss on the output
  • Where the norms go
  • Embeddings and initialisation
  • Gated attention

Notes: architecture-fixes.html, lab-recipes.html, failure-modes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: QK-norm does not fit MLA (why Kimi K2 needed QK-Clip); soft-capping fights fused attention kernels; reordered norms change residual-stream scale.

Diagram: Transformer block annotated with where QK-norm, post-sublayer norm, z-loss and the attention gate act.

40-optimizer.html: Optimizer settings that keep AdamW steady (about 1000 words)

AdamW with beta2 0.95, clipping at 1.0, small epsilon and selective weight decay is the default, and each setting addresses a named failure.

  • Beta2, epsilon and update clipping
  • Warmup, clipping and weight decay
  • Transferring hyperparameters across scale

Notes: optimizers-schedules.html, failure-modes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: Lower epsilon helps at scale but underflows in low precision; muP reduces tuning risk but few labs say they use it in the final run.

50-muon-and-qk-clip.html: Muon and QK-Clip (about 800 words)

Muon is the first optimizer change in years that frontier labs ship, and it brought its own instability, attention logit explosion, plus its own fix.

  • What Muon changes
  • Logit explosion and QK-Clip
  • How much Muon actually buys

Notes: optimizers-schedules.html, lab-recipes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: Gains shrink against a well-tuned AdamW as models grow; QK-Clip is a post-step weight edit, not a loss term.

60-schedules.html: Schedules that let a run keep going (about 700 words)

Warmup-stable-decay and batch-size ramps turned the learning-rate schedule from a fixed contract into something a lab can extend mid-run.

  • Warmup-stable-decay
  • Ramping the batch size

Notes: optimizers-schedules.html, lab-recipes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: A long constant phase needs a decay to see the real loss, so mid-run evaluation needs a cooldown branch.

Diagram: Learning-rate curves: cosine versus warmup-stable-decay, with a branch-and-cooldown from a stable-phase checkpoint.

70-precision-and-moe.html: Low precision and mixture-of-experts (about 950 words)

Lower precision and sparse experts each add instabilities the dense bf16 recipe never had; the fixes are scaling granularity, high-precision islands and router control.

  • From fp16 to FP8
  • Outliers and FP4
  • Routers and load balancing

Notes: precision-moe-systems.html, lab-recipes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: Fine-grained scaling costs kernel complexity; keeping blocks in bf16 costs throughput; balancing losses cost quality.

80-running-a-long-job.html: Running a long job (about 800 words)

Past a few thousand GPUs, the run is an operations problem: what to monitor, when to roll back, how to clean data that triggers spikes, and how to survive hardware faults.

  • What to watch
  • Rollback, skip and data filters
  • Hardware faults and silent corruption

Notes: failure-modes.html, precision-moe-systems.html, lab-recipes.html.

Trade-off or failure mode: Skipping data and rolling back cost tokens and determinism; Marin 32B shows that sometimes the only fix is an architecture change mid-run.
