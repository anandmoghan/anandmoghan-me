Optimizers, hyperparameters and schedules for stable pretraining

Research note for the LLM pretraining stability post. Fetched 2026-10-14 from arXiv PDFs and first-party pages.

How to read this note

  • confirmed means I read the statement in the cited primary source (paper text or model card).
  • reported means a secondary page or an abstract-level summary states it and I did not see the primary text.
  • inference means my own reading across sources, not a claim any source makes.
  • Numbers appear only where the cited source states them.

AdamW beta2 and epsilon

Beta2 and the stale second moment

  • Loss spikes tend to occur 1-8 iterations after AdamW's second-moment estimator underestimates the squared gradients. Source: Wortsman et al., Stable and low-precision training for large-scale vision-language models, arXiv 2304.13013. confirmed
  • The paper tracks \( \mathrm{RMS}_t = \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[g_t^2 / u_t]} \) per tensor. If \( u_t \) is a good estimator of \( g_t^2 \) it stays near 1; "stuck in the past" corresponds to \( \mathrm{RMS}_t \gg 1 \). Source: arXiv 2304.13013, Section 3.4. confirmed
  • Spikes grow with model size, batch size and learning rate at fixed other settings, and lowering beta2 from the PyTorch default 0.999 mitigates them; lowering it too far slows training. The sweep shows 0.99, 0.98, 0.95, 0.9 and 0.5. Source: arXiv 2304.13013, Figures 6-8. confirmed
  • The instability appeared at high beta2 even with 5k iterations of warmup. Source: arXiv 2304.13013, Section 3.5. confirmed
  • DeepSeek-V3 uses AdamW with beta1 = 0.9, beta2 = 0.95, weight decay 0.1. DeepSeek-V4 uses the same beta2 and weight decay for its AdamW parameters. Sources: DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report, arXiv 2412.19437; DeepSeek-V4, arXiv 2606.19348 Section 4.2.2. confirmed
  • Wortsman et al. (2023) small-scale proxy study defaults: beta2 = 0.95, epsilon 1e-8, global-norm gradient clipping at 1. Source: Small-scale proxies for large-scale Transformer training instabilities, arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Beta2 interacts with batch size: Marek et al. propose holding the second-moment half-life fixed in tokens rather than holding the decay rate fixed across batch sizes, and report stable training down to batch size one. Source: Small Batch Size Training for Language Models, arXiv 2507.07101. confirmed
  • Reading: 0.95 is the common frontier choice because it shortens the second-moment memory, which matters most when gradient scale changes quickly (early training, large LR, MoE routing shifts). inference

Epsilon

  • OLMo 2 lowered AdamW epsilon from 1e-5 to 1e-8. It states 1e-8 is the PyTorch default while some LM codebases default to 1e-5, and reports that the lower value allows larger early updates and the gradient norm settles faster and stays lower. Source: 2 OLMo 2 Furious, arXiv 2501.00656, Section 3.4.1. confirmed
  • Everett et al. state that epsilon must be scaled correctly to avoid gradient underflow, and propose Adam-atan2, a scale-invariant Adam that removes epsilon. Source: Scaling Exponents Across Parameterizations and Optimizers, arXiv 2407.05872. confirmed
  • DeepSeek-V4 sets AdamW epsilon to 1e-20. Source: arXiv 2606.19348 Section 4.2.2. confirmed
  • The OLMo 2 and DeepSeek-V4 epsilons point the same way (smaller than the old default) but I found no source that explains the 1e-20 choice. inference

Update clipping: Adafactor and StableAdamW

  • Adafactor divides the learning rate at step t by \( \max(\mathrm{RMS}_t, 1) \) ("update clipping"), which slows training exactly when the second-moment estimate is out of date. Source: arXiv 2304.13013, Section 3.5, crediting Shazeer and Stern. confirmed
  • StableAdamW ports this to AdamW with threshold d = 1. The abstract says the AdamW-Adafactor hybrid avoids loss spikes training CLIP ViT-Huge and outperforms gradient clipping at the scales tested. Source: arXiv 2304.13013 (abstract and Algorithm 2). confirmed
  • In Shazeer and Stern's setting the instability appeared without warmup; Wortsman et al. saw it with 5k warmup iterations when beta2 was high. Source: arXiv 2304.13013 Section 3.5. confirmed
  • Cost: one RMS reduction per tensor and a per-tensor LR scale. The paper does not give a throughput cost. inference
  • I found no frontier-lab report that uses StableAdamW; production reports I read use lower beta2 plus global clipping instead. inference
  • QK-Clip in Kimi K2 is a different kind of clipping: it rescales query and key weights after the update, not the optimizer step (see the Muon section). Source: Kimi K2, arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed

Gradient clipping practice

  • DeepSeek-V3 sets the gradient clipping norm to 1.0. Source: arXiv 2412.19437 Section 4.1. confirmed
  • The small-scale proxies study uses global-norm clipping at 1 by default. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Wortsman et al. observed CLIP-style training where gradient clipping did not fix the stale-second-moment spikes that update clipping did. Source: arXiv 2304.13013 (abstract: outperforms gradient clipping). confirmed
  • OLMo-0424 had frequent loss spikes, often preceded by gradient-norm spikes, and a slow upward drift of gradient norm over the run. OLMo 2 fixed it with initialisation, QK-norm and reordered norms, z-loss, epsilon, n-gram data filtering and no weight decay on embeddings, not with clipping alone. Source: arXiv 2501.00656 Sections 3.1-3.4. confirmed
  • Llama 3 405B reports "few loss spikes" and no interventions needed; the paper does not list gradient clipping in the pretraining recipe I read. Source: The Llama 3 Herd of Models, arXiv 2407.21783 Section 3.4.1. confirmed
  • Reading: clipping is a cheap safety net (norm 1.0 is the common value) but it addresses outlier batches, not the structural causes (stale second moment, logit growth). inference

Warmup length

  • Kalra and Barkeshli: the overwhelming benefit of warmup is that it lets the network tolerate a larger target learning rate by moving it to better-conditioned regions of the loss landscape; they also suggest an Adam variance initialisation that gives similar benefits. Source: Why Warmup the Learning Rate?, arXiv 2406.09405. confirmed
  • Wortsman et al. find longer warmup reduces learning-rate sensitivity, as does independent scaling of LR and weight decay. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Warmup lengths in recent reports: Llama 3 405B 8,000 steps (peak LR 8e-5); DeepSeek-V3 2K steps (to 2.2e-4); Kimi K2 500 steps (to 2e-4); DeepSeek-V4 2000 steps (2.7e-4 Flash, 2.0e-4 Pro); Llama 3 smaller-scale runs 2,000 steps. Sources: arXiv 2407.21783 Sections 3.2 and 3.4.1; arXiv 2412.19437; arXiv 2507.20534; arXiv 2606.19348. confirmed
  • Reading: warmup is short in steps for the largest MoE runs because batch sizes are huge, so a few thousand steps already span many billions of tokens. inference

Weight decay and its interaction with learning rate

  • Wang and Aitchison: AdamW's weights behave like an EMA of recent updates, so the EMA timescale (in epochs) is the quantity to tune. For fixed LR each timescale maps to one weight decay; the best timescale is roughly constant across model and dataset size, so optimal weight decay should fall as data grows and rise as the model grows (under muP). Validated on ResNet-18, ViT (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) and NanoGPT on OpenWebText only. Source: How to set AdamW's weight decay as you scale model and dataset size, arXiv 2405.13698. confirmed
  • The same paper states muP's learning-rate scaling breaks down for AdamW later in training unless weight decay is scaled appropriately. Source: arXiv 2405.13698. confirmed
  • Independent (decoupled) weight decay, as in AdamW, keeps decay from being scaled by the Adam preconditioner. Wortsman et al. use an independent weight decay of 1e-4 in their proxy study and cite independent scaling of LR and weight decay as reducing LR sensitivity. Sources: Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization (Loshchilov and Hutter, arXiv 1711.05101, cited, not re-read); arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • OLMo 2 turned off weight decay on token embeddings: with decay 0.1 the embeddings became very small, and small embeddings produce large gradients in early layers because the layer-norm Jacobian scales inversely with input norm. Source: arXiv 2501.00656 Section 3.4.2. confirmed
  • Muon without weight decay: both the weights and the layer outputs kept growing, exceeding the high-precision range of bf16; adding AdamW-style weight decay fixed it and made Muon beat AdamW in the over-train regime. Source: Muon is Scalable for LLM Training, arXiv 2502.16982 Section 2.1. confirmed
  • Weight decay 0.1 is used by DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2 (throughout) and DeepSeek-V4 (both AdamW and Muon parameters). Sources: arXiv 2412.19437; arXiv 2507.20534; arXiv 2606.19348. confirmed

muP, u-muP and hyperparameter transfer

  • Tensor Programs V: tune on a small proxy and transfer to a large model. Abstract states that transferring from a 40M-parameter model, they "outperform published numbers of the 6.7B GPT-3 model, with tuning cost only 7% of total pretraining cost." Source: Tensor Programs V, arXiv 2203.03466. confirmed
  • MiniCPM used muP in what it calls model wind tunnel experiments; across 0.04B to 2.1B the optimal base LR stayed near 0.01, and the stable-phase max LR was 0.01. The muP grid search picked scale_depth = 1.4, scale_emb = 12, init_std = 0.1, lr = 0.01. Source: MiniCPM, arXiv 2404.06395. confirmed
  • u-muP combines muP with Unit Scaling so activations, weights and gradients start at scale one; abstract claims default hyperparameters are near-optimal, loss equal or lower than comparable muP models, and FP8 works out of the box. Source: u-muP paper, arXiv 2407.17465. reported (abstract only)
  • CompleteP (Cerebras) targets depth-wise transfer plus non-lazy learning; abstract reports 12-34% compute-efficiency improvements over the prior state of the art, with experiments on Cerebras CS-3 systems. Source: CompleteP paper, arXiv 2505.01618. reported (abstract only)
  • Everett et al.: all parameterizations, not only muP, can achieve hyperparameter transfer; a per-layer LR prescription for standard parameterization outperformed muP (up to 26.8B parameters in their study). Source: arXiv 2407.05872. confirmed
  • Essential AI combine Muon with muP and describe a telescoping algorithm that accounts for sources of error in muP transfer; tested up to four billion parameters. Source: Practical Efficiency of Muon for Pretraining, arXiv 2505.02222. confirmed
  • Wortsman et al. list muP among the interventions that reduce LR sensitivity, and predict two instability cases at scale from how activation and gradient norms scale. Source: arXiv 2309.14322. confirmed
  • Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers warns blind hyperparameter transfer between optimizers is unfair (optimal settings differ). That is about transfer across optimizers, not across scale. Source: arXiv 2509.02046. confirmed
  • Not found: a first-party statement from a frontier lab (Llama, DeepSeek, Kimi) saying the final run used muP. DeepSeek instead fits scaling laws for batch size and LR against compute (see below). inference

WSD and other schedules that extend runs

  • MiniCPM WSD: warmup, a stable phase at max LR, then a decay phase. A decay of 10% of total tokens was enough for best results while 2.5% fell short; decay is exponential in MiniCPM with T = 5000 steps (about 20B tokens), mixing pretraining data with high-quality SFT data. Source: arXiv 2404.06395. confirmed
  • MiniCPM states "With WSD LRS, we can continuously train the LM to extreme convergence", and loss falls sharply in decay to equal or below cosine at the same step. Stable-phase checkpoints can be reused and decayed at any point, which makes scaling-law studies cheaper. Source: arXiv 2404.06395. confirmed
  • Hagele et al.: constant LR with cooldown scales as predictably as cosine; the cooldown can start at any time (even retrospectively from a checkpoint), which allows many training lengths from a single run plus short cooldowns. Continuing from a pre-cooldown checkpoint avoids loss spikes and brittleness seen when re-warming a cosine schedule. Stochastic weight averaging improves performance along the trajectory at no extra training cost. Source: Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training Beyond Fixed Training Durations, arXiv 2405.18392. confirmed
  • The same paper notes the data mixture can change during the cooldown, like fine-tuning, and cites Llama 3 doing this. Source: arXiv 2405.18392. confirmed
  • Optimal peak LR for cooldown sits slightly below the cosine optimum (roughly half of the optimal cosine maximum LR in their 210M setting). Source: arXiv 2405.18392 Figure 3 caption. confirmed
  • DeepSeek LLM multi-step schedule: 2000 warmup steps, LR drops to 31.6% of max after 80% of tokens and 10% of max after 90%. Authors chose it over cosine because it enables continual training with similar final quality; they also fit optimal batch size (rising) and LR (falling) against compute. Source: DeepSeek LLM, arXiv 2401.02954. confirmed
  • Kimi K2 used WSD: 500-step warmup, 10T tokens constant at 2e-4, then 5.5T tokens cosine from 2e-4 to 2e-5, weight decay 0.1, global batch 67M tokens; then an annealing phase from 2e-5 to 7e-6. Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Section 2. confirmed
  • DeepSeek-V3: warmup 2K steps to 2.2e-4, constant until 10T tokens, cosine to 2.2e-5 over 4.3T tokens, then constant 2.2e-5 for 333B tokens and 7.3e-6 for the last 167B. Source: arXiv 2412.19437 Section 4.1. confirmed
  • DeepSeek-V4: LR held at peak (2.7e-4 Flash, 2.0e-4 Pro) for most of training, decayed to a tenth near the end by cosine. Source: arXiv 2606.19348 Section 4.2.2. confirmed
  • Reading: all three frontier recipes (K2, V3, V4) are a long constant-LR phase followed by a late decay, so the run length need not be fixed at launch and the long constant phase can be monitored before committing to a decay. inference

Batch size warmup and critical batch size

  • Llama 3 405B: initial batch 4M tokens at length 4,096, doubled to 8M (length 8,192) after 252M tokens, doubled to 16M after 2.87T tokens. The paper says a lower batch size early improves stability and that the recipe was very stable, with few loss spikes and no interventions for divergence. Source: arXiv 2407.21783 Section 3.4.1. confirmed
  • DeepSeek-V3: batch size increased from 3072 to 15360 over the first 469B tokens, then constant. Source: arXiv 2412.19437 Section 4.1. confirmed
  • DeepSeek-V4: batch size ramped from small to 75.5M tokens (Flash) and a maximum of 94.4M (Pro). Source: arXiv 2606.19348 Section 4.2.2. confirmed
  • Merrill et al. measure critical batch size directly rather than via gradient noise scale: in OLMo models it is near 0 at initialisation, rises fast, then levels off, at both 1B and 7B. Batch size warmup on OLMo 1B reached slightly better loss with 43% fewer gradient steps. Source: Critical Batch Size Revisited, arXiv 2505.23971. confirmed
  • Counter-trend: Marek et al. find small batches train stably if beta2 half-life is fixed in tokens and recommend against gradient accumulation unless training on multiple replicas. Source: arXiv 2507.07101. confirmed
  • Essential AI report Muon retains data efficiency at batch sizes far beyond the critical batch size relative to AdamW. Source: arXiv 2505.02222. confirmed

Muon, Moonlight and MuonClip

What Muon is and what it costs

  • Muon runs SGD momentum, then orthogonalises the update of each 2D hidden-layer weight with a Newton-Schulz iteration (stable in bf16). Embeddings, the output head and scalar/vector parameters should use AdamW. Source: Keller Jordan, kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/. confirmed
  • Reported by Jordan: NanoGPT speedrun (3.28 val loss on FineWeb) improved by 1.35x; FLOP overhead below 1% (0.7% for the speedrun record, 0.5% for Llama 405B training by his calculation). Source: same blog. confirmed
  • Moonshot: Muon uses one momentum buffer versus two for AdamW; Distributed Muon communication is between 1x and 1.25x of distributed AdamW; the optimizer's end-to-end latency is usually 1% to 3% of forward-backward time. Source: arXiv 2502.16982 Section 2.3. confirmed

Muon is Scalable for LLM Training

  • Two techniques make Muon scale: add weight decay and carefully adjust the per-parameter update scale. Source: arXiv 2502.16982 abstract. confirmed
  • Update RMS matching: Muon's theoretical update RMS for a full-rank \( A \times B \) matrix is \( 1/\sqrt{\max(A,B)} \), so it varies with shape. They scale updates by \( 0.2 \cdot \sqrt{\max(A,B)} \) to match AdamW's typical update RMS (0.2 to 0.4), so Muon reuses AdamW's LR and weight decay. Source: arXiv 2502.16982 Section 2.2. confirmed
  • Claim: about 2x computational efficiency over AdamW in compute-optimal training, or about 52% of the training FLOPs to match. Moonlight is a 3B/16B-parameter MoE trained on 5.7T tokens with Muon. Source: arXiv 2502.16982. confirmed

MuonClip and QK-Clip in Kimi K2

  • Kimi K2 report abstract: "Based on MuonClip, K2 was pre-trained on 15.5 trillion tokens with zero loss spike." The introduction says "without a single loss spike." Source: Kimi K2: Open Agentic Intelligence, arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed
  • Moonshot say Muon training is more prone to exploding attention logits than AdamW in their experiments. A mid-scale run (9B activated, 53B total MoE) with vanilla Muon saw max attention logits exceed 1000; they say logits at that level usually lead to significant loss spikes and occasional divergence. Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Section 2.1 and Figure 2. confirmed
  • Why not existing fixes: logit soft-cap clips after the dot product can already have grown; QK-Norm does not apply to MLA because key matrices are not materialised at inference. Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Section 2.1. confirmed
  • QK-Clip: per head, compute the max pre-softmax logit \( S_{\max}^h \). If it exceeds threshold \( \tau \), rescale the query and key projection weights after the update by \( \gamma_h = \min(1, \tau / S_{\max}^h) \), split with balancing parameter \( \alpha \) (typically 0.5). It does not change the current step's forward or backward pass. For MLA it only touches head-specific components; the shared rotary key is untouched. Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Section 2.1. confirmed
  • K2 used \( \tau = 100 \). Max logits rose to the cap of 100 and decayed to a stable range after about 30% of training steps without any change to \( \tau \). Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Figure 2 and text. confirmed
  • QK-Clip was only transiently active: in the first 70,000 steps 12.7% of attention heads triggered it at least once; after step 70,000 all heads had reduced their max logit below 100 and it became inactive. Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Appendix D. confirmed
  • Small-scale ablation (0.5B activated, 3B total MoE) with an aggressive \( \tau = 30 \) showed negligible effect on loss and no statistically significant downstream degradation. Source: arXiv 2507.20534 Appendix D. confirmed
  • Scope of the claim: the "zero loss spike" statement is Moonshot's own, about the loss curve in their Figure 3; no independent reproduction at scale was found. inference

Adoption beyond Moonshot

  • DeepSeek-V4 (Flash 284B and Pro 1.6T parameters, pretrained on 32T and 33T tokens) uses Muon for most modules and AdamW for the embedding, prediction head and RMSNorm weights. Muon settings: momentum 0.95, weight decay 0.1, update RMS rescaled to 0.18, Nesterov, and hybrid Newton-Schulz with 10 iterations in two stages. Source: DeepSeek-V4, arXiv 2606.19348 Sections 2.4 and 4.2.2. confirmed
  • V4 does not use QK-Clip because its attention applies RMSNorm directly on queries and KV entries, which they say prevents attention logit explosion. Source: arXiv 2606.19348 Section 2.4. confirmed
  • V4 still hit loss spikes tied to outliers in MoE layers and fixed them with Anticipatory Routing (route with parameters from step t minus delta; about 20% wall-clock overhead when always on, applied only after automatic spike detection and a short rollback) and SwiGLU clamping (linear part to [-10, 10], gate upper bound 10). The report says rollbacks alone did not stop recurrence and a theoretical understanding is still open. Source: arXiv 2606.19348 Section 4.2.3. confirmed
  • Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers (Sep 2025) states Kimi K2 is the notable exception to AdamW dominance among real-world pretraining runs it surveyed (it also lists Llama 3, DeepSeek and Qwen as AdamW users). Source: arXiv 2509.02046 introduction. confirmed
  • I did not verify Muon use in GLM-4.5 or other labs; searches returned nothing primary. inference

Shampoo, SOAP and optimizer comparisons

  • AlgoPerf (time-to-result benchmark on fixed hardware) was introduced to fix unfair tuning and timing comparisons. Source: AlgoPerf benchmark paper, arXiv 2306.07179. confirmed
  • AlgoPerf v0.5 competition: 18 submissions from 10 teams; Distributed Shampoo won the external-tuning ruleset (the paper credits non-diagonal preconditioning over Adam, even on wall-clock) and a Schedule Free AdamW-based entry won the self-tuning ruleset. Top submissions were surprisingly robust to workload changes. Source: Accelerating Neural Network Training: An Analysis of the AlgoPerf Competition, arXiv 2502.15015 (abstract). confirmed
  • A secondary summary quotes about 28% faster training for Shampoo and 8% for Schedule Free AdamW; I could not confirm these in the paper. reported
  • Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them (Wen, Hall, Ma, Liang): ten optimizers, four sizes (0.1B to 1.2B), 1x to 8x Chinchilla. Claimed 1.4x to 2x speedups mostly reflect weak baselines: tuning only the AdamW learning rate in the GPT-3 recipe gave nearly 2x. Source: arXiv 2509.02046. confirmed
  • Fastest optimizers (Muon, SOAP, Kron) use matrix preconditioners and gave about 1.3x over AdamW below 520M parameters, but the speedup decays to about 1.1x at 1.2B parameters at 8x Chinchilla. At 0.1B the matrix-based speedup is 1.4x. Source: arXiv 2509.02046. confirmed
  • Rankings can flip during training due to LR decay, so comparisons must be at the end of training. The best optimizer also depends on the data-to-model ratio. Source: arXiv 2509.02046. confirmed
  • The paper notes the Moonshot Muon paper only considered the 1x Chinchilla regime, and the original SOAP paper only went up to 0.6B parameters. Source: arXiv 2509.02046 footnote 1. confirmed
  • In Search of Adam's Secret Sauce: setting beta1 = beta2 performs near-optimally in their sweeps (up to 410M); Signum closes most of the perplexity gap between SGD and Adam. Source: arXiv 2505.21829. confirmed

Open questions and conflicting sources

  • Muon speedup: Moonshot report about 2x compute efficiency (52% of FLOPs) in compute-optimal training with a 5.7T-token Moonlight run; Wen et al. find at most about 1.1x over a well-tuned AdamW at 1.2B and 8x Chinchilla, shrinking with scale. The regimes differ (MoE versus dense, tuning budget, token ratio), so these may not truly conflict, but nobody has run the same comparison at tens of billions of parameters publicly. inference
  • Does Muon stabilise or destabilise? DeepSeek-V4 says Muon gives faster convergence and improved training stability; Moonshot say Muon is more prone to attention-logit explosion than AdamW and needed QK-Clip. V4 sidesteps this with RMSNorm on queries and keys, then still reports MoE-outlier loss spikes. Sources: arXiv 2606.19348; arXiv 2507.20534. confirmed (the disagreement; the cause is open)
  • Moonshot's explanation for why Muon is more prone to logit explosion is in an appendix I did not read in detail. reported
  • Zero loss spikes: K2 claims zero over 15.5T tokens, V4 reports spikes and needs two mitigations; the two models differ in architecture (MLA, QK-Clip versus hybrid attention, mHC, RMSNorm on QK), so the comparison is not clean. inference
  • Is global gradient clipping still doing work in Muon runs? I did not find clipping settings in the K2 or V4 optimizer sections I read. inference
  • muP in frontier runs: the transfer literature is strong at small to mid scale (up to 26.8B in Everett et al., 4B in Essential AI, 6.7B comparison in Tensor Programs V), but no first-party report from a frontier lab confirms muP in a final run. inference
  • Weight-decay scaling rules (Wang and Aitchison) were validated on small vision and NanoGPT setups only. confirmed
  • StableAdamW evidence is CLIP ViT-Huge; I found no language-model pretraining report using it. inference
  • The AlgoPerf speedup figures from a secondary site are unconfirmed; check arXiv 2502.15015 body before quoting numbers. reported
  • Hagele et al. and MiniCPM use different decay shapes (1-sqrt and linear versus exponential); Hagele et al. leave open whether one cooldown shape is optimal for a given compute budget. confirmed
