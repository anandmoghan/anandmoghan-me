From diffusion to flow matching: the theory

Research note for the flow-matching speech generation post. Written 2026-10-13.

Conventions

  • Papers disagree on the time direction. DDPM indexes noise at \( t = T \). Lipman et al. and Liu et al. put noise at \( t = 0 \) and data at \( t = 1 \). This note follows the flow-matching convention unless a section says otherwise.
  • Every statement carries a grounding tag: Confirmed means I read the primary source, Reported means a secondary source, Inference means my own reasoning.

DDPM: forward, reverse and the epsilon loss

Source: Ho, Jain, Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models", arXiv 2006.11239.

  • The forward process adds Gaussian noise in small steps, and \( x_t \) can be sampled at any \( t \) in closed form (Eq. 2 and Eq. 4). Confirmed
  • The reverse process is a learned Gaussian; the mean is parameterised through a noise predictor (Eq. 11). Confirmed
  • The simplified loss \( L_{\text{simple}} \) (Eq. 14) is an unweighted version of the per-step bound term (Eq. 12), and the authors found it beneficial for sample quality. Confirmed
  • Experiments use \( T = 1000 \) and a linear \( \beta_t \) schedule from \( 10^{-4} \) to \( 0.02 \). Confirmed
  • The authors also considered predicting \( x_0 \) and report it "led to worse sample quality early in our experiments". Confirmed
\[ q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\ \beta_t I\big) \qquad \text{(Eq. 2)} \] \[ q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_t) I\big), \qquad x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon \qquad \text{(Eq. 4)} \] \[ \mu_\theta(x_t, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\Big) \qquad \text{(Eq. 11)} \] \[ L_{\text{simple}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\,x_0,\,\epsilon}\Big[\big\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\ t\big)\big\|^2\Big] \qquad \text{(Eq. 14)} \]
  • \( x_0 \): clean data. \( x_t \): data after \( t \) noising steps. \( \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \): the noise that was added.
  • \( \beta_t \): fixed per-step noise variance. \( \alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t \). \( \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} \alpha_s \): fraction of signal variance that survives to step \( t \).
  • \( \epsilon_\theta \): the network that predicts the noise. \( \mu_\theta \): mean of the reverse step \( p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t) \), whose variance \( \sigma_t^2 \) is fixed (Algorithm 2).
  • \( t \): uniform on \( \{1, \dots, T\} \) in training (Algorithm 1).

Sampling (Algorithm 2) starts at \( x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \) and iterates \( x_{t-1} = \mu_\theta(x_t, t) + \sigma_t z \), with \( z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \) for \( t > 1 \) and \( z = 0 \) at the last step. Confirmed

Because \( \nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t \mid x_0) = -\epsilon / \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t} \) under Eq. 4, \( \epsilon_\theta \) is a rescaled score estimate: \( \epsilon_\theta \approx -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla \log p_t \). The paper says only that Eq. 12 "resembles denoising score matching"; the exact scaling is my derivation. Inference

Score-based SDE view and the probability-flow ODE

Source: Song, Sohl-Dickstein, Kingma, Kumar, Ermon, Poole, "Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations", arXiv 2011.13456.

  • The forward noising is an SDE (Eq. 5) and its reverse-time SDE depends only on the score of the perturbed marginals (Eq. 6). Confirmed
  • DDPM's chain is a discretisation of the variance-preserving (VP) SDE (Eq. 10 and Eq. 11); the SMLD chain gives the variance-exploding (VE) SDE. Confirmed
  • For every diffusion process there is a deterministic process with the same marginals \( p_t(x) \), which is an ODE (Eq. 13). The paper names it the probability flow ODE. Confirmed
  • The abstract says this ODE enables exact likelihood computation and improved sampling efficiency. Confirmed
  • The score is learned by denoising score matching with weighting \( \lambda(t) \) (Eq. 7). Confirmed
\[ \mathrm{d}x = f(x, t)\,\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}w \qquad \text{(Eq. 5, forward SDE)} \] \[ \mathrm{d}x = \big[f(x, t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}\bar w \qquad \text{(Eq. 6, reverse-time SDE)} \] \[ \mathrm{d}x = \big[f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g(t)^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]\mathrm{d}t \qquad \text{(Eq. 13, probability flow ODE)} \] \[ \mathrm{d}x = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,x\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,\mathrm{d}w \qquad \text{(Eq. 11, VP-SDE)} \]
  • \( f(x, t) \): drift of the forward process. \( g(t) \): scalar diffusion coefficient. \( w \): forward Wiener process; \( \bar w \): Wiener process when time runs backwards.
  • \( p_t(x) \): marginal density of \( x(t) \). \( \nabla_x \log p_t(x) \): its score, estimated by \( s_\theta(x, t) \).
  • \( \beta(t) \): continuous-time noise rate whose discretisation is DDPM's \( \beta_t \).

x0, epsilon and v prediction

Source: Salimans and Ho, "Progressive Distillation for Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models", arXiv 2202.00512 (variance-preserving, \( \sigma_t^2 = 1 - \alpha_t^2 \)).

  • Predicting \( \epsilon \) implicitly sets \( \hat x = (z_t - \sigma_t \hat\epsilon) / \alpha_t \), which equals an \( x \)-space loss weighted by \( \alpha_t^2 / \sigma_t^2 \), the SNR (Eq. 9). Confirmed
  • At zero SNR, \( z_t = \epsilon \) carries no information about \( x \), so \( \epsilon \)-prediction no longer implies an \( x \) prediction and the SNR weight is zero. This is why distillation needed a different parameterisation. Confirmed
  • v-prediction is \( v \equiv \alpha_t \epsilon - \sigma_t x \), and then \( \hat x = \alpha_t z_t - \sigma_t \hat v \). Confirmed
  • In their ablation, v-prediction is described as the most stable because it makes DDIM step sizes independent of the SNR, while x-prediction gave slightly better empirical results. Predicting \( \epsilon \) with truncated-SNR weighting was unstable. Confirmed
  • Geometrically, with \( \alpha = \cos\phi \) and \( \sigma = \sin\phi \), \( v \) is the derivative of \( z_\phi \) with respect to the angle \( \phi \), and a DDIM step rotates along a circle (Appendix D). Confirmed
\[ z_t = \alpha_t\,x + \sigma_t\,\epsilon, \qquad v = \alpha_t\,\epsilon - \sigma_t\,x, \qquad x = \alpha_t z_t - \sigma_t v, \qquad \epsilon = \sigma_t z_t + \alpha_t v \]
  • \( \alpha_t, \sigma_t \): signal and noise scales, with \( \alpha_t^2 + \sigma_t^2 = 1 \). \( z_t \): noisy latent. \( v \): the "velocity" in angle space.
  • The \( \epsilon \) expression is from the paper's angular derivation (Appendix D, after Eq. 30).

The diffusion \( v \) is not the flow-matching velocity. With \( \alpha_t = t \) and \( \sigma_t = 1-t \), the time derivative of \( z_t \) is \( x - \epsilon \), the flow-matching target. Salimans' \( v \) is the derivative with respect to the angle \( \phi \), on the variance-preserving circle. Inference

Classifier-free guidance

Source: Ho and Salimans, "Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance", arXiv 2207.12598.

  • One network is trained jointly as conditional and unconditional by replacing the condition \( c \) with a null token \( \varnothing \) with probability \( p_{\text{uncond}} \) (Algorithm 1). Confirmed
  • Sampling uses the linear combination in Eq. 6, so each step costs two forward passes (one conditional, one unconditional). The two-pass cost follows from Eq. 6; the paper does not state it as a headline. Confirmed
  • On ImageNet 64x64 the authors compared \( p_{\text{uncond}} \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.5\} \). \( 0.5 \) was consistently worse than \( 0.1 \) and \( 0.2 \), which performed about equally. They conclude only a small share of capacity is needed for the unconditional task. Confirmed
  • The paper is about diffusion models. Applying the same extrapolation to a flow-matching velocity network is a direct substitution, as ZipVoice-style and F5-style TTS do, but this paper does not cover it. Inference
\[ \tilde\epsilon_\theta(z, c) = (1 + w)\,\epsilon_\theta(z, c) - w\,\epsilon_\theta(z) \qquad \text{(Eq. 6)} \] \[ \hat v = v_\theta(x, t, c) + w\big(v_\theta(x, t, c) - v_\theta(x, t, \varnothing)\big) \qquad \text{(velocity analogue)} \]
  • \( c \): conditioning (class, text, or speech prompt). \( \varnothing \): null condition. \( w \): guidance strength; \( w = 0 \) is the plain conditional model.
  • \( \epsilon_\theta(z) \) is the same network evaluated with \( c = \varnothing \).
  • The second equation is Eq. 6 with \( \epsilon_\theta \) swapped for a velocity network, the same form as the existing materials fragment.

Flow matching and conditional flow matching

Source: Lipman, Chen, Ben-Hamu, Nickel, Le, "Flow Matching for Generative Modeling", arXiv 2210.02747.

  • Flow matching trains a continuous normalizing flow by regressing a vector field, simulation-free. Confirmed
  • The FM loss (Eq. 5) needs the intractable marginal field \( u_t(x) \). The conditional loss (Eq. 9) uses a per-sample field \( u_t(x \mid x_1) \), and the two have identical gradients with respect to \( \theta \) (Theorem 2). Confirmed
  • Gaussian conditional paths \( p_t(x \mid x_1) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_t(x_1), \sigma_t(x_1)^2 I) \) have a unique closed-form field (Theorem 3, Eq. 15). Confirmed
  • The family "subsumes existing diffusion paths as specific instances", and using FM with diffusion paths is reported as a more robust and stable alternative for training diffusion models. Confirmed
  • The VP diffusion conditional field (Eq. 19) coincides with the probability flow field of Song et al. restricted to these paths. Confirmed
\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\ q(x_1),\ p_t(x \mid x_1)}\big\|v_t(x;\theta) - u_t(x \mid x_1)\big\|^2 \qquad \text{(Eq. 9)} \] \[ u_t(x \mid x_1) = \frac{\sigma_t'(x_1)}{\sigma_t(x_1)}\big(x - \mu_t(x_1)\big) + \mu_t'(x_1) \qquad \text{(Eq. 15)} \]
  • \( x_1 \sim q \): a data sample. \( p_t(x \mid x_1) \): conditional probability path. \( v_t(x;\theta) \): the network's vector field. \( u_t(x \mid x_1) \): the conditional target field.
  • \( \mu_t, \sigma_t \): mean and standard deviation of the Gaussian path. Primes are time derivatives. \( t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1] \).

The optimal-transport path and the sigma_min term

  • The OT path sets the mean and std to change linearly in time (Eq. 20), generated by the field in Eq. 21, with flow map \( \psi_t \) (Eq. 22). Confirmed
  • \( \sigma_{\min} \) is the std of the target Gaussian at \( t = 1 \), so \( p_1(x \mid x_1) \) is a narrow Gaussian around \( x_1 \) rather than a point mass. The regression target after substituting the flow map is \( x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})x_0 \) (Eq. 23). Confirmed
  • The conditional flow \( \psi_t \) is the optimal-transport map between the two conditional Gaussians \( p_0(x \mid x_1) \) and \( p_1(x \mid x_1) \), moving with constant speed along straight lines. Confirmed
  • Setting \( \sigma_{\min} = 0 \) gives the familiar target \( x_1 - x_0 \) used in speech systems and in the materials fragments. Inference
  • The OT field has constant direction in time, whereas the diffusion conditional field can overshoot the final sample and backtrack (Figure 3 discussion). Confirmed
\[ \mu_t(x_1) = t\,x_1, \qquad \sigma_t = 1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\,t \qquad \text{(Eq. 20)} \] \[ u_t(x \mid x_1) = \frac{x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})\,x}{1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})\,t} \qquad \text{(Eq. 21)} \] \[ \psi_t(x_0) = \big(1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})t\big)\,x_0 + t\,x_1, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\ q(x_1),\ p(x_0)}\big\|v_t(\psi_t(x_0)) - \big(x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})\,x_0\big)\big\|^2 \qquad \text{(Eq. 22, Eq. 23)} \]
  • \( x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \): noise sample. \( \sigma_{\min} \): small positive constant. \( \psi_t \): the conditional flow map that carries \( x_0 \) to the point at time \( t \).
  • Sampling integrates \( \mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t = v_\theta(x, t) \) from \( t = 0 \) to \( t = 1 \) with an ODE solver.

Rectified flow and reflow

Source: Liu, Gong, Liu, "Flow Straight and Fast: Learning to Generate and Transfer Data with Rectified Flow", arXiv 2209.03003.

  • Rectified flow learns an ODE \( \mathrm{d}Z_t = v(Z_t, t)\,\mathrm{d}t \) by least-squares regression of \( v \) onto \( X_1 - X_0 \) along the linear interpolation (Eq. 1). This is the same regression as Lipman's OT conditional loss with \( \sigma_{\min} = 0 \). Confirmed for the Liu loss; the equivalence is Inference.
  • The exact minimiser is the conditional expectation of the line directions through \( x \): \( v^X(x, t) = \mathbb{E}[X_1 - X_0 \mid X_t = x] \) (Eq. 2). Confirmed
  • Marginal preserving property (Theorem 3.3): \( \mathrm{Law}(Z_t) = \mathrm{Law}(X_t) \) for every \( t \), so \( (Z_0, Z_1) \) is a valid coupling of \( \pi_0 \) and \( \pi_1 \). Confirmed
  • Convex transport costs do not increase (Theorem 3.5), and the reflow iteration \( Z^{k+1} = \mathrm{RectFlow}((Z_0^k, Z_1^k)) \) increases straightness (Theorem 3.7). Confirmed
  • A straight flow (\( Z_t = tZ_1 + (1-t)Z_0 \)) is exactly simulated by one Euler step. Making a learned flow straight is non-trivial, because it must satisfy the inviscid Burgers equation \( \partial_t v + (\partial_z v)\,v = 0 \). Confirmed
  • The authors say it is not recommended to apply too many reflow steps, because estimation error on \( v^X \) accumulates. Confirmed
  • They report one step of reflow already gives nearly straight flows that work with a single Euler step. Confirmed as the authors' claim on images.
\[ \min_v \int_0^1 \mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|(X_1 - X_0) - v(X_t, t)\big\|^2\Big]\mathrm{d}t, \qquad X_t = tX_1 + (1-t)X_0 \qquad \text{(Eq. 1)} \] \[ S(Z) = \int_0^1 \mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|(Z_1 - Z_0) - \dot Z_t\big\|^2\Big]\mathrm{d}t \qquad \text{(Eq. 3)} \]
  • \( X_0 \sim \pi_0 \): noise. \( X_1 \sim \pi_1 \): data. \( (X_0, X_1) \): any coupling (independent by default). \( X_t \): linear interpolation.
  • \( Z_t \): the ODE trajectory driven by the learned \( v \). \( \dot Z_t \): its time derivative.
  • \( S(Z) \): straightness; zero means exactly straight at constant speed.
  • Theorem 3.7 bounds \( \min_{k \le K} S(Z^k) \le \mathbb{E}\|X_1 - X_0\|^2 / K \).

Stochastic interpolants as the unifying view

Source: Albergo, Boffi, Vanden-Eijnden, "Stochastic Interpolants: A Unifying Framework for Flows and Diffusions", arXiv 2303.08797 (JMLR 26, 2025, v4). It extends Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden, "Building Normalizing Flows with Stochastic Interpolants", arXiv 2209.15571, which I downloaded but did not read in detail.

  • A stochastic interpolant bridges any two densities \( \rho_0, \rho_1 \) in finite time with \( x_t = I(t, x_0, x_1) + \gamma(t) z \) (Eq. 2.1), with \( I(0) = x_0 \), \( I(1) = x_1 \) and \( \gamma(0) = \gamma(1) = 0 \). Confirmed
  • The density of \( x_t \) satisfies the transport equation \( \partial_t \rho + \nabla\cdot(b\rho) = 0 \) (Eq. 2.9) with velocity \( b = \mathbb{E}[\dot x_t \mid x_t = x] \) (Eq. 2.10), and also a family of Fokker-Planck equations with tunable diffusion coefficient. This yields both an ODE and SDEs with the same marginals. Confirmed
  • \( b \) is the unique minimiser of a quadratic objective (Theorem 7, Eq. 2.13), and the score has a separate one (Eq. 2.14 and Eq. 2.16). Confirmed
  • The paper states it unifies flow-based and diffusion-based methods. Section 5 covers score-based diffusion and rectified flows; score-based diffusion is recast as a one-sided interpolant (\( x_t = \alpha(t) z + J(t, x_1) \), Eq. 3.14) after a reparameterisation of time. Confirmed
  • The paper's related-work section says Liu et al. (rectification) "improves sampling efficiency but introduces a bias", and that Lipman et al.'s noise convolution is used "at the cost of biasing the method". Their own noise injection is presented as unbiased. Confirmed as the authors' characterisation of others.
\[ x_t = I(t, x_0, x_1) + \gamma(t)\,z, \qquad b(t, x) = \mathbb{E}\big[\partial_t I(t, x_0, x_1) + \dot\gamma(t)\,z \ \big|\ x_t = x\big] \qquad \text{(Eq. 2.1, Eq. 2.10)} \] \[ s(t, x) = \nabla \log \rho(t, x) = -\gamma^{-1}(t)\,\mathbb{E}\big[z \mid x_t = x\big] \qquad \text{(Eq. 2.14)} \]
  • \( x_0 \sim \rho_0 \), \( x_1 \sim \rho_1 \): endpoints drawn from a coupling \( \nu \). \( z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \): independent latent noise. \( I \): interpolation function. \( \gamma(t) \): latent noise amplitude, zero at both ends.
  • Example: \( x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1 + \sqrt{2t(1-t)}\,z \) (Eq. 1.1).
  • With \( \gamma = 0 \) and \( I = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1 \), this reduces to the rectified-flow regression.

How diffusion and flow matching relate

  • Both are Gaussian-path models. Under \( x_t = a_t x_1 + s_t \epsilon \), diffusion regresses \( \epsilon \) (or the score, or \( v \)), flow matching regresses \( \dot x_t \), and the targets are linear reparameterisations of each other. The VP diffusion path is a special case of the Gaussian family, and its conditional field equals the probability flow field. Confirmed for the VP case (Lipman, Eq. 18 and Eq. 19); the general linear-map statement is Inference.
  • Velocity and score are linked by a closed form. From \( x_t = a_t x_1 + s_t \epsilon \), \( \mathbb{E}[\dot x_t \mid x] = (\dot a_t / a_t)\,x + (\dot a_t s_t^2 / a_t - s_t \dot s_t)\,\nabla\log p_t(x) \). For the OT path (\( a_t = t \), \( s_t = 1-t \)) this becomes \( x/t + \frac{1-t}{t}\nabla\log p_t(x) \). This is my derivation from the score formula in Eq. 2.14 of the interpolants paper. Inference
  • The differences are the path and the target, not the model family. Diffusion uses a variance-preserving (or exploding) path that only approaches noise asymptotically in the SDE form; flow matching with the linear path reaches noise at \( t = 0 \) in finite time and the target \( x_1 - x_0 \) has a stable scale at every \( t \). Lipman et al. note the diffusion paths "do not actually reach a true noise distribution in finite time". Confirmed
  • Diffusion also carries a noise schedule and a loss weighting over noise levels. Under \( \epsilon \)-prediction the implied weight on the \( x \)-space loss is the SNR (Salimans Eq. 9); flow matching removes the schedule but keeps a choice of how \( t \) is sampled in training. Confirmed for the diffusion weight; the "keeps \( t \) sampling" point is Inference (the materials page cites a large rectified-flow study for biased time sampling; I did not read it here).
  • Sampler: diffusion can be sampled with an SDE or the probability-flow ODE. Plain flow matching and rectified flow sample the ODE only; stochastic interpolants add back a tunable SDE with the same marginals. Confirmed

Why straight paths need fewer solver steps

  • A perfectly straight constant-speed flow is exactly simulated by a single Euler step. Confirmed (Liu et al.)
  • Lipman et al. report that OT-path flow matching reaches a given per-pixel MSE against a 1000-NFE solution with "roughly only 60% of the NFEs" needed by diffusion paths, on ImageNet-32 with fixed-step solvers. They also report better FID at very low NFE. Confirmed (images, not speech)
  • A solver's step error grows with how fast the velocity changes along the trajectory. A constant-direction field has none of this error, a curved one needs short steps. Inference
  • The learned marginal field averages over every noise-data pair passing through a point, so the learned trajectories are only straighter than diffusion, not straight. This follows from \( v^X(x,t) = \mathbb{E}[X_1 - X_0 \mid X_t = x] \) and is why reflow exists. Inference grounded in Liu et al. Eq. 2.
  • Reflow re-pairs noise with the model's own outputs, giving straighter paths. After one reflow the authors report good single-step results on images; many reflows are discouraged. Confirmed

Open questions and conflicts

  • Time direction differs across papers (DDPM noise at \( T \); Lipman, Liu and the interpolants paper noise at \( t = 0 \)). Equations from different papers need a conversion before they are combined.
  • The materials fragments omit \( \sigma_{\min} \) (they use \( x_1 - x_0 \)). The value of \( \sigma_{\min} \) used by Lipman et al. and by speech systems such as F5-TTS or Voicebox was not checked.
  • The fragment says flow matching has "no noise schedule and no SNR weighting". That holds for the loss, but a time-sampling distribution and an inference step schedule still exist (see the sway-sampling discussion in the speech-to-speech chapter, not re-verified here).
  • The CFG scale convention differs between papers. Ho and Salimans use \( (1+w)\,\epsilon(c) - w\,\epsilon(\varnothing) \); some speech papers may use \( w\,\epsilon(c) + (1-w)\,\epsilon(\varnothing) \). I did not check which convention F5-TTS, ZipVoice or Voicebox use.
  • The "60% of NFEs" and low-NFE FID results are for ImageNet-32 images, not speech. Whether the straight-path advantage transfers to mel or codec-latent generation is not established by these papers.
  • Liu et al. frame reflow as improving efficiency, while Albergo et al. say it "introduces a bias". How much that matters in practice for speech was not investigated.
  • I did not read Esser et al., "Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers" (arXiv 2403.03206), or InstaFlow (arXiv 2309.06380). Both were downloaded but unread, so nothing here relies on them.
  • DDIM (arXiv 2010.02502) was not fetched, so deterministic-sampler claims rest only on Song et al. and Salimans and Ho.
