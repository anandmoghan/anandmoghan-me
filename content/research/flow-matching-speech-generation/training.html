How flow matching speech models are trained in practice

Research note for the flow matching and diffusion speech post. Checked 2026-10-13.

Scope and grounding

Every claim carries a tag. Confirmed means I read the primary paper. Reported means a secondary source. Inference means my own reasoning.

Numbers appear only with the paper that states them. Where a paper does not state a number, the note says so.

Notation: \( x_0 \) is Gaussian noise, \( x_1 \) is the target mel or latent, \( m \) is the binary frame mask, and \( x_{ctx} = (1-m) \odot x_1 \) is the visible context. The Voicebox path and target are

\[ x_t = \big(1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t\big)x_0 + t\,x_1, \qquad u_t = x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})\,x_0 . \]

The masked loss is \( \lVert m \odot (u_t - v_\theta(x_t, x_{ctx}, c, t)) \rVert^2 \), where \( c \) is the text condition.

What is generated, and what comes after

  • Voicebox generates an 80-dim log mel at 100 Hz. A HiFi-GAN adapted from V1 (16 kHz, 13M parameters) renders it. That vocoder was trained for 1.5M updates on 8 GPUs, which took 7.5 days. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Section 5.1 and Appendix A.1. Confirmed
  • E2 TTS generates a 100-dim log mel from 24 kHz audio, with a frame every 10.7 ms. A BigVGAN-based vocoder renders it. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 3.2. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS generates a 100-dim log mel at 24 kHz with hop length 256. It uses the pre-trained Vocos vocoder. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 4. Confirmed
  • Matcha-TTS and P-Flow also generate mels and use pre-trained HiFi-GAN vocoders. Matcha-TTS uses the LJ Speech checkpoint. P-Flow uses the universal HiFi-GAN with a 22 kHz, 80-bin mel. Source: Matcha-TTS (arXiv 2309.03199), Section 4; P-Flow (NeurIPS 2023, no arXiv id found), Section 4. Confirmed
  • Audiobox predicts continuous latents instead of a mel. These are the dense Encodec features taken before the residual quantiser. The authors say HiFi-GAN does not generalise to non-speech audio, which motivated the change. Source: Audiobox (arXiv 2312.15821), Section 4.1. Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS has a diffusion-only variant, Seed-TTS_DiT. It turns Gaussian noise directly into the vocoder's latent representation from text alone, then a vocoder makes the waveform. The main Seed-TTS pipeline is AR LM, then a token diffusion model, then an acoustic vocoder. Source: Seed-TTS (arXiv 2406.02430), Section 1 and the Seed-TTS_DiT section. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice and CosyVoice 2 generate a mel from semantic tokens with OT-CFM. CosyVoice names HiFi-GAN as the vocoder. CosyVoice 2 says only "a pre-trained vocoder". Source: CosyVoice (arXiv 2407.05407), Section 1; CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Section 2. Confirmed
  • DiTTo-TTS is a latent diffusion DiT, and MiniMax-Speech has a "Flow-VAE". I did not re-open either paper this session. Source: local note speech-lit-review/03-speech-generation/30-diffusion-and-flow.html (cites arXiv 2406.11427 and 2505.07916). Reported
  • Among the papers I read, mel plus a separately trained vocoder is the default for the TTS-first flow models. Latents appear when the target must cover non-speech audio (Audiobox) or when the system is a large production stack (Seed-TTS_DiT). Inference

Masked infilling and how prompting falls out of it

  • Voicebox sets the masked frames of the context to zero and feeds the context, the frame-level phones and the noisy input to the network. The network is written to model all frames (Eq. 5), but the reported model uses a loss on masked frames only (Eq. 6). Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Section 3. Confirmed
  • Voicebox masks a contiguous segment of r% of the sequence, with r drawn from U[70, 100]. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Section 5.1, Training. Confirmed
  • E2 TTS and F5-TTS use the same 70 to 100% mask range. CosyVoice 2's flow model masks 70 to 100% of the final mel frames. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 3.2; F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 4; CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Section 2. Confirmed
  • At inference, E2 TTS builds the input from the prompt mel, a zero block for the part to generate, and the character string "prompt transcript, target text, filler tokens". F5-TTS does the same and then discards the prompt region of the output mel. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 2.2; F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 3.1. Confirmed
  • Voicebox treats prompt plus target as one utterance in which the target speech is masked. The same infilling objective gives it noise removal and content editing without extra training. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Sections 3.4 and 4. Confirmed
  • Voice cloning needs no speaker encoder because the voice is the visible context. A 70 to 100% mask during training matches the inference shape: a short prompt followed by a long blank. Inference
  • P-Flow does not use infilling. It feeds a random 3-second mel segment into a speech-prompted text encoder, with no prompt transcript. It masks the loss over that segment, because the model otherwise collapses to copy-pasting the prompt. Source: P-Flow (NeurIPS 2023), Section 3.1. Confirmed
  • Audiobox pre-trains with infilling alone, with no transcript. It then fine-tunes with the Voicebox-style transcript-guided objective. During pre-training the input is fully masked with probability p_cond = 0.1, otherwise a subset of frames is masked with n_mask ~ U[70%, 100%] and minimum span 10. Source: Audiobox (arXiv 2312.15821), Sections 4.1 and 4.2. Confirmed

Conditioning signals

Text: aligned phones versus padded characters

  • Voicebox forced-aligns the transcript with the Montreal Forced Aligner and expands phones to frame level. It concatenates phone embedding, noisy input and context frame by frame, then projects them. The flow step embedding is added as one extra token in the sequence. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Section 3 and Section 5.1. Confirmed
  • E2 TTS replaces the frame-level phones with a character sequence padded with a filler token to the mel length. This removes the grapheme-to-phoneme converter, the aligner and the duration model. The character vocabulary has 399 entries, and case and punctuation are kept. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Sections 2.1, 2.4 and 3.2. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS keeps the filler padding but passes the characters through ConvNeXt V2 blocks before concatenating with the speech input. The base model uses 4 ConvNeXt layers (512/1024) with an absolute sinusoidal embedding, and a vocabulary of 2,546 symbols. Chinese is converted to full pinyin. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Sections 3.2 and 4. Confirmed
  • Matcha-TTS uses monotonic alignment search (MAS) and a deterministic duration predictor. The predicted durations (rounded up) duplicate the text-encoder vectors. Source: Matcha-TTS (arXiv 2309.03199), Section 2. Confirmed
  • P-Flow also uses MAS in training. Its duration predictor regresses log durations with MSE. Source: P-Flow (NeurIPS 2023), Section 3.1. Confirmed

Semantic tokens from an AR model

  • CosyVoice's flow model is conditioned on a speaker embedding, the semantic tokens, and a masked mel. The tokens come from a supervised ASR-derived tokenizer with one 4,096-entry codebook. Source: CosyVoice (arXiv 2407.05407), Sections 2.1 and 3. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2 uses FSQ tokens at 25 Hz, up-sampled to match a 50 Hz mel at 24 kHz. A look-ahead convolution precedes the up-sampling. The speaker embedding comes from a CAM++ model in the 3D-Speaker toolkit. Source: CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Sections 2.1 and 2.3. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2 removes the speaker embedding from its LM, to avoid information leaking. It says the utterance-level vector carries language and paralinguistic information that hurts prosody naturalness and cross-lingual ability. Speaker information is then recovered mainly by the flow model. Source: CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Sections 2.2 and 4 (ablation). Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS's main pipeline has the AR LM write speech tokens and a diffusion transformer add acoustic detail. The report gives no step counts, hours or GPUs for the main model. It says only that data and model scale are "orders of magnitude larger" than previous work. Source: Seed-TTS (arXiv 2406.02430), Section 1. Confirmed
  • Because AR tokens fix content and length, the hybrid needs no duration estimate. Inference

Duration modelling

  • Voicebox's default duration model is a regression Transformer trained with L1 loss on masked phone durations. It also considers a flow-matching duration model. The English duration model has 8 layers, 512/2048 dimensions and 28M parameters. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Sections 3.3 and 5.1. Confirmed
  • Audiobox trains a flow-matching duration model instead. It cites Voicebox as showing better diversity than a regression model, and it averages over a small number of sampled durations for stability. Source: Audiobox (arXiv 2312.15821), Section 5. Confirmed
  • In E2 TTS the total length (prompt plus generated) can be chosen freely. The main experiments still used a Voicebox-style regression duration model (8 layers, 75,000 updates), for a fair comparison. Rates from 0.7 to 1.3 gave only a moderate WER increase and a high SIM-o. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Sections 3.2 and 3.6.3. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS estimates the duration from the ratio of the character counts of the generated text and the reference transcript. Results text describes it as the ratio of "audio prompt's transcript length and the text prompt length". Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Sections 3.1 and 5. Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS_DiT estimates only total duration and lets the network learn the local alignment. The authors say adding a phoneme-level duration model reduced naturalness. For its editing features they feed the total duration in directly. Source: Seed-TTS (arXiv 2406.02430), Seed-TTS_DiT section. Confirmed
  • E2 TTS makes a similar claim: explicit duration models and carefully designed architectures are "sometimes even harmful to naturalness". Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 1. Confirmed
  • Total length doubles as a speech-rate control, since fewer frames means faster speech. Inference

Time-step sampling

  • Training samples \( t \sim U[0,1] \) in Voicebox, Matcha-TTS, F5-TTS and CosyVoice 2. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Appendix on flow matching; Matcha-TTS (arXiv 2309.03199), Eq. 2; F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 3.2; CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Section 2.3. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS explicitly declines logit-normal sampling for training. It cites Esser et al. (arXiv 2403.03206), who use a single-peak logit-normal on intermediate steps for images, and keeps uniform \( t \). Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 3.2. Confirmed
  • I did not find a time-step sampling statement in the E2 TTS text. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS applies the non-uniform choice at inference instead, as Sway Sampling. It draws \( u \sim U[0,1] \) and maps it to
\[ t = u + s\Big(\cos\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}u\big) - 1 + u\Big), \qquad s \in \big[-1, \tfrac{2}{\pi-2}\big]. \]
  • The default is \( s = -1 \), which puts more steps at small \( t \). The authors argue that early steps sketch the contour of the speech. Their "leak and override" experiment starts from a noisy copy of the prompt mel at \( t' = 0.1 \) with different text. The model follows the new text with Sway Sampling and repeats the prompt content with uniform steps. It needs no retraining. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Sections 3.2 and 5.2. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice and CosyVoice 2 use a cosine inference schedule \( t := 1 - \cos(\tfrac{1}{2}t\pi) \), which gives more steps to the early stage. Source: CosyVoice (arXiv 2407.05407), Eq. 13; CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Eq. 9. Confirmed
  • Voicebox's reduced-setup ablation finds the OT path trains faster than diffusion paths. The OT flow-matching model reached its best result by 100K updates, and beat the score-matching model with only 50K. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Appendix B.2. Confirmed
  • So the speech papers I read keep training uniform and move all the non-uniformity to inference. Inference

Architectures

  • Matcha-TTS uses a 1D U-Net decoder: two down blocks, two mid blocks and two up blocks. Each block has one Transformer layer (hidden size 256, 2 heads, snake-beta activations). It states that Voicebox's 330M parameters are 18 times larger than its own model. Source: Matcha-TTS (arXiv 2309.03199), Sections 2 and 4. Confirmed
  • The Voicebox audio model is a Transformer with 24 layers, 16 heads, 1024/4096 dimensions and 330M parameters. It uses convolutional position embeddings and symmetric bi-directional ALiBi, with the ALiBi bias for the flow step set to 0. U-Net-style skips join symmetric layers by channel-wise concatenation and a linear layer. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Section 5.1. Confirmed
  • E2 TTS uses the same skip-connected Transformer with 24 layers, 16 heads, 1024/4096 and 335M parameters. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 3.2. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS drops the U-Net skips for a DiT with adaLN-zero and RoPE. The base DiT has 22 layers, 16 heads and 1024/2048 dimensions. With the ConvNeXt V2 text branch the total is 335.8M parameters. The flow step conditions adaLN-zero, instead of being appended to the sequence as in Voicebox. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Sections 3.2 and 4. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2's flow network is a causal convolutional Transformer UNet. It is trained with four attention masks (non-causal, full-causal, chunk-M, chunk-2M) sampled uniformly per example. Its loss is L1 on the vector field, not L2. Source: CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Section 2.3. Confirmed
  • P-Flow uses a 3M-parameter Transformer text encoder and a WaveNet-based flow decoder. Source: P-Flow (NeurIPS 2023), Section 3.3. Confirmed

Loss masking

  • Voicebox compares loss on all frames with loss on masked frames only, in a reduced setup (1K h, 12 layers, 150K steps). For flow matching, masked loss gave SIM-r 0.597 against 0.528 for all frames, with WER 2.1 against 2.0. The paper concludes that masking the loss improves similarity and diversity with little effect on intelligibility. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Appendix B.1, Table B2. Confirmed
  • Audiobox computes its flow-matching loss only on masked frames. Source: Audiobox (arXiv 2312.15821), Section 4.1. Confirmed
  • P-Flow inverts the idea: the loss excludes the prompt segment, to stop copy-paste collapse. Source: P-Flow (NeurIPS 2023), Section 3.1. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS writes its objective without a mask (Eq. 3) and says the model "is trained to reconstruct m ⊙ x1". CosyVoice 2's L1 loss (its Eq. 8) also has no mask term. I could not settle from the papers whether the loss covers all frames. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Sections 2 and 3.1; CosyVoice 2 (arXiv 2412.10117), Eq. 8. Confirmed

Common training failure modes

Alignment and slow convergence

  • In E2 TTS's own convergence plots, Voicebox had good WER at 10% of training because of its frame-level alignment. E2 TTS needed significantly more training to converge, and ended with the better WER. The authors speculate it learned a better grapheme-to-phoneme mapping. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 3.6.1. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS's reproduced E2 TTS converged slowly in Mandarin small-model experiments. It consistently failed on 7% of test samples (WER above 50%) throughout training, and re-ranking could not fix it. Dropping the audio prompt removed those failures. The F5 authors read that as entanglement of semantic and acoustic features in E2 TTS. This is their hypothesis, from their own reproduction and not from the E2 authors. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 5.1 and Appendix B.2. Confirmed
  • A pure adaLN DiT without the ConvNeXt text branch failed to learn alignment from padded characters. MMDiT "learned fast and collapsed fast", giving severe repeated utterances with wild timbre and prosody. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 5.1. Confirmed
  • Moving ConvNeXt text refinement into the flat U-Net Transformer, or adding long skips to the DiT, both degraded results. F5-TTS's fixes are therefore a package, not independent add-ons. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 5.1. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS reports stable training on small data: 158M models on LibriTTS (585 h) and LJSpeech (24 h) learned text-speech alignment without a grapheme-to-phoneme step. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Appendix B.7. Confirmed

Robustness and WER

  • On ELLA-V hard sentences, F5-TTS shows a high deletion rate when the text contains stacks of repeated words, which the authors call word skipping. Its insertion rate is low, so they say it avoids endless repetition. Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Appendix B.5. Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS uses REINFORCE-based RL (SIM and WER rewards) as a post-training step to improve robustness and speaker similarity. Source: Seed-TTS (arXiv 2406.02430), RL section. Confirmed
  • Voicebox depends on a phonemizer and forced aligner. Its authors say word-based phonemizers miss context-dependent pronunciation, such as liaisons in French. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Conclusion. Confirmed

Prompt boundary and leakage

  • E2 TTS must find the boundary between the prompt text and the target text inside the padded string. If it gets this wrong, the output may contain part of the prompt text or miss part of the target. In the paper's test, WER showed no obvious trend with prompt length up to about 10 s, while SIM-o improved with longer prompts. Source: E2 TTS (arXiv 2406.18009), Section 3.6.2. Confirmed
  • Leaked content can be overridden early in the flow, as the F5-TTS "leak and override" experiment shows (see time-step sampling above). Source: F5-TTS (arXiv 2410.06885), Section 5.2. Confirmed
  • Voicebox cannot control voice, style and acoustic condition independently. A prompt transfers all of them. It was also trained on read audiobook speech and may not transfer to conversational speech. Source: Voicebox (arXiv 2306.15687), Conclusion. Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS reports a self-distillation scheme for timbre disentanglement. Source: Seed-TTS (arXiv 2406.02430), Section 4. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2's removal of the LM speaker embedding is evidence that prompt-derived vectors leak more than speaker identity (see conditioning above). Inference

Open questions and conflicts

  • F5-TTS's abstract says a public 100K hour dataset. Its experimental section says about 95K hours after filtering. The figures are consistent but not identical, so the post should say "about 95K hours (Emilia, after filtering)".
  • Voicebox lists "p_drop = 0.3" without naming what is dropped. My reading (whole-sequence mask) comes from Audiobox's clearer wording and is an inference.
  • Voicebox quotes CFG alpha as 1.0 in one setup and 0.7 in another. I did not trace which applies to the headline zero-shot TTS numbers.
  • F5-TTS and CosyVoice 2 give no explicit statement of whether the flow-matching loss is restricted to masked frames. This should be checked in the released code before the post says either way.
  • CosyVoice 2 does not state its CFG dropout rate, flow-model step count or GPU count in the text I read. CosyVoice's "64 V100 for 800,000 steps" does not say which component it refers to.
  • E2 TTS gives no GPU count and no time-step sampling statement in its text.
  • F5-TTS's account of E2 TTS failures (7% of samples above 50% WER) comes from its own reproduction, in Mandarin small models. It is not a result from the E2 authors.
  • Which number F5-TTS's duration ratio scales (prompt audio length or prompt text length) is not spelled out. The two sections of the paper describe the ratio differently.
  • Seed-TTS's claim that a duration model reduces naturalness is unquantified in the report. E2 TTS's similar claim is also qualitative.
  • P-Flow has no arXiv id that I could find. The post should cite the NeurIPS 2023 proceedings.
  • Not covered: DiTTo-TTS and MiniMax-Speech latent details were not re-read. VAE-latent flow models beyond Audiobox's Encodec features would need a separate pass.
