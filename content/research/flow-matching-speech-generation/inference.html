Flow matching and diffusion for speech: inference and optimisation

Research note, 2026-10-13. Source-checked claims for a blog post.

Conventions

Confirmed means I read the arXiv abstract or paper text (or first-party code or README) in this pass. Reported means a secondary source, a search summary, or an earlier note that I did not re-read. Inference is my own reasoning.

NFE is the number of network evaluations per utterance. Papers differ on whether NFE counts the extra classifier-free guidance (CFG) pass, so compare NFE across papers with care. RTF figures come from different GPUs and implementations; the conflicts are listed at the end.

ODE solvers and NFE

  • Voicebox uses the midpoint solver with step size 0.0625, which the paper labels NFE=32, and it uses torchdiffeq, which offers fixed and adaptive solvers. Source: Voicebox, arXiv 2306.15687, Section 5.1. Confirmed
  • Voicebox says it reaches very high quality with fewer than 10 NFEs. Zero-shot TTS WER stays at a mean of 2.0 (variance 0.005) across NFE settings. Source: arXiv 2306.15687, Sections 3.4 and 5.6. Confirmed
  • Voicebox timing: NFE=2 without CFG takes about 0.31 s for a 10 s sample, about 20 times faster than VALL-E. At NFE=64 it is only 4% slower than VALL-E. Source: arXiv 2306.15687, abstract and Section 5.6. Hardware is not stated. Confirmed
  • Audiobox speech uses midpoint with step 0.0625, "invoking the derivatives 32 times". With CFG that is 64 model calls, so its NFE does not count the guidance pass. Source: Audiobox, arXiv 2312.15821, Section 5.3. Confirmed
  • Adaptive dopri5 appears in Audiobox Sound as the default solver at absolute and relative tolerance 1e-5. The Bespoke solver is claimed to be over 25 times faster than dopri5 without loss of performance. I could not read the Bespoke result tables (the paper text was truncated), so I give no Bespoke NFE. Source: arXiv 2312.15821, abstract, introduction, Section 6.3. Confirmed
  • I found no speech paper that reports a dopri5 NFE count for TTS. A search summary says another paper (Shallow Flow Matching, arXiv 2505.12226) found default torchdiffeq tolerances too strict and relaxed them to 1e-5. I did not read that paper. Reported
  • F5-TTS defaults to Euler. In Appendix B.4 Euler is "inherently faster and performs slightly better typically for larger NFE with Sway Sampling". Without Sway Sampling, Euler degrades. Source: F5-TTS, arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • Matcha-TTS uses first-order Euler only and has no solver ablation. Source: Matcha-TTS, arXiv 2309.03199. Confirmed
  • ZipVoice uses an Euler solver. Source: ZipVoice, arXiv 2506.13053, Section IV-D. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2 runs at 10 NFE with CFG strength 0.7. Source: CosyVoice 2, arXiv 2412.10117. Confirmed
  • StreamFlow uses an Euler solver with 10 time steps and CFG strength 0.5. Source: StreamFlow, arXiv 2506.23986. Confirmed
  • Midpoint and Heun cost more evaluations per step, and the speech evidence says they buy little over Euler once the schedule is good. This is my reading of the F5-TTS solver table below. Inference

Classifier-free guidance cost

With guidance strength \( \alpha \), Voicebox uses \( \tilde v_t = (1+\alpha)\,v_t(\text{cond}) - \alpha\, v_t(\text{uncond}) \). Source: arXiv 2306.15687, Section 3.5. Confirmed

  • Each guided evaluation needs two forward passes, and the Voicebox figure caption says "Inference time will be doubled with CFG". Source: arXiv 2306.15687, Section 3.5 and Figure 5. The paper describes no mitigation beyond choosing NFE. Confirmed
  • In the F5-TTS reference code the conditional and unconditional passes run in one batched transformer call (cfg_infer=True). The output is split with torch.chunk(pred_cfg, 2, dim=0) and combined as pred + (pred - null_pred) * cfg_strength. If strength is below 1e-5 it runs a single pass. Source: SWivid/F5-TTS, src/f5_tts/model/cfm.py. Confirmed
  • Batching the two passes saves a sequential launch, but it still doubles the FLOPs and the activation memory. Speed-up from batching depends on whether the GPU is already saturated. Inference
  • F5-TTS trains with two-stage dropout: masked speech dropped at 0.3, then speech plus text dropped at 0.2. Its default inference CFG strength is 2. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Sections 4 and 5. Confirmed
  • Reported guidance values: Voicebox \( \alpha = 0.7 \) (also Audiobox speech), CosyVoice 2 0.7, StreamFlow 0.5, F5-TTS 2. The code form pred + (pred - null) * s is the same convention as Voicebox's \( \alpha \), so F5's 2 corresponds to a total weight of 3 on the conditional term. I did not verify the CosyVoice 2 or StreamFlow convention. Inference
  • Voicebox guidance and NFE interact: at NFE of 4 or fewer, lower guidance (\( \alpha = 0 \) or 0.3) gives higher speaker similarity, and from NFE 8 stronger guidance helps. With \( \alpha = 0 \), WER rises slightly from 2.8 to 3.1 as NFE goes from 2 to 32 in diverse sampling. Lower NFE and low guidance both reduce sample diversity (FSD improves with NFE). Source: arXiv 2306.15687, Section 5.6 and Figure 5. Confirmed
  • ZipVoice's distilled student is conditioned on the CFG strength \( \omega \), so it needs no unconditional pass. A teacher made of two CFG-guided steps supplies the target for one student step. ZipVoice inference also uses time-dependent CFG: early steps drop only the text condition, later steps drop text and audio. The \( \omega \) range is not given in the text I read. Source: arXiv 2506.13053. Confirmed
  • Other CFG-removal routes: CuteTTS folds CFG and several solver steps into one interval-conditioned student, and the unified guidance paper distils conditional knowledge into the weights. Numbers are in the few-step section. Sources: arXiv 2608.08638, arXiv 2607.00363. Confirmed

Sway sampling and other time-step schedules

Sway Sampling warps a uniform \( u \in [0,1] \) with

\[ t = u + s\Big(\cos\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}u\big) - 1 + u\Big), \qquad s = -1 \text{ by default.} \]
  • Negative \( s \) puts more steps at small \( t \). The valid range is \( s \in [-1, 2/(\pi-2)] \), \( s = 0 \) is uniform, and Table 6 also tests \( s = -0.8 \). Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • Ablation at 32 NFE, midpoint, on LibriSpeech-PC: with Sway WER 2.41 and SIM 0.66, without Sway WER 2.84 and SIM 0.62. On Seed-TTS test-en WER is 1.87 with and 1.93 without. On test-zh it is WER 1.58 and SIM 0.75 with, against WER 1.93 and SIM 0.69 without. At 16 NFE with Sway (midpoint) LibriSpeech-PC WER is 2.43, SIM 0.66, RTF 0.26. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 5. Confirmed
  • It is inference-only: no retraining and no extra run-time cost. The F5-TTS code applies the same warp to the time grid when a coefficient is set. Source: arXiv 2410.06885; SWivid/F5-TTS cfm.py. Confirmed
  • Empirically Pruned Step Sampling (EPSS) keeps dense early steps under Sway and prunes later steps. Its 7-NFE grid is {0, 1/16, 1/8, 3/16, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1}. Source: Accelerating Flow-Matching-Based TTS via EPSS, arXiv 2505.19931, training-free. Confirmed
  • The current F5-TTS code builds its default grid with an EPSS function when the start time is 0, otherwise a linear grid, and applies the sway warp afterwards. The warp default is None in sample(). Source: SWivid/F5-TTS cfm.py. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2 uses a cosine schedule \( t := 1 - \cos(\tfrac{1}{2} t \pi) \) at inference only, which gives more steps to the initial stage. Source: arXiv 2412.10117. Confirmed
  • DMOSpeech 2's 4-step student uses Sway with \( s = -1 \) at \( t \in \{0, 0.0761, 0.2929, 0.6173\} \), which is the sway image of \( \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\} \). Source: DMOSpeech 2, arXiv 2507.14988, Section 3.1 and Appendix B.2. Confirmed
  • Learned or searched schedules: IntMeanFlow proposes Optimal Step Sampling Search (O3S) with no added inference cost, and the flow-map paper proposes a TD-DP sampling schedule derived from teacher dynamics. Sources: arXiv 2510.07979, arXiv 2609.36324 (abstracts). Confirmed

Few-step methods applied to speech

Consistency models and consistency distillation

  • Consistency models map any point on a trajectory to the same endpoint, giving one-step sampling, and can be distilled from a diffusion model or trained from scratch. Source: Consistency Models, arXiv 2303.01469. Confirmed
  • CoMoSpeech distils a consistency model from a diffusion teacher for one-step speech and singing synthesis. It reports more than 150 times faster than real time on one A100, comparable to FastSpeech2. Teacher models give the best quality; the one-step model matches or beats multi-step diffusion baselines. Source: CoMoSpeech, arXiv 2305.06908. Confirmed
  • RapFlow-TTS enforces velocity consistency along the straightened ODE path, adds time interval scheduling and adversarial learning, and reports "a 5- and 10-fold reduction in synthesis steps" against flow-matching and score-based baselines. Source: arXiv 2506.16741 (Interspeech 2025). I did not read the result tables. Confirmed

Reflow and rectified flow

  • Rectified flow learns straight paths by repeated rectification; the authors show good samples with a single Euler step. Source: Flow Straight and Fast, arXiv 2209.03003. Confirmed
  • ReFlow-TTS applies it to mel generation, with one-step synthesis and no pre-trained teacher. On LJSpeech it beats other diffusion-based models and its one-step version is competitive with other one-step TTS models. The abstract has no numbers. Source: arXiv 2309.17056 (ICASSP 2024). Confirmed
  • VoiceFlow uses rectified flow matching for TTS at "a limited number of sampling steps". The abstract gives no step counts. Source: arXiv 2309.05027 (ICASSP 2024). Confirmed
  • SlimSpeech builds on ReFlow-TTS with annealing reflow for one-step sampling. Source: arXiv 2504.07776, via a search summary only. Reported

Shortcut models and mean-flow style

  • Shortcut models condition one network on noise level and step size, and need one network and one training phase. Source: One Step Diffusion via Shortcut Models, arXiv 2410.12557 (images). Confirmed
  • R-VC applies shortcut flow matching to zero-shot voice conversion, down to two sampling steps. This is voice conversion, not TTS. Source: arXiv 2506.01014 (ACL 2025). Confirmed
  • I did not find a shortcut-model TTS paper. A search summary also lists shortcut flow matching for speech enhancement (arXiv 2509.21522) and REF-VC (arXiv 2508.04996); I did not read either. Reported
  • IntMeanFlow approximates average velocity with the teacher's instantaneous velocity over an interval, avoiding the Jacobian-vector-product memory cost and self-bootstrapping instability that MeanFlow causes in TTS. It reports 1-NFE token-to-spectrogram and 3-NFE text-to-spectrogram inference. Source: arXiv 2510.07979. Confirmed
  • FlashTTS uses an X-pred mean-flow decoder at exactly 2 NFE, a 16-layer DiT with hidden size 768 (about 159.25M parameters) that predicts the clean mel. Source: FlashTTS, arXiv 2606.09141. Confirmed
  • MeanFlow itself (average velocity, FID 3.43 on ImageNet one-step) is cited from an earlier note. Source: arXiv 2505.13447, not re-read here. Reported
  • DSFlow distils flow-matching TTS to few and one step with endpoint matching plus mean-velocity alignment and lightweight step-aware tokens. The abstract has no numbers. Source: arXiv 2602.09041. Confirmed

Distribution matching distillation (DMD)

  • DMD2 removes the regression loss, adds a two time-scale update rule and a GAN loss on real data, and supports multi-step sampling. Source: arXiv 2405.14867 (images). Confirmed
  • DMOSpeech distils a 128-step teacher to a 4-step student with DMD2 and trains CTC and speaker-verification losses through the student. RTF drops from 0.96 to 0.07 on a V100. Student WER is 1.94 with SIM 0.69, against ground truth 2.19 and 0.67. Student timesteps are {1.0, 0.75, 0.50, 0.25}. Source: DMOSpeech, arXiv 2410.11097. Confirmed
  • DMOSpeech 2 uses teacher-guided sampling: the F5-TTS teacher runs 14 steps, the student 2, for 16 total. On Seed-TTS-en the 32-step F5 teacher has WER 1.947, SIM 0.662, RTF 0.1671. The 4-step student has WER 1.752, SIM 0.698, RTF 0.0316. The 16-step mix has WER 1.738, SIM 0.699, RTF 0.0941. RTF was measured on one H100. Source: arXiv 2507.14988. Confirmed
  • The paper gives a general range of about 6 to 14 teacher steps and 2 to 3 student steps. Source: arXiv 2507.14988, Section 3.3.2. Confirmed

Adversarial post-training

  • Adversarial post-training (APT) after diffusion pre-training gives one-step generation in Seaweed-APT (2-second 720p video in real time). Source: arXiv 2501.08316 (video, not speech). Confirmed
  • APTTS (Interspeech 2025, LG AI Research) treats a pre-trained latent flow-matching TTS model as a few-step generator trained with reconstruction and adversarial losses, and also applies the method to Matcha-TTS. It reports better quality with lower inference time. I found no arXiv version and no numbers; sources are the ISCA archive and LG Research listing. Reported
  • DMOSpeech's student is trained with a conformer discriminator, and RapFlow-TTS adds adversarial learning. DriftTTS explicitly avoids adversarial discrimination. Sources: arXiv 2410.11097, arXiv 2506.16741, arXiv 2610.03390. Confirmed

Streaming and chunked flow matching

  • CosyVoice 2 samples one of four masks per training example, uniformly: non-causal, full-causal, chunk-M (past plus M future frames) and chunk-2M. A right-padded 1-D convolution (kernel P+1) is the look-ahead layer before 2x up-sampling from 25 Hz tokens to 50 Hz mel. Table 8's caption gives chunk size 15 for the streaming modules. Source: arXiv 2412.10117. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 2 gives first-packet latency as \( L_{TTS} = M d_{lm} + M d_{fm} + M d_{voc} \) (LM, flow and vocoder per-token times) and the text-speech mixing ratio N:M as 5:15. It gives no measured first-packet latency, names no vocoder, and does not mention KV caching. Source: arXiv 2412.10117. Confirmed
  • StreamFlow (a DiT with block-wise attention masks) uses a default block of 24 frames (0.24 s), chunks of 2 blocks, and a receptive field of \( (p+q+1) b \) for \( p \) backward and \( q \) forward blocks. First-packet latency is about 180 ms on an A100, and per-chunk latency stays near 180 ms in long-form generation because of the fixed window, while the full-history DiT baseline's per-chunk time grows. Source: arXiv 2506.23986. Confirmed
  • Larger future context raises quality but also cost and first-packet latency. Source: arXiv 2506.23986 (abstract summary); table below. Confirmed
  • FireRedTTS-1S chunk-wise flow matching: a 150M-parameter flow model, 1 s chunks (25 semantic tokens at 40 ms), a look-ahead convolution over the future 3 tokens, left context limited to 2 s, RTF 0.1 and latency under 300 ms on an L20. Its bound is latency \( \le (l+3) t_s + t_a + t_c \) for LM step, flow and BigVGAN times. Chunks are joined by crossfading, with 8 previous mel frames as vocoder context. NFE and a chunk-size ablation are not reported. Source: FireRedTTS-1S, arXiv 2503.20499. Confirmed
  • Kimi-Audio's detokenizer uses a chunk-wise causal mask in training and inference. It appends the first n semantic tokens of the next chunk (example n = 4) as look-ahead and keeps only the current chunk's mel. Look-ahead "will only delay the generation of the first chunk" by n tokens. It fine-tunes with chunk sizes of 0.5 to 3 s, uses BigVGAN, and reports no NFE or latency in the text I read. Source: arXiv 2504.18425. The example chunk length is garbled in the text I read (it showed "11 second"); my earlier note says 1 s. Confirmed
  • Qwen3-TTS (25 Hz variant with a DiT): the DiT waits for 16 LM tokens before producing the first 8-token mel chunk (320 ms of audio per steady packet). BigVGAN adds a 130 ms right-context look-ahead. Source: Qwen3-TTS, arXiv 2601.15621. Confirmed
  • VoXtream reports 102 ms first-packet latency on GPU with a limited look-ahead. Source: VoXtream, arXiv 2509.15969 (abstract). That flow-matching decoders on chunks hurt first-packet latency in systems like CosyVoice 2 comes from a search summary, not the abstract. Reported
  • SyncSpeech reports 0.06 s first packet against 0.22 s for CosyVoice 2 on LibriSpeech test-clean, and starts after 2 text tokens (CosyVoice 2 needs 5). Source: arXiv 2502.11094, from an earlier note. Reported
  • FlashTTS reports no block size or look-ahead for its block-wise attention, and says CosyVoice 2 buffers five text tokens while FlashTTS starts at one. Source: arXiv 2606.09141. Confirmed
  • A chunked non-causal decoder waits half a chunk on average for the decision plus one look-ahead block, and that wait adds directly to first-packet time. Inference

Caching, real-time factor and first-packet latency

  • CosyVoice's README lists a streaming mode "including kv cache and sdpa for rtf optimization", vLLM support for CosyVoice 2/3, and a claim that TensorRT-LLM "could give 4x acceleration" over a Hugging Face implementation. The 4x figure has no benchmark details, and the README names no flow-decoder acceleration or RTF. It claims "latency as low as 150ms" in bi-streaming. Source: github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice README. Confirmed
  • Qwen3-TTS measures latency on an internal vLLM (V0) engine and uses torch.compile and CUDA Graph for the tokenizer decoder. The paper does not mention KV cache. Source: arXiv 2601.15621. Confirmed
  • VoxAudio, a causal autoregressive flow matching model, uses chunk-wise factorisation with per-chunk noise levels and sliding-window streaming with KV caching. Source: arXiv 2608.12951 (abstract). That RTF improves roughly inversely with chunk size while first-chunk latency stays constant comes from a search summary. Reported
  • The AR stage can reuse its KV cache across steps, but the flow stage cannot reuse activations across ODE steps because its input \( x_t \) changes each step. Only the prompt-dependent parts (text encoding, reference mel features) are step-invariant. I found no paper that caches the prompt computation for a flow decoder. Inference
  • StreamFlow's per-chunk time for the full-history DiT baseline grows with history, while its sliding window stays flat. That is a bounded-context substitute for a KV cache on the flow side. Source: arXiv 2506.23986, Figure 5 (values not given in text). Confirmed

Vocoder cost relative to the flow model

  • Matcha-TTS RTF includes HiFi-GAN (13.9M parameters) against an 18.2M parameter acoustic model. The split of RTF between them is not given. Source: arXiv 2309.03199, Table 1. Confirmed
  • FlashTTS uses a 50M-parameter HiFi-GAN at 24 kHz alongside a 159.25M-parameter decoder. Source: arXiv 2606.09141. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS uses Vocos by default, and swapping to BigVGAN lowers LibriSpeech-PC WER from 2.42 to 2.11 at 32 NFE and from 2.53 to 2.21 at 16 NFE. Table 8 has no RTF column, so the speed cost of BigVGAN is not measured there. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 8. Confirmed
  • BigVGAN scales to 112M parameters, as noted in the earlier note. Source: BigVGAN, arXiv 2206.04658, not re-read here. Reported
  • Both CosyVoice 2 and FireRedTTS-1S include a vocoder term in the first-packet latency formula, and Qwen3-TTS's BigVGAN look-ahead adds 130 ms. Sources: arXiv 2412.10117, arXiv 2503.20499, arXiv 2601.15621. Confirmed
  • Once the flow model is cut to 2 to 4 NFE, the vocoder and the look-ahead start to dominate, because the flow cost has shrunk while the vocoder cost has not. I found no paper that reports the flow-versus-vocoder time split directly. Inference

Serving considerations

  • Under concurrency the Qwen3-TTS DiT variants degrade much faster than the causal ones: for the 0.6B models, RTF goes from 0.234 to 0.709 (25 Hz) vs 0.288 to 0.434 (12 Hz) between 1 and 6 streams. The paper calls the 12 Hz decoder "batch-friendly". Source: arXiv 2601.15621. Confirmed
  • Matcha-TTS training memory is 4.8 GiB for MAT, against 7.8 GiB for Grad-TTS and 12.4 GiB for VITS, measured at batch size 32 in fp16. This is training memory, not serving memory. Source: arXiv 2309.03199, Table 1. Confirmed
  • ZipVoice reports CPU RTF with a single Xeon thread: F5-TTS 37.284, ZipVoice 16 NFE 9.5529, Distill 8 NFE 2.4177, Distill 4 NFE 1.2202. Source: arXiv 2506.13053, Table II. Confirmed
  • Flow-stage cost scales with NFE times (1 + guidance pass) times model size times sequence length, so a 10 NFE guided model is about 20 model calls per utterance. Distillation and guidance folding cut both factors. Inference
  • Memory per stream for a sliding-window flow decoder is bounded by the window, whereas a full-history decoder grows with utterance length. I found no paper that reports GPU memory per stream for a flow TTS decoder. Inference

Open questions and conflicts

  • F5-TTS RTF for 16 NFE is 0.15 in the F5-TTS paper (RTX 3090, Euler) but 0.065 in the EPSS paper on the same GPU class, and the EPSS 32-NFE value is 0.123 against 0.31. Same-GPU gaps this large point to different implementations or measurement code, and the EPSS paper does not state its 16 and 7 NFE baseline solver.
  • The EPSS text claims a 78% cost reduction "compared to 32-NFE uniform sampling", while its Table 2 labels the 32-NFE baseline as Sway Sampling.
  • F5-TTS Table 1 lists RTF 0.15 for 16 NFE (Euler), while Table 5 lists 0.26 for 16 NFE (midpoint). They are different solvers, not a contradiction, but they are easy to mix up.
  • NFE counting: Voicebox and Audiobox count solver evaluations without the CFG pass (32 NFE is 64 model calls). I did not establish the convention in F5-TTS, CosyVoice 2 or ZipVoice.
  • DMOSpeech's 128-step teacher WER was returned as 9.51 with SIM 0.55 in my fetch. That looks low-quality for a teacher and I did not verify the table, so I use only the student numbers.
  • Time direction: DMOSpeech lists student timesteps {1.0, 0.75, 0.50, 0.25} while DMOSpeech 2 lists sway times {0, 0.0761, 0.2929, 0.6173}. The papers may use opposite conventions. DMOSpeech 2 also uses both \( u \) and \( s \) for the sway coefficient.
  • Kimi-Audio's chunk length and up-sampling factor are garbled in the text I read, so the 1 s chunk figure rests on an earlier note.
  • RTF teachers disagree across papers on the same F5-TTS model: 0.3 on an H20 (ZipVoice, 32 NFE, 0.2958), 0.1671 on an H100 (DMOSpeech 2, 32 steps), 0.31 on an RTX 3090 (F5-TTS, 32 NFE Euler).
  • Not found: a speech paper reporting dopri5 NFE; a shortcut-model TTS paper; an arXiv version of APTTS; a dedicated chunk-size versus WER or SIM ablation for flow-matching TTS (StreamFlow's is objective-metric only); a measured flow-versus-vocoder time split; GPU memory per concurrent stream.
  • Not checked: the Bespoke solver result tables, RapFlow-TTS result tables, and the claims taken from earlier notes without re-reading (MeanFlow 2505.13447, BigVGAN 2206.04658, Moshi, Qwen3-Omni, SyncSpeech).
