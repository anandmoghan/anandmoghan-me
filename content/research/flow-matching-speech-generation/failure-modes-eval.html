Flow matching speech: failure modes and evaluation

Research note, 2026-10-13. Claims carry a source and a grounding tag.

How to read this note

Confirmed means I read the primary page or paper abstract. Reported means a secondary source or a search summary. Inference means my own reasoning. Numbers appear only next to the source that states them.

How these models are evaluated

  • F5-TTS evaluates on a 1,127-sample LibriSpeech-PC test-clean subset (4 to 10 s), Seed-TTS test-en (1,088 samples) and test-zh (2,020 samples). Source: F5-TTS, arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • WER uses Whisper-large-v3 for English and Paraformer-zh for Mandarin. SIM is the cosine of WavLM-large speaker-verification embeddings. Source: arXiv 2410.06885; github.com/BytedanceSpeech/seed-tts-eval. Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS-eval also ships a Mandarin hard-case list (zh/hardcase.lst). The model weights are not released, only the test sets and metric scripts. Source: github.com/BytedanceSpeech/seed-tts-eval. Confirmed
  • F5-TTS reports CMOS on a -3 to +3 integer scale against ground truth, and SMOS on 1 to 5 in 0.5 steps. Raters: 20 native speakers. UTMOS is used for objective naturalness in the appendix ablations. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • Reference points on LibriSpeech-PC test-clean: ground truth WER 2.23 and SIM-o 0.69. F5-TTS at 16 NFE gets WER 2.53 and SIM-o 0.66. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 1. Confirmed
  • Audiobox uses Whisper large-v2 for WER, because HuBERT-L is less robust on non-audiobook data. Its table values computed with HuBERT-L are marked as not directly comparable. Source: Audiobox, arXiv 2312.15821, Section 5.2. Confirmed
  • Audiobox's SIM model is WavLM-TDCNN, but this was inferred from surrounding context by the page reader, not stated explicitly. Source: arXiv 2312.15821. Reported
  • CosyVoice 3 evaluates on Seed-TTS-Eval (test-zh, test-en, test-hard) and its own CV3-Eval. It uses Whisper-large V3 and Paraformer for ASR. It reports speaker similarity with two encoders (WavLM-based and ERes2Net), plus DNSMOS and 1 to 5 MOS from 10 native raters on 200 sentences. Source: CosyVoice 3, arXiv 2505.17589. Confirmed

Known pitfalls of the metrics

  • The ASR family changes verifier rankings. On LibriSpeech-PC with F5-TTS, best-of-N verifier rankings shift across Whisper, wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT evaluators. Same-family verifier and evaluator pairs recover more oracle headroom than cross-family pairs. Source: Best-of-N TTS Evaluation is Confounded by ASR Family Alignment, arXiv 2607.08256. Confirmed
  • Different papers use different speaker encoders (WavLM-large in Seed-TTS-eval, ERes2Net alongside WavLM in CosyVoice 3). A SIM value from one encoder does not compare with another's. Source: arXiv 2505.17589; Seed-TTS-eval readme. Confirmed The conclusion about cross-paper comparison is Inference.
  • Reference-free quality predictors (UTMOS, DNSMOS, SCOREQ) agree with listeners when a clip has audible defects, but not when both clips are clean. Some do worse than choosing the longer clip. Source: The Limits of Reference-Free Speech Quality Metrics..., arXiv 2609.13150. Confirmed
  • WER near zero is mostly evaluator noise, and text normalisation changes counts. Source: a practitioner blog (altsoph.substack.com) and search summaries, not a paper. Reported
  • Ground truth is not a perfect floor. In F5-TTS, F5-TTS at 32 NFE has test-en WER 1.83 against ground truth 2.06, and test-zh 1.56 against 1.26. WER therefore cannot rank systems that already match human recordings. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 2 (numbers); the conclusion is Inference.
  • F5-TTS urges more rigorous, transparent subjective evaluation, noting that some earlier work used few raters. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed

Failure modes of flow matching and diffusion TTS

Word skipping and repetition

  • E2 TTS consistently failed on about 7% of Mandarin test samples (WER above 50%) during training, and re-ranking could not fix it. An MMDiT variant of F5-TTS collapsed into repeated utterances with unstable timbre and prosody. A pure adaLN DiT without text refinement failed to learn alignment. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • On ELLA-V hard sentences, F5-TTS WER is 4.40 against E2 TTS 8.58 and CosyVoice 8.30. The authors attribute part of the error to deletions on stacks of repeated words. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 7. Confirmed
  • Flow-matching TTS remains prone to skip and repeat errors from imperfect alignment. An augmentation method drops Seed-TTS-eval WER from 1.44 to 1.38. Source: RobustSpeechFlow, arXiv 2605.22083. Confirmed

Duration and speaking rate

  • F5-TTS has no duration predictor. Total length is set from the character-count ratio of prompt text to target text. Replacing it with ground-truth duration moved the small-model WER only from 4.17 to 3.87. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • A wrong global length estimate is a plausible cause of rushed or stretched speech on unusual text. Inference
  • DMOSpeech 2 trains the duration predictor with GRPO using speaker similarity and WER rewards. Source: arXiv 2507.14988 (abstract gives no metric values). Confirmed

Prosody flatness

  • GRPO on CER and NLL rewards lowered error rates but made speech flat. Adding speaker similarity as a reward destabilised training. Iterative DPO with a few hundred human preference pairs per round recovered naturalness. Source: No Verifiable Reward for Prosody, arXiv 2509.18531. Confirmed The model there is a TTS policy trained on Korean call-centre data, not a flow-matching model specifically.
  • F5-TTS lists a lack of fine-grained paralinguistic control (emotion) as a limitation. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 3 reports accuracy dropping sharply for sad and angry when the emotion is not in the text. Source: arXiv 2505.17589. Confirmed

Low NFE and few-step generation

  • F5-TTS goes from 16 to 32 NFE with slightly better WER on LibriSpeech-PC (2.53 to 2.42) and RTF from 0.15 to 0.31. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 1. Confirmed
  • Without Sway Sampling, 32 NFE WER is 2.84 and SIM 0.62, against 2.41 and 0.66 with it. Euler sampling degrades without Sway Sampling. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 5. Confirmed
  • Applying MeanFlow directly to TTS causes GPU memory overhead and training instability from self-bootstrapping. IntMeanFlow reports 1-NFE token-to-spectrogram and 3-NFE text-to-spectrogram synthesis. Source: IntMeanFlow, arXiv 2510.07979. Confirmed
  • I found no source that measures over-smoothing at low NFE in speech. Treat "low NFE blurs prosody" as unsupported. Inference

CFG artifacts

  • F5-TTS uses CFG strength 2 by default. CFG needs two forward passes, roughly doubling inference cost. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • Single-weight CFG ties text and speaker conditioning together. Branch-selective guidance trades text accuracy against speaker similarity. Reweighting text, speaker and joint residuals independently improves both on F5-TTS, CosyVoice 2 and GLM-TTS, with no retraining. Source: Joint Residual Reweighting for CFG..., arXiv 2606.25672. Confirmed
  • A search summary reports a CFG sweep from 2.5 to 10.0 in which WER improves with stronger guidance and SIM-o peaks at 3.5. The paper's abstract does not mention a sweep, so I could not confirm it. Source: BareWave, arXiv 2606.09048. Reported
  • Training the model to absorb CFG removes the second pass. Source: arXiv 2504.20334 (9x inference speed-up on F5-TTS with LibriTTS training, comparable quality, authors' claim). Confirmed

Prompt leakage and noisy prompts

  • Noise in the audio prompt significantly degrades flow-matching zero-shot TTS. Masked-speech-denoising pre-training, DNSMOS-based data filtering and noise-mixing fine-tuning outperformed enhancing the prompt first. Source: arXiv 2406.05699. Confirmed
  • Existing zero-shot systems often struggle with prompts that contain background sound. A dual speaker encoder with a control signal chooses to keep or remove the background. Source: arXiv 2502.07345. Confirmed
  • Timbre leakage in flow matching is blamed on residual acoustic traces in the content representation. Heterogeneous data augmentation targets it. Source: arXiv 2607.00363 (abstract; the authors' framing is about speaker identity, not channel). Confirmed
  • In-context conditioning copies the prompt's channel and noise into the output by the same mechanism as timbre. The papers above support the noisy-prompt problem, not the copy mechanism itself. Inference

Chunked streaming boundaries

  • CosyVoice 2 uses a chunk-aware causal flow-matching model, so one model serves streaming and non-streaming. Source: arXiv 2412.10117. Confirmed
  • BinauralFlow argues CosyVoice 2 lacks feature buffers for its causal convolution layers, which could cause interruptions and discontinuities in real streaming. Source: arXiv 2505.22865, Appendix G. Confirmed This is a competing group's claim, not a measurement.
  • FireRedTTS-1S adds look-ahead convolution to smooth chunk transitions; JoyVoice randomises chunk size. Sources: arXiv 2503.20499, arXiv 2512.19090, from a search summary only. Reported

Long text and code-switching

  • F5-TTS reports code-switching and speed control as capabilities, with no failure analysis. Source: arXiv 2410.06885 abstract. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 3 gives no discussion of hallucination, code-switching or long text. It does report language-specific errors, with Japanese CER about 9% and Korean about 6% in a polyglot experiment. Source: arXiv 2505.17589. Confirmed
  • Autoregressive Transformer TTS drops or repeats words on long utterances. Very Attentive Tacotron adds relative-location cross-attention to fix it. Source: Battenberg et al., arXiv 2410.22179. Confirmed This is autoregressive, not flow matching.

AR plus flow hybrids

  • dots.tts is a 2B continuous autoregressive model with a flow-matching head conditioned on full history, to reduce drift. It reports Seed-TTS-Eval WER 0.94 (zh), 1.30 (en), 6.60 (zh-hard). These are the authors' own numbers. Source: arXiv 2606.07080. Confirmed
  • A search summary says RobustSpeechFlow states that hybrid LM-diffusion systems also show alignment errors. The abstract I read does not say that. Source: arXiv 2605.22083. Reported
  • In CosyVoice 3, hard test-hard CER stays at 5.09 to 5.66 after RL, and the 1.5B model trails the 0.5B on test-hard. The authors blame limited challenging training data. Source: arXiv 2505.17589. Confirmed

Mitigations reported

  • Better text conditioning: F5-TTS refines text with ConvNeXt before alignment, and credits it for the robustness gain over E2 TTS. On the small model, WER is 4.17 against 9.63 for E2 TTS. Source: arXiv 2410.06885, Table 4. Confirmed
  • Training-time augmentation of skip and repeat latents (RobustSpeechFlow) needs no external aligner. Source: arXiv 2605.22083. Confirmed
  • Best-of-N reranking with an ASR verifier; a cross-family ensemble gives a 12% relative WER reduction over F5-TTS at N=10. Source: arXiv 2607.08256. Confirmed
  • F5R-TTS applies GRPO to a flow-matching model by turning outputs into Gaussians, with WER and SIM rewards. It reports a 29.5% relative WER reduction and 4.6% relative SIM gain over conventional flow-matching TTS. Source: F5R-TTS, arXiv 2504.02407. Confirmed
  • CosyVoice 3 post-trains with a differentiable reward model (DiffRO) on speech tokens. The RL targets the token language model, not the flow stage. On Seed-TTS, 0.5B CER goes 1.16 to 0.75 (test-zh) and WER 2.02 to 1.76 (test-en). The authors state RL slightly lowers speaker similarity and attribute it to hacking. The abstract does not name "DiffRO"; the term appears in the full text. Source: arXiv 2505.17589, Table 4. Confirmed
  • GROW (on DiTAR) avoids the ODE-to-SDE conversion. Average WER goes 2.016 to 1.558 and SIM 0.676 to 0.715, with UTMOS unchanged. Source: arXiv 2608.03215. Confirmed
  • FlowTTS-GRPO converts ODE paths to SDE and fine-tunes CosyVoice 3.0 and F5-TTS. Omitting CFG during training speeds convergence, and synthesising hard cases helps robustness. Source: arXiv 2606.23190. Confirmed
  • Reward hacking is reported across these methods. A single quality predictor as reward leads to worse held-out and human ratings; an equal-weighted ensemble resists it. Source: arXiv 2609.13150. Confirmed RRPO reports that DiffRO-style reward models can be exploited with acoustic artifacts. Source: arXiv 2512.04552. Confirmed
  • Stepwise preference optimisation scores noisy intermediate states of diffusion TTS (EASPO, emotion focus). Source: arXiv 2509.25416. Confirmed
  • Data filtering: DNSMOS-based filtering of pre-training data for noise robustness. Source: arXiv 2406.05699. Confirmed

Safety

  • The F5-TTS ethics statement warns of voice-identification spoofing and recommends watermarks and detection models. Source: arXiv 2410.06885. Confirmed
  • Seed-TTS-eval states weights and code are withheld "due to considerations for AI safety". Source: github.com/BytedanceSpeech/seed-tts-eval. Confirmed
  • AudioSeal trains a generator and detector jointly with a localisation loss. Detection is localised down to the sample level, and the detector is up to two orders of magnitude faster than prior models. Source: AudioSeal, arXiv 2401.17264. Confirmed
  • Audiobox applies the Seamless Watermark to its TTS and sound outputs. Averaged over tasks with no augmentation, FPR is 0.001 and FNR is 0. The highest FNR among augmentations is 0.003 (slower speed). Source: arXiv 2312.15821, Section 9.2 and Table 16. Confirmed Audiobox voice authentication was not found in the text I read.
  • Watermark removal, deepfake-detector shortcuts and fingerprint drift under fine-tuning are covered in the existing materials page 06-control-and-quality/30-safety-provenance.html. I did not re-fetch those papers. Reported

Open questions and conflicts

  • Over-smoothing at low NFE in speech: no direct source found.
  • The CFG sweep numbers from BareWave (arXiv 2606.09048) are unconfirmed. The abstract has no sweep. Check the paper body.
  • arXiv 2609.13150 shows a submission date of 2 July 2026, which does not match a 2609 identifier. Verify before citing.
  • The CosyVoice GRPO result in the research file (CER 4.08% to 3.36%) comes from a mirror search summary. I did not confirm it against the first-party repo.
  • The CosyVoice 3 SS values are ambiguous between WavLM and ERes2Net in my page extraction, so I quote no SS numbers.
  • Whether Audiobox uses WavLM-TDCNN for SIM is inferred, not stated.
  • Prompt channel and noise leakage into outputs is not directly measured in any source above.
  • Long-text and code-switching failure rates for flow-matching TTS: no quantitative source found.
  • Whether "hybrid" AR plus flow systems hallucinate less than pure flow is not established by anything I read.
  • DiffRO's own paper (the materials page cites arXiv 2507.05911) was not fetched.
  • The Audiobox voice-authentication and fingerprint claim needs the full PDF.
